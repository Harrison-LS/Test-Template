About the Montmorency Falls

Located just minutes from Quebec City, Montmorency Falls is one of the most popular attractions in the area. The park is named after the Montmorency River, which was named in 1613 by Samuel de Champlain after the Duke of Montmorency. The Montmorency River Falls are 83 meters (272 ft) high, making them nearly 100 feet taller than Niagara Falls, and therefore the tallest waterfalls in Quebec and the eighth tallest in Canada. The difference in water volume and the height of the waterfall in comparison to Niagara Falls makes this difference in height less noticeable, however. Montmorency Falls has a significant history as the site of the 1759 Battle of Montmorency, which was fought between the French and English. In 1885, the first station to provide hydropower to the city of Quebec was built at Montmorency Falls. In recent years, this attraction has also been featured in a few movies. The falls are beset popular for picnicking by residents of Quebec City and are a great stopping point for passing tourists.

The best time to visit Montmorency Falls

People who visit this park often say that it beautiful in every season. But for tourists looking to get the full experience of hiking, rides, and climbing to see the full view from the summit, summer and fall would be the ideal seasons to visit. During summer, visitors can see the awesome views from the top of the cable car. There is also zip-lining from May to October and Via Ferrata in July and August. During winter the view has snow which does create a beautiful view, but it does cover some of the best activities in the park. The walkways become dangerous and some walkways are closed. The cable car is also closed most days in the winter, so it is good to consider visiting the park in other seasons. Even though some activities are closed in winter, it does create a beautiful view for snowshoeing and ice canyoning. Guided ice climbing is available for those who want to climb, and experienced climbers can climb freely. Even if winter is the only season to visit the park, there are many activities to enjoy around Montmorency Falls.

Traveling to Montmorency Falls

Just a 15-minute drive from downtown Quebec, Montmorency Falls Park is just 13 kilometers away. This location makes it easily accessible for a quick and enjoyable picnic for people looking to escape from the city. Additionally, it makes the park a nice stop on road trips. People driving on Autoroute 440 East frequently see the falls and pull over to check them out. If you don't have a car, you can get to the park easily using public transport as a number of bus routes head to the park. If you want to bike to the park, you can use the bike path called Corridor du Littoral that goes all the way to the park. If you're booking a city tour, it might include a stop at the falls. If not, you can book a tour that goes specifically to the falls. If you're taking a nature tour, it's best to plan for a full day so you don't get rushed. There are a lot of great places to see along the way and if you want to get out and explore them it could take you four hours just to get to the end of the day.

Exploring the waterfall

If you have a full day, you can take your time walking around Montmorency Falls Park. The park is free, but drivers have to park. The price of parking depends on the season, type of car, and the number of passengers. Parking also depends on activities and rides you want to do. There is parking near the Visitor Centre at the bottom of the falls and at the top by Montmorency Manor. You can decide to park based on activities, or walking the other areas of the park. For the best navigation of the park, it is recommended to get a free booklet from the park which describes walking paths, and provides history and geography information about the park.

Several viewpoints around the park provide different perspectives on the waterfalls. To get a view of the entire waterfalls, start from the bottom. A paved trail leads to a viewing platform at the bottom of the falls from the Visitor Centre. From the platform, you can see the waterfall, the suspension bridge above the waterfall, and the zip line that connects the two sides of the park. This is a great spot to get a view of the falls and enjoy a picnic. If you take your time there are family-friendly places to stop like a picnic area and a playground.

To reach the summit from the Visitor Centre, you can take the cable car, or if you want some exercise, you can take the panoramic staircase that has 487 steps. The cable car gives you a chance to see the park and other attractions from a bird’s-eye view. If you want to experience the Panoramic Circuit, you should take the cable car up to the suspension bridge and then come down the steps. This way, you can see all of the highlights of the park without having to do too much climbing.

Cable car tickets cost approximately 12.10 for adults and 28.10 for families, although children under 5 years of age travel for free. You can purchase one-way or return tickets, which is a convenient way to avoid having to walk down the stairs. At the top is the Manoir Montmorency which was the country home of Prince Edward, Duke of Kent and father of Queen Victoria. The original house was built in 1780 but was destroyed by a fire in 1993. The current building is a reconstruction that remains true to the original design but with some modern updates. Inside is a souvenir shop and interpretation center where visitors can learn about the history of the site. Additionally, there is an outdoor restaurant with great views of the surrounding area, and if you plan to eat there, it is recommended that you make a reservation.

Close to Manoir Montmorency, a suspension bridge crosses the falls and connects both sides. Although it's built solidly, it might be a little scary for those afraid of heights. However, those who dares to look over will enjoy an unbeatable view of the waterfall, seeing the rushing water from above to the cascade below while the deep rumble of the falls can be felt and heard below your feet. Multiple bridges have existed over these falls, but the most recent one was built in 1993 with the new manor.

Stepping down the panoramic staircase leads you on a cliffside journey. With 487 steps, descending is a lot easier than going back up. If you walk down in the morning, you might see some athletes using the stairs in their training routine. Even though you need some level of fitness to walk up without stopping to rest, it’s encouraged to take breaks to enjoy the scenery. As you walk down, look for the covered rest areas that offer some great views and take a break.

Upon completing the entire path, visitors reach another paved walking path, this one featuring a concrete viewing platform that allows guests to experience the falls up close and personal, with sprays and cool mist coming from the water. In the winter, the spray freezes and creates a stunning natural ice cone, which regular visitors love to see.

For visitors to Montmorency Falls looking for some thrills

You can add some adrenaline to your day at Montmorency Falls Park by choosing from several options. There are two Via Ferrate routes and a third option that combines hiking and zip lining. Via Ferrate is an adventure where trail walking and rock climbing is done, and participants are secured by a harness that is attached to a cable along the cliff face. The gentlest route is for ages 8 and up, while the other two routes have minimum age requirements of 12 and 14.

The Explorer Route is the easiest of the Via Ferrata hikes. It includes a walk-in approach with quite a bit of rock climbing up to the summit. Metal footholds and handholds are embedded into the rock for secure foot placement and handholds. You will stay connected to a safety line that runs along the route. This is a good option, even for children 8 years and older, although it can still be quite strenuous.

If you want a more challenging experience, take on the Torrent of Montmorency route that is 270 meters long. This route runs along the cliff edge next to the falls. Rock face climbers will get close enough to the falls to feel the cool spray on their faces and hands. Seeing as this is a more difficult route, participants have to be 12 years and older.

To end your hike on a high note, try the park’s zip line. It’s 300 metres across the water fall cove and you go from one ledge to the other. You’ll have plenty of time to scream and laugh. Also, the double zip line allows you to cross with a friend or partner, which is as thrilling as it sounds.

This winter, the park provides a special opportunity for ice climbing on the frozen waterfalls! As part of the Montmorency Falls freezes, a large ice wall is created, locally known as Sugar Loaf. While experienced climbers can tackle the route on their own, those who are new, or if you're just looking for some company, you can join a guided ascent with a professional. Climbing gear is available for rent on site.

Dining Options at Montmorency Falls

Manoir Montmorency's own restaurant has a wide ranging menu that includes Sunday brunch. Their terrace is a highlight, featuring stunning views of the falls and the rest of the landscape. The Sandwicherie, located at the base of the falls, is a great place to grab a drink and pick up some sandwiches. LE 1759, which is the park's mobile restaurant, is located at the top of the panoramic staircase and serves inexpensive meals that are inspired by street food. If you are near the Boischatel sector entrance, you can get a quick bite or a drink at the Bridge Snack Bar.

Additional useful details about Montmorency Falls

The whole park is open to wheelchair users and those with limited mobility.

There is no charge for park entry, but rides and activities are subject to seasonal fees.

Dogs are allowed in most areas, and service animals can access the entire site.

The Visitor Centre also has a souvenir shop, restrooms, and a snack bar to facilitate your visit.

Interesting information about the falls

Montmorency Falls might appear to be a standard tourist site but the geology is more interesting than the average photo stop. The falls are located on an ancient geological formation—a fault, in the words of a geologist. A large fracture runs through this portion of land, creating the dramatic precipice over which the Montmorency River cascades.

Despite the fact that Montmorency’s Duke of Damville, never saw the dam, it has since become a landmark in the Anglo-French conflicts of the next 150 years. Remains of British Army fortifications from 1759 still exist on the eastern side of the park. The war spawned a legend that persists to this day. While Louis, a soldier, was killed in a battle at the falls, and his bride, Mathilde, is said to have grieved, jumped from the falls in a wedding dress. The White Lady is said to appear to believers.

Some sources indicate that a romance occurred between the Duke of Kent and a married woman named Thérèse-Bernardine Montgenet at Manoir Montmorency. It is said that their clandestine romance lasted 27 years.

A Seasoned Driver’s Guide for a Saipan Island Road Trip

One of the things travelers love to do is road trip around an island. Saipan is a great place to do just that. Other islands may have stunning views, and a lot of options to consider, but Saipan makes things more simple for the traveler. Rent a car, drive to see the sights. It is perfect for beginners and makes for a great road trip island adventure.

## Saipan Saipan is an island in the western Pacific, southeast of the Philippines. It is the biggest island in the U.S. Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands. People come to Saipan for tropical scenery, clear water, beach holidays, diving, and even road trips around the islands. Saipan has historical significance because of World War II. The Japanese built a lot of fortifications on Saipan when they controlled the Northern Mariana Islands. The 1944 Battle of Saipan was one of the most important battles in the Pacific Theater of World War II. Many wartime sites and monuments can be visited on the island, and some relics can even be found in the water. The Blue Grotto is on the northern tip of Saipan. It is a large limestone cave with openings that connect to the Pacific. It is a popular spot for scuba diving due to its clear water and striking blue color. Because the conditions inside can be dangerous, scuba divers should only go with experienced local guides. ## Other Islands Near Saipan The islands of Tinian and Managaha are close to Saipan and are often visited together. Managaha is great for swimming, snorkeling, and other water sports. Tinian has a much slower beach scene with lots of interesting rocks and historical sites. Saipan has a lot to do and should be explored by car. There are lots of historical sites from World War II, beaches, and look out points along the coast.

**Things to do on Managaha Island**. Managaha Island is about 1.5 km long and has amazing clear water making it a popular destination for **snorkelling with\/against marine life and sunken wartime ships**. Also popular are water sports like jet skiing and banana boat rides. The speedboat ride from Saipan to Managaha takes less than 10 minutes, and you can walk the island in 20 to 30 minutes. Please verify tour guides. Trip length is adjustable but 1 day is enough time to complete activities, and the tour involves reaching the island.<\/p>

On the Micro Beach there are some ferry tours departing from just north of the old Hyatt Regency Saipan Hotel in Garapan. If you are swimming and want to do water sports, book a tour. This includes most of the sports equipment needed for swimming and doing sports. Also worth it if you are going to do all the sports. <\/p>

Tinian is an amazing island for people who like to relax. The island is only a little smaller than Saipan, but has an even smaller population than Saipan. This means it is an even better option for travelers wanting a more quiet, higher quality experience in the style of older island trips. The island has World War II sights to see like airfield landmarks and stone structures, as well as some nice, simple attractions and visitor facilities (in addition to the local restaurants and of course, the beaches). Driving Opportunities Around Saipan

Sea cliffs around Saipan

Saipan is the largest and most inhabited island out of all three main ones. Due to the limited public transport options on the island, visitors often find themselves having to rely on either walking, taking a taxi, joining a guided tour, or renting a car. Because taxis can be expensive and may not always be an option, many people end up choosing the self-driving option to explore the island at their own pace. It is possible to see most of Saipan’s main attractions in about two days if you drive. As you go along the island, there are numerous WWII historical sites, scenic view points, and marine activities in the water to enjoy, so an easy road trip is a good way to go. For your itinerary, the island is split into a northern, southern and central routes to help you organize what you want to do each day.

1. Important Information About Driving

Renting a car in Saipan can make getting around the island easy, however all car companies have different requirements. Most companies require drivers to be at least 21 years of age, posses a valid driver's license, and present a passport as well as a credit card. If renting a car in Saipan and you are a Chinese national with a driver's license, you should call the company to see if a translation is needed along with your International Driving Permit.
What Kind of Car Should You Rent? The west coast of Saipan has a flatter terrain which may make some driving routes more suitable they some more scenic and relaxed. However, a Mustang convertible can make the journey to the north and west coasts more fun. For the southern and inland driving routes, an SUV may actually be the better option. Planning to use the southern part of Saipan? Then that means the roads and comfort of an SUV set-up will be important.
How to Book a Rental Car: If you are considering renting a car in Saipan when you get to Garapan, you can do a bit of window shopping because there are many rental agencies to choose from, including one located next to the I Love Saipan store. However, I recommend that you try to book a rental car prior to arriving in Saipan. This will give you an opportunity to see the various agencies’ license requirements, car insurance info, mileage restrictions, and Chinese speaking rental agents if needed, plus you can choose a specific car rental class. During busy travel times, walk-in rentals may have limited availability for popular and specialty cars, including convertibles.

**Parking, Speed Limits and Driving Basics** When traveling on Saipan, note that it drives on **the right-hand side of the road.** The **speed limits** posted on the road are in **miles per hour.** The maximum limit on most main roads is **35 mph** (about 56 km/h). To avoid fines, be sure to follow the posting and roadside all legally enforceable requests. When renting a car, be sure to check the rental car agreement for fuel costs. Rental cars provide a full tank and should also be returned full. Tell parkers to return the vehicle in the same condition leased. Here on Saipan, there are plenty of places to park. In order to park, check parking rules and to avoid fines. ### 2. Suggested Island Driving Itinerary **Saipan's self drive offerings have exhibits in all 3 regions: the north, south and central. It is a good idea to dedicate one day for the north, then to combine the south and central regions together for another day.** Airport and hotel staff will often give you a map of where the attractions are located. If you don't, do not forget to map out what you will be driving before you go.
For driving purposes and for most of the attractions, the name of the destination can be entered into **Google Maps** or another offline navigation app that you will need to download and do not forget to carry your mobile data. The options are an eSIM, local SIM card and portable Wi-Fi. For multiple mobile devices in a group, portable Wi-Fi is the best and for single mobile users, the eSIM us the best.

North Route
Points of Interest: Mariana Seaside Chapel, Bird Island, and The Grotto
North Route is an easy and relaxing drive with several stops along the way. The route is mostly flat with easy to read roads, and multiple road signs to direct you to all the major attractions. The North Route runs along Middle Road which is the only road going all the way to the north side of the island. There are numerous attractions along the road, and depending on when you visit, blooming flame trees may provide beautiful red and orange flowers along the route in May and June.

The Grotto
The Grotto is located on the northeast coast of Saipan, close to Bird Island. The Grotto is a natural limestone cave that opens to the sea through multiple underwater passageways that have sunlight beaming down, creating a bright blue glow. The Grotto is famous for its incredibly clear water and beautiful rock formations which makes it a popular spot for experienced divers.

Entry to The Grotto can be challenging due to strong waves and tough rocky conditions. In addition, if you wish to dive, you must organize a dive with a local licensed operator. Snorkeling is highly discouraged as conditions can be dangerous and you will need to have local guides. The Grotto is simply stunning whether you choose to visit above the water or take the plunge and explore the waters underneath. Saipan is known for many natural sights but The Grotto is arguably the best.

Because the Blue Hole is connected to the Pacific, there are strong underwater currents. Do not enter the water without a qualified instructor. There are usually instructors monitoring the safety of participants. To dive here, you will need to book a guided diving experience in advance. You will also be provided with the necessary equipment to dive, so you will not need to prepare your own.
Tips: 1) Diving is usually less safe in the afternoons. For this reason, most dives take place in the mornings. 2) When diving, you will need to enter the water with an instructor. There is a staircase with a little over 100 steps, so you need to be fit. Before the dive, it is a good idea to eat a light snack. 3) Diving is very demanding, and the self-drive tour you have planned after the dive should not be long. You can use your self-drive visit to the northern sights after taking a hotel transfer half-day tour and resting at the hotel post-activity. This will give you plenty of time to see the sights afterward.
Mariana Seaside Chapel

Mariana Seaside Chapel Drive 15 to 20 minutes north of the city center along Middle Road. You'll see the Aqua Resort Club and the Mariana Resort, where Mariana Seaside Chapel is located. This white chapel overlooking the coast is a popular wedding photography venue. To find the chapel facing the sea, go to the main entrance of the hotel and walk through the lobby. Usually, access to the hotel grounds and outdoor photography is free, but it's best to confirm the latest resort policies before visiting.

**Last Command Post**
Head 20 minutes north from the hotels and pull over at the Korean Peace Memorial and Last Command Post. Located at the Last Command Post, we find a memorial park dedicated to the Battle of Saipan, which was fought during World War II. This was the last command post area of the Japanese forces before they lost the battle of Saipan in 1944. Some of the remaining relics of war, including rusted tanks, artillery, and other structures are on the site. The park is small and snacks are sold at a few vendors near the parking lot.

Bird Island
Head back to the intersection of Middle Road and Route 322, then go uphill on Route 322. Stay on Middle Road, and at the signs, turn right Bird Island and left to the Blue Grotto.

Bird Island is named for the many seabirds that used to nest there. The island's waters are two distinct shades of blue due to depth and coral. At high tide, the islet is a deep blue Pacific isolated green jewel; at low tide, it connects to the main island.

There is a viewing platform on the Bird Island. Do not forget to pack a small set of binoculars to try to catch the view of the birds. The view from the platform is great at sunrise and sunset. If Bird Island is the last stop on your northern self-drive route, stay for the sunset and enjoy the drive back to the city centre.

**Bird Island** From Suicide Cliff, head back to the intersection of Middle Road and Route 322 (the point where Route 322 goes uphill). Drive on Middle Road and take the sign that says Bird Island to the right and Blue Hole to the left. Bird Island got its name from the numerous birds that used to nest on the island. The surrounding sea is known for its unique bands of light and deep blue. During high tide, the vegetation on the island is totally submerged. During low tide, it remains connected to the main island. There is a turnoff along the road that offers good views. Bring some binoculars to check for birds on the rocks. Sunsets and sunrises can be quite nice from here. If you're on a self-drive tour of northern Guam, you can spend some time at Bird Island before heading back to Tumon or central Guam. 【South + Central Routes】
Classic stops: Mount Carmel Cathedral, Ladder Beach, Obyan Beach, Lau Lau Beach, Tank Beach, Forbidden Island, Mount Tapochau, and the Our Lady of Lourdes Shrine
Unlike the North Route, the South and Central routes have more winding mountain roads and more inconsistent road conditions. The attractions may also be harder to find. They are more suited to drivers who are confident and have experience working with narrow or uneven roads. Along the way, there are several Obyan Beeac, Lau Lau Beach, and Tank Beach. You can enjoy one of the beaches in two ways: 1. Drive to the beach and swim and relax in the sun; 2. Join an ATV tour that goes along the coast.

Tip: If you do not feel comfortable driving along mountain roads, do not self-drive on the South and Central Routes For a more safe and relaxed experience, a guided day tour may be better. You will still be able to see the main attractions without having to drive on difficult roads or miss turnoffs that are difficult to see.

The big Jesus on Mount Tapochau

**Mount Tapochau** Mount Tapochau is about 474 metres tall, which makes it the tallest point on Saipan. To reach the summit by car, head north from the town area along Middle Road, take a turn onto Isa Drive and continue along Tapochau Road. Note that some of the roads nearer the summit are not well maintained, so an appropriate vehicle and cautious driving are important. On a clear day, you can see stunning views all around Saipan from the summit. This includes the coastline as well as a giant Jesus statue that is located nearby. While the Mariana Trench is much deeper than Mount Tapochau, it is also positioned off to the west of the mountain. Because of this, the contrast between the high ground of Saipan and the deep ocean makes the scenery very noticeable.

**Santa Lourdes Shrine** Return to Route 1 and drive for approximately 15 minutes. Turn left onto Chalan Santa Lourdes; Santa Lourdes Shrine will be on your right in less than 10 minutes. The shrine is built around one of Guam's most famous freshwater springs. Local legend says this is the one place on the island that was spared during World War II because the Virgin Mary was protecting it. The grotto is built into a hillside under a big, old, tree that grows rock face and provides a natural canopy. People may drink and collect the spring water so bring a container if you want to try some. Many local residents come here to collect the spring water.

**Tank Beach** To get to Tank Beach, head back along Isa Drive, go to Kagman Road, and make a right. Follow Kagman Road past the golf club entrance to a T-junction by a school. A left turn will take you to Tank Beach, and right is the way to Forbidden Island. This stretch of coast got its name from the military vehicles that were landed during World War II when U.S. forces stormed the beach. Tank Beach is also famous for it star sand, composed of the remains of tiny marine organisms. The sand is a unique feature of the beach. **Forbidden Island** (seen in the distance)

**Forbidden Island**
Head straight until the next T-junction and take a left. Follow the uphill road. There are parts where the road gets really steep so be careful! Towards the end, you should be able to spot the Forbidden Island sign and a flat area nearby where you might be able to park. The walk to the viewpoint takes about 20 minutes and provides great views of Forbidden Island and the coastline. Forbidden Island is a small tidal islet that is located at the southeast coast of Saipan. At high tide, it is separated from the main island but at low tide, a temporary land bridge connects the two. There is a local legend that speaks of a drought ritual that involves a sacrificial offering to the gods to appease them and bring rain.
In addition to the legend, the region is recognized for its remarkable coastal scenery. The slopes are also covered in wildflowers during spring, usually from April to May. The viewpoint provides an aerial perspective of the cliffs and the rocky shore. Adventurous hikers can also go down to the water to snorkel. The water underneath the cliffs is calmer than the open sea and is a natural pool that is sheltered by reef. You can also see sea turtles there. \
The trail down to the cove from the viewpoint generally takes 30 to 40 min. It is located near the Forbidden Island sign and goes through a forest. The trail can be steep, slippery, and poorly marked, and the route may vary after a storm. Be sure to wear solid shoes, bring enough water, and check the weather and sea conditions before entering the water during strong surf or currents. If you do not know the trail, consider hiring a local guide.

Tips:

The hike to the Forbidden Island destination can be steep and tricky; for this reason, it's good to have shoes that can grip the surface well. Since the area is surrounded by forests, it's better to have mosquito repellent handy. If you will be swimming, you need to put on your swimsuit before you start the hike. There are no changing facilities at the bottom of the cliff. The rocks at Forbidden Island are very slippery, so be careful and watch your steps.

Lau Lau Beach
Lau Lau Beach is a popular diving spot in Saipan, and is good for all levels of divers. The waters have lots of sea life, like clown fish and other colorful reef fish. There are also many corals, rocky arches, and different underwater formations. When you reach the beach, please be careful because the access roads can be rocky and they are likely to damage your car if you drive fast. If you want to relax by the beach, you can do so. Also, If you do not want to dive, you do not have to. If you want a diving tour, you have to book either a half-day tour or a full-day tour. There are many diving tours, so you will have to do your own research to find one that is better. Also, most of the tours give you diving instruments and also have a tutor. Most of the tours also provide you with shuttle services to your hotel.?**

Mount Carmel Cathedral
Return to Isa Drive, heading south towards the coast. Take a left turn on Beach Road, and in less than ten minutes you will see the white Mount Carmel Cathedral on your left.

One of the most significant Catholic churches on Saipan, Mount Carmel Cathedral was built during the Spanish colonial times and rebuilt after the Second World War. The white building has a Spanish influence, and a statue of the Virgin Mary with baby Jesus on her lap is located at the entrance. In the garden, many flowers are planted, and during blooming seasons, the flame trees next to the white building create a beautiful sight.

Advice:
The main entrance of the cathedral may be closed, but a side entrance is likely to be open. Sunday services are held in the cathedral, so during worship, visiting is not allowed. The best time to take photos of the front side of the cathedral is around midday or in the afternoon, because the sun directly shines on it.

Ladder Beach and Obyan Beach
After finishing up at Mount Carmel Cathedral, go back into town and get some respite before heading out later in the afternoon to discover Saipan's southern coastline! If you want a more adventurous way of getting around, think about a guided ATV tour.
Ladder Beach and Obyan Beach are calm, and quite undeveloped beaches in Southern Saipan just a short distance from the airport. Ladder Beach has a rough coral shoreline instead of soft sand, so some sturdy water shoes are recommended. At Obyan Beach, you can find clearest of waters and an offshore coral formation, where you may see some colorful fishes in calm sea conditions. Remember that the accessibility and swimming conditions may alter due to weather, tides, and coastal conditions.
ATVs, also known as all-terrain vehicles, are perfect for exploring the unpaved paths and coastal areas of Saipan. Southern route guided tours include transfers to the ATV base, safety briefing, and guided rides by an instructor. Make sure to choose a good operator, stay on the instructor’s route, and use the provided safety gear.

Some last tips

A calm itinerary in Saipan can also include a day trip to Managaha Island, a day at Tinian, and one or two days of driving around Saipan to explore it. This way, you get to experience the island and enjoy it more instead of running out of time. If time permits and the conditions are good, tandem skydiving is an activity to remember.

**Skydiving in Saipan** Saipan has beautiful views of Managaha Island and nice blue waters. You can see the reefs and beautiful shoreline from the air by skydiving. The skydiving operators leave from Saipan International Airport. A lot of them give you a hotel transfer. Before you make a reservation, check the schedule, weather, health and weight restrictions. **Things to Know:** If you have to choose, do not go scuba diving before skydiving. There are both involve pressure changes and diving before skydiving increases the risk of getting decompression sickness. The minimum recommended time is about 12-24 hours, and after deeper dives, you have to wait longer. Snorkeling is not the same. While diving at Lau Lau Beach, ensure you have confirmed the right time with a qualified professional before diving because shallow dives are not risk-free.

Visit Saipan's food scene and you may be interested in the Chamorro Night Market in Garapan. This weekly event happens every Thursday and has a plethora of food stalls and vendors selling clothing and handicrafts. The event also has local music and cultural performances. While there, you can try local drinks like tuba and eat specialty foods like  apigigi which is a food that is wrapped in coconut leaves, and red rice (hineksa agaga). Sample the Chamorro cuisine and get some souvenirs from the market.

Another option could be the sunset barbecue dinner at Hyatt Regency Saipan, which is also a resort that was showcased in the final episode of Season 2 of a Running Man Chinese reality show. Sunset barbecue dinner is one of Saipan's known dinner activities. You get to eat Chamorro food and watch cultural shows like fire dancing and Chamorro dancing. Performers invite guests on stage, so don't be shy.

Most dinners start at 6:00 pm and you need to book in advance. Booking online is ideal so you can arrive at the resort a bit earlier.

Another dining experience to consider in Saipan is a seafood buffet. Seafood buffets have previously been enjoyed at Hyatt Regency Saipan and Aqua Resort Club, but due to COVID-19, their schedules and menus may have changed. Contact them directly.

Hyatt Regency Saipan is located on Micro Beach in the center, and is a short distance from the shops and retail area of central Garapan. Aqua Resort Club is a bit further north on the west coast of Saipan near Middle Road, so it can be a good lunch or dinner stop when you are going north along the island.

Conclusion

Self drive trips on Saipan are ideal because of the short distances between places. An island loop is possible without needing to rush to make passing points. There are many beaches, view points, and restaurants along your route. Make sure to have a plan for what you want to do and check the opening hours and make reservations. You will have the best time exploring the island.

Golden Gate Bridge—if you haven't taken a photo here, do you really claim to have visited San Francisco?


The Golden Gate Bridge (Golden Gate Bridge) is a symbol of San Francisco and even California. The blue water reflects the orange-red structure of the bridge, and the beauty of the bay also benefits from it.

The Golden Gate Bridge is also a frequent setting in Hollywood blockbusters. However, typically bridges appear in disaster movies such as "San Andreas," "Rise of the Planet of the Apes," and "X-Men," being destroyed over and over again......


Walking or cycling along the bridge with sea breezes is the best way to appreciate this magnificent structure. Dedicated walkways are set up on both sides of the bridge, with bicycle access allowed on the west side.


There are many viewing points along the bridge, and everyone can choose a few based on their own route:

The Golden Gate Bridge Pavilion on the south side features extensive parking, mountain views ideal for morning photography. Visitors can explore visitor centers and educational exhibits here.

The Vista Point on the north side offers a spectacular downward view of the Golden Gate Bridge (just off the bridge), perfect for capturing morning light and cityscapes.

Fort Point below on the south-eastern side provides an excellent vantage point from below the bridge, ideal for photography with favorable morning lighting.

The western Marin Headlands on the north side offers extensive parking along the way and panoramic views of the Golden Gate Bridge.

Battery Spencer, the observation tower located westward on the other side of the bridge, is also quite excellent.

If you want to drive onto the bridge, take US Highway 101 or SR 1.


For a chance to see the misty Golden Gate Bridge at its best, visit in the morning when it's most likely to occur.


On the Golden Gate Bridge area, the most famous is the boat cruise. Taking a boat tour on the water and viewing the bridge from another angle offers a unique experience.


You can also choose different routes. While visiting the Golden Gate Bridge, also explore the Bay Bridge to fully enjoy San Francisco Bay Area.


Golden Gate Bridge

·Constructed with over 100,000 tons of steel, this is the symbol of San Francisco. The orange-red bridge spans across the San Francisco Bay and the Golden Gate Strait, creating an impressive and majestic landmark.

·During the Gold Rush era, it was named because it resembled a gateway to gold mining territory. The observation point on the northern side offers the best panoramic view of the entire bridge.

·The bridge frequently appears in many major American films such as "Rise of the Planet of the Apes" and "X-Men".

·Dedicated walkways are specially built on both sides of the bridge. Walking across it with coastal breezes or cycling back and forth are excellent experiences.


Fisherman's Wharf – The liveliest, most "delicious" pier


Fisherman's Wharf generally covers the area from Ghirardelli Square to Pier 39. The landmark of Fisherman's Wharf is a round sign featuring a large crab; find this one and you've arrived at Fisherman's Wharf.


A dazzling array of shops, thrilling and quirky wax museums, uniquely flavorful restaurants, and captivating street performances are enough to make your visit unforgettable.

Certainly when coming here you cannot miss the famous sourdough bread. It is both healthy and natural, crafted using traditional methods – a must-try delicacy for any San Francisco visitor.

The renowned old establishment Boudin Bakery here is very famous; I recommend trying it!

As a pier, the seafood offered here will surely satisfy your appetite. If you're interested in seafood and can get up early, head to Pier 47 at dawn. Watch the lively scene of fishermen unloading their catch and get fresh seafood first-hand!


You must not miss Pier 39; it is famous for its reputation and rich content to explore – the highlight of Fisherman's Wharf.

Moreover, this area serves as a sea lion habitat. Even before reaching the shoreline, you can hear their continuous calls. As you get closer, you'll see numerous lazy sea lions basking in the sun on floating platforms beneath the pier, giving you an opportunity for close encounters with these fascinating marine mammals.

Lombard Street - The Most Challenging Road for New Drivers


Lombard Street is actually a section of this famous road, situated between Hyde Street at the higher elevation and Leavenworth Street at the lower point. This steep incline features eight sharp switchbacks along its short stretch. The combination of the slope and winding Z-shaped design forces all traffic to descend in one direction only.


Experienced drivers can use this challenging route as a way to test their own driving skills;

New drivers should still stroll leisurely through it. The flower beds on both sides of the lane are filled with roses, and residents on both sides of the street also grow flowers and plants at their doorsteps. During the blooming season in June and July, from afar, it looks like a velvet embroidery hanging diagonally, extremely beautiful.

From the higher part of Hyde Street, you can overlook the bay and "Alcatraz." If you want to capture the complete winding effect, take photos from the lower elevation on Leavenworth Street.

United States Palace of Fine Arts

The original American Palace of Fine Arts in San Francisco was built in 1915. It was constructed for the Panama "Pan-American International Exposition" and attracted 18 million visitors during that time, but it was abandoned after the exposition ended. It wasn't until 1962 when famous German-American architect Mebbeck was commissioned to design a reconstruction - he specialized in detailed decoration work, which is the origin of this Palace of Fine Arts' exquisitely carved style.

Today, the Palace of Fine Arts has become a favorite leisure destination for San Francisco locals. Swans and ducks frolic together in the pond, and the Palace of Fine Arts Theater is also located here—a wonderful place to relax during your holidays.


Alcatraz Island—No demons present, yet equally thrilling


Alcatraz Island was formerly a prison where the US government incarcerated notorious criminals. Today, the historic prison remains well-preserved and open to visitors. Under dim lighting, within cold cells, you can learn about the prison's structure and history through an audio guide, and even hear original recordings from the inmates who were once held there.


Beyond its eerie reputation as a prison, numerous ghost stories abound. For adventure enthusiasts, special nighttime tours are offered on the island to enhance your experience with added excitement.

Alcatraz Island

Alcatraz Island is a 22-acre, rocky small island, this was where US federal government once imprisoned serious criminals. Here housed notorious inmates including Chicago "Don" Capone, ruthless yet gifted "Birdman" Stroud and cold-blooded "Machine Gun" killer Kelly etc. Prisoners could only through prison iron windows gaze at vibrant San Francisco, accepting another cruel spiritual punishment. Now entire island strives to preserve original prison atmosphere, on the island there are also bird park, museum, bookstore facilities. In island stores, sold memoirs of those prisoners whose sentences ended, they all claim that was a "painfully unforgettable experience".


Union Island - A Sanctuary for Wildlife

San Francisco City Hall

The San Francisco City Hall is the government building at the center of municipal administration in San Francisco, USA. It features a distinctive Collegiate Gothic architectural style, and its massive dome ranks fifth among the world's largest domes. The current City Hall replaced the old one that was completely destroyed in the 1906 San Francisco earthquake. For architecture enthusiasts, this place offers an undisputed visual feast.

Chinatown——Where Every Chinese Person Finds a Sense of Belonging


San Francisco's Chinatown (Chinatown San Francisco) is the only place in Western America comparable to New York's Chinatown, located at the intersection of Grant Avenue and California Street.


The streets resemble Hong Kong-style architecture from last century, with a nostalgic feel reminiscent of 1980s Hong Kong movies. All the signs you see are written in Chinese characters, and all you hear is Mandarin - it's a place that feels very familiar to Chinese people.

San Francisco Chinatown

San Francisco's Chinatown is the only place in Western America comparable to New York's Chinatown, with the intersection of Grant Avenue and California Street forming its center.

There are approximately eighty thousand plus overseas Chinese residents living here, making it completely a city within a country, filled with exotic charm. Although San Francisco's grandest Union Square and the western financial center are located right beside Chinatown, inside Chinatown you'll see signs written in Chinese characters, and everything you hear is spoken in Mandarin.


California Academy of Sciences—Sailing Through the Ocean of Natural Science


The California Academy of Sciences is absolutely a paradise for natural science enthusiasts. It's the only venue in the world that combines an aquarium, planetarium, natural history museum, and four-story tropical rainforest into one.


You can watch scientists at work through glass partitions, and feel free to ask them questions if you have any. Every Thursday evening, there's a "Museum Night" event waiting for you to explore.

California Science Museum

The California Science Center is the first comprehensive museum in America integrating marine, terrestrial, and aerial biology, combining education, exhibits, and research into one natural history institution. It ranks among the top 10 natural history museums worldwide and serves as a leading international center for modern natural science research. The facility features the Steinhart Aquarium, Morrison Planetarium—the world's largest fully digital planetarium, Kimball Natural History Museum, and an indoor tropical rainforest. At the planetarium, visitors can experience breathtaking cosmic journeys; at the aquarium, explore the underwater coral reef world where over 2,000 fish species live.

This is also home to the world's largest breathing ecological living roof. Through glass partitions you can watch scientists at work. If you have questions, feel free to ask directly. You can even go up to the second floor's Natural Science Center to seek answers from scientists in person.


It is highly recommended that you choose the San Francisco CityPASS. With one ticket in hand, save time on planning itineraries, avoid queuing times, and enjoy 50% off admission fees - extremely cost-effective!

Monterey——Embrace a bohemian lifestyle in this seaside town


Monterey is a seaside town in northern California, with the reputation of California's "first city". It can be reached by driving from San Francisco along Highway 1 for about 90 minutes.


It is recommended to drive, cycle or even hike on the famous 17-Mile Drive, admiring the winding Pacific coastline scenery between Monterey town and Carmel town.

There's also Pebble Beach Golf Links, Cannery Row, the Monterey Bay Aquarium, Carmel Beach, and more. The streets are filled with charming boutiques, restaurants, hotels, and art galleries worth exploring.

IV. Transportation Recommendations


External Transportation


San Francisco has three airports in total, San Francisco International Airport (SFO) is the main airport of San Francisco, located in the southern part of San Francisco. Oakland International Airport (OAK) is on the east coast, spanning the Bay Bridge. And Norman Y. Mineta San Jose International Airport (SJC) is one hour's drive south of the city.


Car service for airport pickup and drop-off can be booked at San Francisco Airport.


Internal Transportation


Many places in the US are suitable for self-driving, but San Francisco is one of the cities not ideal for driving due to extremely steep street slopes and expensive parking.

However, San Francisco has a well-developed public transportation system. Buses, subways, historic cable cars, and trams all offer options that can serve your journey, taking you to explore every corner of this city.


Cable cars are located in downtown and represent San Francisco's most famous vintage form of transportation, remaining extremely popular.


There are three routes: Powell-Hyde, Powell-Mason, and California Street. Riding the cable car feels like traveling back to San Francisco a century ago - the clanging motion creates an incredibly nostalgic atmosphere, very pleasant.


The F-line streetcar travels between Market St. and Fisherman's Wharf.

You can purchase tickets at various ticket outlets, or buy them from the conductor after boarding.


Naturally, relying solely on public transportation for sightseeing has its limitations; you may also choose to rent a car. However, San Francisco's city streets are quite hilly and not very friendly to new drivers, though experienced drivers might enjoy the challenge.


If planning to visit attractions around San Francisco, it is more recommended to rent a car for your trip.


V. Recommended Accommodation Areas


San Francisco is the birthplace of boutique hotels, featuring uniquely styled rooms. Accommodation rates are mostly between $100-$300 and a nightly parking fee of $35-$50. Most visitors choose to stay near Union Square, offering convenient transportation and easier access to attractions.

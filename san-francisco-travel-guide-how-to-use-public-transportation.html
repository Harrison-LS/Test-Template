San Francisco Travel Guide: How to Use Public Transportation

It’s peak travel season again. Lately, the streets of San Francisco have been flooded with tourists, which made me realize that the annual summer vacation season is here once more. Traveling has become a common pastime, but in a country like the United States, which is particularly well suited to road trips, are there any convenient alternatives to driving yourself?

As someone who has lived in San Francisco for nearly ten years, I’d like to give you a complete overview of the city’s public transportation options!


Muni Buses


Fare: $2.75

Validity: You can take unlimited rides with your ticket within two hours.


Compared with other U.S. cities, San Francisco has a fairly extensive bus system. Wherever you want to go within the city, Muni can usually get you there with the right connections.

Muni is a unique bus system. Unlike other cities, where buses are simply called buses, San Francisco’s entire public transit system has its own branding and distinctive name: Muni.


A typical Muni bus looks like the one shown below 👇. It’s a standard city bus and generally isn’t particularly fast. However, some routes have suffixes such as X or R—for example, the 8X—which usually indicates an express service. Express buses make fewer stops, with greater distances between them, so they can save you quite a bit of time when you’re in a hurry.


Now let’s talk about another type of Muni. Although it looks like a regular Muni vehicle when running at street level, it travels on rails. These lines are usually identified by letters, such as the K and L lines. Unlike regular Muni vehicles, they can run both on the street and underground. When underground, they feel similar to a subway. These Muni lines generally travel farther toward the outskirts of the city, such as the Sunset District in San Francisco and beyond.


Muni Fares


1. Single-ride ticket

A Muni ticket looks like this 👇

The ticket shows an expiration time. After you pay your fare upon boarding, the driver will give you a ticket and tear it at a specific time, which indicates when the ticket expires. Until then, you can use the ticket to ride any regular Muni service. Once it expires, you will need to pay again. Of course, if you’re lucky, the driver might tear your ticket all the way to the end of the day first thing in the morning. 😂


2. Muni monthly pass

Besides purchasing individual tickets, Muni also offers monthly passes, as shown below 👇 The passes come in different colors each month and year. A monthly pass is suitable for people who need to ride Muni about twice a day almost every day. If you only use Muni occasionally, buying a monthly pass is not recommended.


3. Clipper

Since Muni only accepts the exact cash fare and does not provide change, you may wonder what to do if you do not have exact change. Most people use the item shown below 👇, called a Clipper card. Its design has not changed much, so it still looks like this today. Clipper cards are available at major stores such as Walgreens. You can load any amount onto the card, then tap it when boarding and again when getting off, just like transit cards back home.


BART


Fare: $?? (calculated according to distance traveled)

Validity: Each ticket is for one trip and cannot be reused.


For regular commuting, especially for office workers, BART is probably one of the most common options. Many people who work in downtown San Francisco live quite far away. Although some buses may reach their destinations, they are relatively slow, so many people choose BART because it is faster and more efficient. BART is also one of the best options for traveling to the airport or crossing the Bay to places such as Berkeley and Oakland.


In terms of infrastructure, public transit in the United States is definitely not as modern as back home. BART is somewhat run-down, gets especially noisy underground, and has fairly dated interiors.


Since BART fares are based on distance, every ticket machine displays fare information. The first price is for a one-way trip, and the second is for a round trip. If you buy a round-trip ticket, remember to retrieve it when exiting the fare gate so you can use it again. Round-trip tickets do not have a time limit.


Tips:

1. The only restriction on BART is the fare amount. For example, if you bought a $7.50 ticket to Station A but need to get off at Station B for some reason, and the correct fare is $8.75, you will need to use a machine to add the difference before exiting through the fare gates.

2. The Clipper card mentioned in the Muni section can also be used on BART. Simply tap your card when entering and exiting the fare gates.


Taxi


Fare: Starting at $3.50

Availability: Get in or out at any time


There are fewer and fewer taxis in San Francisco. The taxi industry was already struggling, and since Uber and Lyft arrived, taxis have become relatively uncommon. They are still available, of course. If you do not trust newer ride-hailing services like Uber and Lyft and cannot find a taxi on the street, you can call a taxi company at 333-3333.

When I first came to the United States, Uber and Lyft did not exist yet. Before every trip to the airport, I would book a taxi in advance and arrange a pickup time and location. It was convenient, though a little expensive.


San Francisco taxis come in many sizes, especially at the airport. One time, I returned with my family carrying about four large suitcases, two carry-on suitcases, and several backpacks and tote bags of various sizes. While we were waiting for a taxi at the airport, the staff arranged a vehicle based on our needs. We ended up with an enormous SUV taxi that looked big enough for moving house. 😂 It was very practical!


Taxi fares can be paid in cash or by card. However, keep in mind that San Francisco is a tipping city. If you pay in cash, you should add a tip. If you pay by card, the system will prompt you to select a tip amount, such as 15% or 20%.


Tips:

Personally, if you do not want to tip as much as 15%, paying in cash gives you more flexibility.


UBER & Lyft


Fare: Varies by route

Convenience: Book a ride in advance for fast and convenient service


Uber and Lyft are pioneers of app-based ride-hailing and have become essential means of transportation for people living in San Francisco. If you live in the city and use Uber or Lyft regularly, you may often receive discount coupons, making each ride more affordable.


Overall, Uber and Lyft are much cheaper than taxis. There are plenty of drivers in San Francisco, and the service is generally quite good.

Neither company is necessarily better than the other. I usually compare their prices before choosing one, as the level of service is about the same.


You will also often find drivers who work for both Uber and Lyft. You can request a ride at any time through either company’s mobile app. Simply enter your pickup point and destination, then wait for a driver to accept the ride. The process is similar to using Didi.


Both Uber and Lyft allow you to add multiple stops. If people in your group are getting off at different locations, you can conveniently book a single ride for everyone. You can also schedule a ride in advance for a specific pickup time. This is useful if you need a ride at an unusual hour or think it may be difficult to find one at that time. You can also choose a vehicle type based on your needs.

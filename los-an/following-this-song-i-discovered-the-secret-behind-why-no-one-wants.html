Following this song, I discovered the secret behind why no one wants to leave San Francisco.

🌼 Wear Flowers Upon Your Head, Unbind Your Inner Self 🌼

Once I really loved "San Francisco," the theme song from "Forrest Gump." The lyrics inside always make you feel that even if various big and small things in life demoralize you again, this energetic city of San Francisco always has a way to restore your energy.

Just like the lyrics sing ⬇️


When listening to the song, you always wonder why one would wear a few flowers on their head? Only after delving deeper into it did this question find its answer.


🌸In this three-seaside-enclosed city, the climate is very friendly, and flowers and all things naturally unfold.

🌸People walking on the street have "faces blooming like flowers." Even without wearing flowers on their heads, they seem to have planted a sunflower in their hearts.

🌸The city's relaxed atmosphere enables people to quickly shed their heavy "turtle shells," instantly capturing a vacation mindset.


San Francisco also has a translation I prefer: "Saanfanshi." Beyond being a phonetic transliteration, the action of wearing flowers on one's head pairs better with this name. The character "Fan" carries water and grass elements, creating ideal conditions for floral growth.


Here you encounter fascinating things: same-sex couples joyfully hold hands and kiss on the street, completely free from worrying about others' judgments;

Programmers of different skin colors excitedly discuss code in a café, creating a harmonious scene; street food from all over the world extends olive branches to you—here your Chinese stomach can transform into a global palate.


🌼 Wear flowers on head, loosen inner self 🌼


Here you can temporarily let your mind rest and freely be a salted fish. If you want to peacefully be a salted fish, first go to Fisherman's Wharf to feel the salty sea breeze.


Sea breeze at Fisherman's Wharf


Many port cities have Fisherman's Wharfs, but San Francisco's is unique: besides eating seafood and enjoying the sea breeze, it's also a gathering place for street artists. Various music performances play 365 days a year, creating your most California memories.


Pier 39 is the best street to explore at Fisherman's Wharf. The most eye-catching feature here is the large crab sculpture that gestures friendly greetings to everyone, so next comes: eat it!


Dungeness Crab is very famous here—actually it's "King Crab."

Mid-November to June of the next year are the best times to eat it, whether boiled, baked, or roasted... The flavor will make you taste every bite.


Over 100 wonderful shops make you feel a day isn't enough to explore them, even if you walk into flat feet it doesn't matter.


Watching sea lions lazily sleep with adorable expressions, they must be dreaming beautifully. Occasionally when someone on the opposite shore looks up and roars a few times, it seems like they are the rulers of Pier 39.


Recommend not driving there! Parking fees are super expensive, and parking spots are also very limited (the parking garage directly opposite charges $12/hour).

Better to rent a bicycle, chase the sunset by Pier 39, mix with local football fans, runners, and cyclists, spending an incredibly comfortable afternoon.


Golden Gate Bridge Sunset View


As a must-visit destination for west coast trips, the Golden Gate Bridge's aesthetic is genuinely impressive, especially at sunset.


Frequently featured in films, it sometimes appears stunning but more often gets dramatically destroyed.

In countless blockbusters like Mission Impossible, X-Men, Rise of Planet, Terminator, it always ends tragically. Curious about how large the psychological shadow area from the Golden Gate Bridge is?


Sometimes death comes to visit it↓


The changing light each day, against the backdrop of this vast canvas of sky, has made photographing with the Golden Gate Bridge a global trend


Twin Peaks overlooking the city


When discussing San Francisco's most charming sights, besides the Golden Gate Bridge, there's also the panoramic view from Twin Peaks.


Especially when reaching the summit as night approaches. As the city begins to glow with distant sunset still lingering on the horizon, experiencing this wonderful transition at day's end atop the mountain is simply one of the most delightful experiences on Earth.


Actually when you reach the top, you'll discover that compared to New York's towering skyscrapers, San Francisco's skyline appears somewhat modest. This is probably because while maintaining its position as a financial and technology hub, this city also wants residents to enjoy life more comfortably.


Tip: After evening, it gets quite cold on the mountain. If you plan to view night scenery, bring a jacket to avoid catching a chill.


Unwind your mind at coffee shops


Have to say, San Francisco has so many coffee shops! It's like an European version, with some being stunning viewpoints as well.


For example, Warming Hut Bookstore & Cafe located by the seaside, inside a national park, is one of the best viewing spots for Golden Gate Bridge.


Besides the above scenic-focused establishment, these coffee shops below are locals' favorites. Not only is the food delicious, but each shop's design is intriguing too.


Wrecking Ball Coffee


Co-founder Trish is a passionate traveler to global coffee-growing regions, journeying like Tang Monk on pilgrimage to each coffee bean-producing place, seeking wisdom from coffee professionals worldwide.


The storefront is small and simple, but the coffee inside is top-tier San Francisco flavor.


The large murals on the walls create a strong visual impact; chatting or working in this art-filled café might have inspiration quietly knocking at your door.


The "Flowers and Pineapple" photo wall here is an Instagram popular spot. Whether it's people or coffee, everything looks fresh and clean together with it.


Address: 2271 Union Street, San Francisco, CA 94123

Business hours: Monday to Friday, 7:00 AM to 6:00 PM

Saturday and Sunday, 8:00 AM to 6:00 PM

(Closes at 6:00 PM, too inhumane 😭)


Le Marais


From the name alone, you can guess it might be related to Paris. Indeed, it's a super authentic French bakery + coffee shop.


Here are complete French breakfasts and lunches; the restaurant is extremely selective about ingredients—all must be the best, such as various organic fruits and vegetables.


A comfortable and bright style, a French romantic breakfast experience—this place is simply a charging station for both soul and body.


Location: 498 Sanchez St, San Francisco, CA 94114

Hours: Monday–Friday 10:00-19:00

Saturday 10:00-18:00; Sunday closed

(San Francisco shops tend to close quite early)


A summer city filled with love ☀️


Continuing to follow the music's flow, I've discovered that besides making one feel incredibly relaxed, San Francisco's summer time is also full of love.


This place has mild winters and summers, abundant sunlight—summer temperatures rarely exceed 20°C, truly nourishing weather perfect for escaping the heat.


Highway 1: Experiencing Love and Freedom


In American movies I watched as a child, there were scenes of carefree road trips by the sea (though I'm not sure if it was Highway 1 or not), and from that time on, I planted a desire in my heart to play on the West Coast.


Highway 1, this road connecting San Francisco and Los Angeles, is rated as one of the fifty places you must visit in your lifetime. Here you can not only see the most charming scenery on the West Coast but also create a sunniest memory.


Driving while playing music, both eyes and ears get greatly relaxed; this is definitely a feeling of love and freedom.


When you're tired from driving, you can also get out to explore along the way...


17 Mile Drive


The 17 Mile Drive (17 miles) is one of the most beautiful sections of Highway 1. With its expansive view of azure waters, both body and mind feel a sense of bliss. Without crowds of people bustling around, these seabirds that nest here are truly the "owners".


Besides the ocean, driving passes various estates and mansions as well as golf courses; the charming scenery makes California's wealthy willing to settle here.


Although US highways are free, this is a private area so you also need to pay $10.50 as an "entrance fee." Truly envying how the wealthy can whimsically purchase a stretch of golden coastline for themselves.


Danish Village Solvang


Along Highway 1 besides ocean views, small specialty towns like Danish Village are also worth seeing. Located in the middle section of Highway 1, although not seaside, it's still full of exploration fun.


At first, a Danish immigrant who came to America in the early 20th century wanted to recreate their previous living environment upon arriving at this foreign land, so planned out a Danish-style town on the west coast edge.


Wandering through the Danish-style village, the galleries along the road, dessert shops, and handicraft stores seem to wrap people into Andersen's fairy tales.


After exhausting myself in this enchanted realm, I'll transition to exploring a dream-like dimension.

Let us first bid farewell to Highway 1 and return once more to San Francisco downtown.


Time Travel in Downtown San Francisco


Intersection of California Street & Powell Street


San Francisco streets are all hilly, the most distinctive one is this one - it seems to take you into the movie "Inception."


Amazingly, old cable cars still run on this street. Cable cars are San Francisco's most representative old-style wooden cable cars. Riding them to view the city scenery, you can't tell if you're in San Francisco from 20 years ago or today.


Chinatown


Going down this road further, you'll also pass Chinatown - stretching between San Francisco's stylish upscale buildings, the somewhat retro Chinatown makes you experience time travel.


Maybe it's because there are so many Chinese people in San Francisco, making up 12% of the total population - here is the most authentic Chinese food in America. It is said that 100,000 Chinese immigrants live near Chinatown. Eating, shopping, sending packages, depositing and withdrawing money... they have settled in here with real gusto.


          The Mandarin and Cantonese echoing in your ears instantly transports you across the Pacific Ocean. For tourists, this is a large place to satisfy cravings. If you can't stomach American food, come here and indulge freely in hot spicy soup, hot pot, and stir-fried dishes!


          Art and Exotic Charm of the Mission District


          If you want to see the exotic charm of San Francisco, besides Chinatown, there's also this place. The Mission District completely defies expectations - it's far from the typical postcard views tourists recognize. This area represents a hidden gem most visitors never discover.


          Street Art Culture Showcase


          Graffiti art surrounds every corner, embodying the vibrant spirit of this youthful artistic neighborhood. Visitors can enjoy these incredible wall murals completely free - no admission fee required.


          Early Mexican and Latino cultural influences have shaped this area's unique character. Balmy Alley features stunning murals exploring diverse themes - celebrating Latin American heritage and depicting LGBTQ+ rights struggles.


This huge graffiti house is called Women's Building, one of the most distinctive graffiti houses in San Francisco. The world's most representative women are all painted on this building. For example, our most familiar Guanyin Bodhisattva even has a mural here.


In short, whether it's beautiful graffiti murals or delicious food, this street is a vibrant paradise.


Mission Community Market


In the church district, the local specialty market is also quite fun. Fresh fruits and vegetables picked by farmers, homemade pastries from home, charming handmade crafts... Wonderful and vivid things are presented one after another in front of your eyes, plus live band performances, this atmosphere creates great comfort.


Opening hours: Every Thursday 4pm~8pm

Location: San Francisco 84 Bartlett St


👱New Generation Gathering Place👩


I, who am obsessed with the song "San Francisco," discovered in the lyrics this passage: "People are changing, a new generation has new interpretations."

Isn't this just talking about Silicon Valley!


The world's newest ideas sprout in Silicon Valley. For those new humans who want to bring something different to the world, in this city they can find many interesting souls with like-minded interests.


In the eyes of new humans, Silicon Valley's brilliance is dazzlingly bright. Global internet giants like Google and Facebook established their headquarters in the San Francisco Bay Area, while various high-tech companies cluster together.


Take a stroll around the companies and schools where new humans gather


Google Headquarters — Googleplex


Mountain View, Santa Clara County, California, USA, near San Francisco city


Regardless of internal or external perspectives, Google headquarters Googleplex can be called one of the happiest workplaces. It very much resembles a university campus, with free Google-style bicycles as transportation tools on campus.


You can ride Google's bicycle around for a stroll, pretending to be a student of "Google University."


The inside of the company is where the highlights are - delicious food and fun activities are all prepared for you by the company. Except for workstations, meeting rooms and tea rooms throughout the company each have their own style, feel free to explore them. However, without a guide from an internal employee, outsiders cannot enter the office area.


Facebook's New Building——MPK 20


Menlo Park in San Francisco Bay Area


Facebook's new headquarters in the Bay Area is simply an art gallery equipped with large ecological gardens.


Although workstations are densely packed, the company provides employees with quite a few interesting public spaces. The rooftop botanical garden is a man-made plant healing sanctuary - when you're tired from work, come here to explore.


The office area is filled with vibrant style, graffiti art, installation art... you can pretend you're at an art museum viewing exhibits


Staying in the office is more comfortable than being at home - this is truly what 996 brings as a blessing.


Silicon Valley is so impressive, thanks to the world's top universities in San Francisco, such as Stanford University, UC Berkeley, San Francisco University, San Francisco Art Institute...


With backing from so many prestigious universities and a constant influx of external talent, Silicon Valley truly has too many brilliant minds!


Stanford University


In the QS World University Rankings, Stanford is ranked second globally. Nobel Prizes, Turing Awards, Fields Medals... it's absolutely not unusual here anyway! Alumni just keep winning awards nonstop! Academic geniuses love running to neighboring Silicon Valley, and if they accidentally start a business, they end up creating the world's most impressive companies.


Stanford is home not only to academically brilliant students but also to stunning campus architecture. Spanish-style buildings paired with proud palm trees create a unique West Coast aesthetic.


Academic geniuses here are particularly entrepreneurial. Founders of global giants like Google, Yahoo, Nike, and Snapchat all graduated from Stanford!


UC Berkeley


It is the most prestigious university within the University of California system. In the 2018-19 US News Global Ranking, UCB ranked fourth worldwide.

This is also where Zhang Ailing and Masayoshi Son studied. UCB does not lack either literary talent or business acumen.


Like Stanford, various world-class awards are completely unremarkable at UCB; Nobel Prizes are practically everyday occurrences. Here, you can buy a school t-shirt, stroll along the routes where they studied and ate, and absorb some of the academic aura from these top students.


Many locals also like to come here to take wedding photos. Whether it's the architecture or the greenery on campus, everything is extremely photogenic.


The San Francisco Bay Area, this gathering place for trendsetters and new generations, naturally cannot do without imagination and art.


Ice Cream Museum


The Ice Cream Museum is a sweet space full of girly charm. When it held its exhibition in New York, 30,000 tickets were sold out in just five days! Both David Beckham and Katy Perry strongly recommended this heavenly place!


These sweets that can only be viewed but not eaten exist with dramatic flair, constructing a sweet world.


Fruits, ice cream, bananas, candy - all in bright, bold colors designed to catch your eye. Feel free to casually pose and create interesting angles, play around with the exhibits.


Colorful popsicles bring a sense of decadence. Eating sweets may be called "self-indulgence," but it's happiness!


Address: 1 Grant Ave, San Francisco, CA 94108

Opening Hours: Monday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday 12:30-20:00

Saturday: 09:30-22:30 Sunday: 09:30-22:00

Tuesday closed (this closing day is a bit unusual)


San Francisco Museum of Modern Art SFMOMA


To see exhibitions, how could you miss SFMOMA? It's the first modern art museum on America's West Coast, divided into the old building and new building. The old building is located within the Veterans Building in downtown; the new building sits right next to the old one with completely different architectural style. Due to its unique design, it has become a target for photographers around the world.


The new building features a modern, stylish design, creating a striking contrast between old and new with the retro-style museum in the back.


SFMOMA, housing 33,000 exhibits, is completely a place where San Francisco's artistic spirit is unleashed.


Address: 151 3rd St, San Francisco, CA 94103

Business Hours: Normally 10:00-17:00; Thursday extended to 21:00; Wednesday closed


LGBT-Friendly City


Art is not just exhibits displayed inside galleries; performance art like the LGBT Pride Parade is also quite interesting.


The world's largest LGBT Pride event takes place in San Francisco. The San Francisco Pride Parade is a massive celebration of LGBTQ+ friends and allies, bringing incredible liveliness every year with countless creative surprises.


After all, Apple CEO Tim Cook has openly come out! Silicon Valley programmers have nothing to fear about it! Cook even said: "If hearing that Apple's CEO is gay can help you reduce confusion and loneliness, or inspire people to strive for equality, then I believe sharing my privacy is valuable."


The Castro District — Neighborhood of Gay Life


Rainbow flags everywhere, lively bars, wonderful shops to browse... arriving at the Castro (The Castro) makes one unusually excited.


The Castro Theatre


Perhaps the most famous thing here is none other than the Castro Theatre itself. Sitting in this historic theater, every Saturday night you can enjoy some of the best organ performances. Besides, despite its vintage charm, there's a good selection of avant-garde films inside.


Skin Color Peace, Culinary Harmony


This sunny city, so incredibly friendly to the LGBT community, has inclusivity as its greatest charm. People from different races around the world brainstorm together in meeting rooms, chat leisurely at cafes, walk their dogs and picnic together in parks — scenes like this make both mind and body feel comfortable.


Strolling through San Francisco's streets, wandering from towering skyscrapers into retro Chinatown, then around the corner to the Church District's wall of graffiti.

Different styles blend perfectly here without seeming strange at all.


Holding a Mexican taco in one hand and Japanese sushi in the other, letting your stomach take a world tour within a single city.


This city doesn't have a distinctly defined style, yet style permeates every corner. If you had to summarize it in one sentence, it would be "makes everyone feel comfortable"—open, inclusive, warm, authentic.


Everything looks great through the camera lens; everything feels wonderfully relaxing. This is why every visitor doesn't want to leave. The psychedelic streets and cityscapes are already captivating enough, but what truly fascinates people about San Francisco is that it gives everyone the freedom to be themselves.

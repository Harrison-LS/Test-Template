Note: San Francisco VS San Francisco Bay Area


Every time we mention San Francisco, terms like San Fran, SF Bay Area, Bay Area, and Silicon Valley inevitably come up. Let's first clarify these regional concepts.


First of all, when we commonly refer to San Francisco (San Francisco), it is a city located in northern California along America's West Coast. It is California's only consolidated city-county administrative division. Its phonetic transliteration includes "Saint Francisco" or "Sanpan." This means that both San Francisco and Sanpan refer to the same location.

The San Francisco Bay Area (San Francisco Bay Area), is usually abbreviated as the Bay Area, which can be understood as the greater San Francisco metropolitan area. The Bay Area mainly includes San Francisco and multiple surrounding counties. If divided by region, it can simply be categorized into five main parts: the City of San Francisco, Peninsula, South Bay, North Bay, and East Bay.


And Silicon Valley, the high-tech hub that we all know well, is actually an alternative name for "Santa Clara Valley in California." It's not just a single city but composed of many cities, with its core being the South Bay area.


What to do in San Francisco


The downtown is divided into three parts


As a renowned tourist destination in Northern California, San Francisco's downtown can be simply categorized into three major areas:


Downtown area: Located at the heart of San Francisco, featuring the vibrant Union Square and the Financial District, known as "Wall Street of the American West Coast"


Fisherman's Wharf area: Northeastern part of downtown, a must-visit destination for every San Francisco traveler. From here you can also visit Alcatraz Island, once infamous as America's most notorious federal penitentiary


Other areas: Centered around the iconic Golden Gate Bridge, which represents this district. Travelers frequently arrive at these locations - the striking Golden Gate Bridge and Haight-Ashbury, birthplace of the hippie movement, are all must-visit spots


Places to explore nearby


If you appreciate fine wines, Napa Valley is an essential stop - as a premier wine region, it will absolutely satisfy any oenophile's palate in one visit;

If you enjoy seeing tall trees, choose one of these locations: Muir Woods National Monument, Redwood National and State Parks, Sequoia & Kings Canyon National Parks, or Yosemite National Park. If you're fascinated by natural landscapes, how could you miss the magnificent Lake Tahoe and Angel Island?


Of course, if you're a true tech enthusiast, nearby Silicon Valley is also worth visiting. Universities and tech companies represented by Stanford University and Googleplex are an essential part of any trip to San Francisco.


Things to Know Before Traveling


Tipping Culture


Many service industry workers in San Francisco are Asian and Latin American new immigrants. They work very hard, with tips typically around 15%～20% of the total bill.

Generally, dinner tips are higher than lunch tips, and service industry requirements for tips are higher than those in restaurants.


In addition, when paying your bill, please note that a small number of restaurants may include tips in the total.


How to Get There


There are three airports serving San Francisco and the Bay Area. Most travelers visiting San Francisco will use San Francisco International Airport (San Francisco International Airport, SFO). The other two airports are Oakland Airport in the East Bay and San Jose Airport in the South Bay. SFO has four terminals with a free 24-hour AirTrain connecting all terminals; you can take subway, airport shuttles, taxis, etc. to downtown.


Tips:

Transportation Options to Downtown:


1. Ride-sharing (Uber/Lyft): Around $40 during normal times, approximately $60-80 during peak hours


2. Taxi: $45-65 plus 15% tip; may encounter overcharging issues


3. Bart Subway: Level 3 at the International Terminal, about 30 minutes to downtown, tickets approximately $10;

Served by stopping at Civic Center, Union Plaza, Powell St, Montgomery St/Embarcadero in downtown San Francisco, among which Powell St is closest to Union Square.


4. Share Van: After exiting the international terminal Level 3, look for Door to Door Van signs to find the pickup location. Each shuttle picks up 7-8 passengers at a time, with fares to downtown around $17 (excluding tips; tips are typically 10%, so it's recommended to have small bills ready).


Local Transportation


The main transportation system in San Francisco city area is operated by Muni, with approximately 80 routes that almost cover the entire downtown area. It mainly includes light rail (Muni Metro & Light Rail), buses (Muni Bus), cable cars (Cable Car), and historic streetcars (Historic Streetcar). Some lines operate 24 hours a day, making San Francisco one of the few American West Coast cities where you can explore the entire downtown area relying solely on public transportation.


Payment methods: Pay upon boarding. Typically you can choose to pay with cash or Clipper Card (San Francisco Bay Area's universal public transit card, usable for riding BART, Muni, Caltrain, and Golden Gate Bridge toll area buses). Additionally, riders on the Muni system enjoy unlimited transfers within 90 minutes (excluding cable cars).

If paying in cash for your fare, remember to request a Fare Receipt from the driver; you can show this receipt when transferring within 90 minutes.


Light Rail Muni Metro & Light Rail


The Muni Metro & Light Rail system has 6 main routes: J, K, L, M, N, and T. Single fare is $2.75 (cash).


Bus Muni Bus


Many routes are available; popular routes typically run every 10 minutes, while less frequent routes operate approximately every 20-30 minutes. Single fare is $2.75 (cash payment). You can use apps or websites like Google Maps, Next Bus, and San Francisco Public Transport to query bus routes.


Cable Car


The historic cable cars are San Francisco's most popular transportation experience. They connect Fisherman's Wharf, Union Square, and Nob Hill. There are 3 main routes: Powell→Hyde (via Lombard Street), Powell→Mason (terminates at Fisherman's Wharf), and California Street line (via the Oakland Bay Bridge). The first two routes are more popular.


Vintage StreetCar Historic StreetCar


Vintage trains in the San Francisco area have been operating since 1962 with over 60 years of history, currently only F and E lines are available for public ridership. The streetcars have distinctive designs and varied colors. Most travelers choose the F line, mainly from Fisherman's Wharf to The Castro, passing through important attractions along the way like Ferry Building, City Hall, Union Bank Building etc.

Uber & Lyft


Hailing rides in San Francisco is relatively convenient and is a first choice for many time-pressed travelers. If choosing ride-sharing options like UberPOOL and Lyft Line can sometimes be even cheaper (Uber's ride-sharing is limited to urban areas only).


Self-driving


Going to San Francisco area, self-driving is the most convenient option. Major rental car companies include Hertz, Alamo, Avis, Enterprise, etc. You can book through their official websites or apps, or also book through platforms like Zipcar and Zuzuche, which usually have lower prices. Additionally, many people in North America recommend using Kyte platform for renting cars.

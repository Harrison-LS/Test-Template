Saipan Island

An Expert Explorer’s Guide to a Quick Driving Loop Around Saipan.

Many people have road trips around an island as an item on their bucket list. After all, you can do this on countless islands. So why suggest a self-drive loop on Saipan? Because other island destinations require more planning and consideration than Saipan which is easy when it comes to road trips. Most travelers can easily arrange everything — car rentals and how to link the attractions. A road trip on Saipan is practically the perfect road trip for beginners.

**Introducing Saipan** Saipan is located in the northern part of the Mariana islands, east of the Philippines, and north of Guam. It is the largest island in the commonwealth of the Northern Mariana islands. Saipan is only a few hours away by air from some parts of the Mainland Chine thus, it feels very close to the US mainland. Due to the location of Saipan, it is easier to visit than the US. However, entry rules frequently change and it is advisable to check the latest entry requirements before traveling. Recently, Saipan has become more recognized as a laid-back tropical destination. While it is a small island, it has two major attractions, its World War Two history and the Blue Hole, which is one of Saipan's best known diving spots that attracts divers from around the world. ![saipan blue hole] Saipan was an important part of Japan's pacific military strategy during the second World War. Today, it is possible to find many reminders of the pacific islands campaign that were fought there. The Blue Hole is situated in the northeastern part of Saipan and is a large natural limestone sinkhole that opens up into the Pacific Ocean. The Blue Hole has become a popular diving destination due to it's unique blue water and attracts divers from all over the world. **Three islands to explore around Saipan** **Saipan, Tinian and Managaha Island are the most popular islands to visit. Managaha Island is best for water activities, Tinian has beautiful island scenery, while Saipan is best for driving around the island and enjoying the scenery.**

Managaha Island is a great place for water sports! This little island is only 1.5 km in circumference. The water is super clear, and there are amazing spots for snorkeling and diving. You can explore underwater wrecks from both world wars! The islands name comes from the wreckage from the Pacific War. The main attractions on this island are water activities. You can go jet skiing, or take a banana boat ride! From Saipan, it is less than a 10-minute speedboat ride! There is generally a fee to enter the island, and then there is a 20 to 30-minute walking trail around the island. The island closes in the late afternoon, which means you should plan a half-day, or full-day excursion. If you just want to go to the island to take a walk, you can grab a regular ferry to the island from Micro Beach which is just north of the Hyatt in downtown Saipan. For snorkeling and water activities, the best option is to book a day tour online. Then, you don't have to worry about renting equipment because most places include it in the package. !

There are plenty of things to do on Tinian Island. While Saipan is a larger island than Tinian, Tinian has a larger island feel due to its extremely low population. Tinian is almost like going to a private island, it's great to slow down from a busy lifestyle. For those looking for a more luxurious resort-style hotel, Tinian also has some nice ones. They also have some WWII relics like stone pillars to explore.

The best way to get to Tinian is by a small plane. Plane rides from Saipan are very short, and because of this, you will want to check the flight schedule for your ride. If you can, get a package deal to make your trip to Tinian for a day which will also account for the ride and all the sights.

Stylish self-drive around Saipan

The biggest and most populated of the three islands is Saipan. There is no public bus service, so the main methods of travel are walking or getting a taxi. Since taxis are quite expensive and quite rare, renting a car and driving around the island is the most convenient option for most visitors. It is possible to drive around Saipan and in under two days see all the major attractions. Most major attractions are also along the coast, creating a great opportunity to drive around the island at a relaxed pace, and enjoy some WWII relics, or some of the great attractions beneath the water. We can easily divide the main driving routes on Saipan into three sections; the north section, the south section, and the middle section.

1. A few important things to know before driving

**About car rentals** The requirements to rent a car depend on the agency, but generally, it's simple. Most companies offer car rental services to drivers over 21 years old and who possess a Chinese driver’s license. Some companies may require an international driving permit, so check the policies before you book. Also, consider the route when picking a car to drive around the island.
**Choosing a car:** The west side of the island is flat while the east side has more hills and jungles. If you’re driving to the scenic northwest, a convertible (Mustang or Camaro) is a great option. For the central and southern routes, an SUV is preferred since there are more mountains, and it’s easier to handle than a low car.
**Booking:** If your English is good, you’ll find most shops near the I Love Saipan souvenir area in downtown Saipan, and it’s best to compare prices and terms. However, online booking is usually the best option since you can ask questions in Chinese and it’s easier to compare prices. Plus, you can choose the specific model of the car you want. Most popular models (including unique and fun convertibles) will not be available for walk-in rentals.

**Parking, speed limits and practical tips** Speed limits are usually 35 mph (around 56 km/h) meaning there are plenty of speed limit signs so do not speed. Local traffic police do enforce the speed limits around the island and tickets are expensive. In the case that you rent a car, fuel is at your own expense and gas prices are similar to the U.S. mainland. Rental cars will be given to you with a full tank so you need to remember to refuel before returning it. There is usually free parking all around Saipan.

Driving itinerary suggestion

**Saipan island loop is divided into 3 parts, north, south and central. You can spend one full day on the north route and then later on the other days do the south and central combined.** You can get a Saipan attraction map at the airport and at your hotel so make sure to grab one. Make sure to mark all the sights and plan the route to get a good visual of the layout of the island. Before you drive, make sure to **download Google Maps on your phone** as it can navigate you to the attractions. You can also **rent a portable wi-fi** as it is usually cheaper than buying a mobile data plan on Saipan. At Saipan Airport, you can typically reserve it beforehand on the internet and then collect it at the Wi-Fi services kiosk. North route map

**North Route** **Scenic stops: Marianas Beach Church, Bird Island, The Grotto** Out of the three routes, the North Route is the easiest to drive. The terrain is flat, the roads are easy to navigate, and signs are posted along the route. Middle Road is the main road that goes all the way to the northern tip of Saipan where most major attractions are located. During May and June, the royal poinciana trees along Middle Road in northern Saipan all bloom and the entire roadside is covered in red. If you catch the trees in bloom, be sure to take some pictures! **The Grotto** The Grotto is located on the eastern coast of Saipan, not far from Bird Island. From the outside, it resembles a dolphin with an open mouth, which is what makes the entrance of the cave appear so big. Inside is a large limestone cave where shadows are cast by the rocks and as a result, many marine animals such as colorful tropical fish, turtles, rays, dolphins, jellyfish, and sea urchins gather there. Because of this, The Grotto is a world-renowned diving site, and was even ranked one of the best cave diving spots in the world by *Scuba Diving* magazine. The Grotto is popular because of its beautiful blue water, so diving is a must! If you have a diving certification you'll be able to go much deeper, but don't let that stop you from snorkeling!

The Blue Hole goes straight down into the Pacific Ocean, making it a dangerous site for untrained swimmers and divers to enter, so it is at the discretion of an experienced team member to enter the Blue Hole.\"
Some additional Blue Hole diving tips:
1) For safety reasons, diving is usually only done in the morning when the waves are gentler.
2) To get down to the Blue Hole you will need to hop in with your instructor. If you would rather walk down, you can use the stairs. Please remember that there are 100+ stairs that you will have to climb back up after your dive, and this will take a lot of energy. Make sure you have a decent amount of food in your system.
3) Even though the dive will last less than an hour, you will likely be burned out after it is all said and done, so it is probably not a good idea to drive to the Blue Hole. You will probably want to take a little nap after your dive. The best option is to schedule the dive for the morning, book a half day tour with hotel pickup in advance, and then you can start your North Coast sightseeing by self-drive in the afternoon. The timing is completely manageable.

Mariana Seaside Chapel

**Mariana Seaside Chapel** Head north from the city center along Middle Road for around 15 - 20 minutes. On the left side of the road, you will see the Aqua hotel, followed by the Mariana hotel; the chapel is located within the grounds of the Mariana hotel. Mariana Seaside Chapel is a popular white beach chapel that captures the hearts of many couples. Walk through the hotel gates, and after passing through the lobby, you will see the chapel facing the sea. There are no fees to enter the hotel or take photos on their grounds. Relics from World War II at the site of the former headquarters of the Japanese army.

**Last Command Post Ruins (Last Command Post)**
After these two hotels, go north for another 20 minutes. There are two vertical attractions closely placed to the road, one regarding Korea, and one regarding Japan (the Last Command Post ruins).
Last Command Post has been converted into a small area park. This location marks a former site of Japanese military command during World War II. Many wartime relics including old tanks and other rusted artillery pieces still remain at the site. The park sees a lot of history and is quite small. If you are looking to grab a snack, there are snack stalls in the parking lot area.

**Bird Island**
Go back to where Middle Road meets Route 322, then go straight along Middle Road. There will be signs: right for Bird Island and left for the Blue Hole.

Bird Island is named so because of the birds that used to nest here. The water surrounding the island is painted in two shades of blue, and at high tide, the island appears to be a small, isolated green island surrounded by blue water. At low tide, the island reverts back to being part of the main island.

Located between the road and the ocean is an observation deck with a great view of Bird Island. Bring a small pair of binoculars to see the cute little animals on the island. Bird Island looks stunning during sunrise and sunset. If you are finishing a North Route self-drive trip here, waiting for the sunset is a wonderful option, and then you can drive back to the city center at a nice pace along the same road.

**Bird Island** After visiting Suicide Cliff, return to Middle Road and Route 322 intersection. From here, drive along Middle Road and follow the signs, right for Bird Island and left for Blue Hole. Bird Island was named after the birds that used to inhabit it. **The sea on both sides of the island is a different color.** Depending on the tide, Bird Island changes its appearance. At high tide, the island is completely submerged and looks like an emerald in the middle of the blue Pacific. At low tide, it connects to the main island. There is a viewing platform at the shoreline of Bird Island. It is ideal for birdwatching as the island is home to many birds. If you enjoy sunsets, this is a great place to relax at the end of your North Route self-drive trip. You will enjoy a calm drive back to the city center after that. South Route + Central Route map

**[South + Central Route]** **Classic stops**: Mount Carmel Cathedral, Ladder Beach, Obyan Beach, Lau Lau Beach, Tank Beach, Forbidden Island, Mount Tapochau, and Our Lady of Lourdes Shrine. In comparison to the North Route, the South + Central Route involves more mountain roads with worse driving conditions and more difficult to navigate routes between points of interest. The most self-driving challenge is present here. Many of the points of interests on the South and Central routes are beaches, like Ladder Beach, Obyan Beach, Lau Lau Beach, and Tank Beach. **Here are two great ways to enjoy the beaches:** 1. **Drive** to the beach, and relax under the sun; 2. **Take an ATV tour** along the coast. **Note**: If it's a single female driving person in the group, self-driving on the South + Central Route is not advisable, as the mountain roads can be quite difficult to navigate. For easier navigation and a safer trip, a guided day tour is usually the best option. This way you can still enjoy most of the attractions without the hassle of navigation.

**Mount Tapochau**
To get to Mount Tapochau, which is the highest point in Saipan, drive North on Middle Road, then turn right onto 1sa Dr. Look for a sign for Tapochau Rd, which is where you will turn right and continue your drive until you reach the end of the road.
Mount Tapochau is 474 meters tall and offers a full 360 degree view of the entire island of Saipan. You can even see the large Jesus statute from that view. The Mariana Trench, which is the deepest part of the world’s oceans, is also located nearby. Because of this, some people refer to this area as the “World's Highest Point.” However, the views from the top are incredible.

Santa Lourdes Shrine You will drive back about 15 minutes along Route 1 and take a left on Chalan Santa Lourdes. In about 10 minutes the Santa Lourdes Shrine will be on the right. Santa Lourdes Shrine is known to be the only freshwater well on the island. Local stories say it was also the only place that was not hit during World War II as if the Virgin Mary was protecting it. The shrine has a grotto that is carved in a hill, and next to it is a big tree that is lush enough to be like a natural umbrella. The water from the well is said to be good for your health and taste good, and even though it is drinkable, the locals still come to fill their containers from it. So don’t forget to take a sip if you come to visit.

Tank Beach
Return along 1sa Dr, then drive along 1sa Dr until you get to Kagman Rd and make a right turn. Continue straight on Kagman Rd. After you pass the entrance to the golf club, you will reach a T-junction at a school. Make a left for Tank Beach, or right for Forbidden Island.
Tank Beach got its name due to the fact that during World War II, U.S. tanks landed on this beach, and some of the remains from that time are still here. However, the beach is not famous for the wartime remains, but for the star-shaped sand that can be found here. Star sand is a local specialty of Saipan, and thus, the beach is also referred to as Star Sand Beach.

**Forbbiden Island** From school, at the T interection, take a right and drive straight. At the next T make a left and start going up the mountain the road is steep, so drive slow and be on the lookout for the Forbidden Island sign. There is a small flat area to park near the sign, and it should take you about a 20 minute walk to a viewpoint. You will get to see all of Forbidden Island from the viewpoint.

Forbidden Island

Although the island has a dark history, it is still a beautiful place that wildflowers bloom in late April and May, making it even prettier. There is a lookout point to see the view and you can also hike down the cliffs to snorkel. There is also a natural swimming pool at the bottom of the cliffs, but be careful! The water outside the reef is rough so stay calm and you could see a turtle. If you want to hike down the base of the cliffs, it will take you about 30 to 40 minutes. You will come across a narrow path, and that will lead you through the forest to where you want to go. There will be several fork in the trail but no worries you will end up at the cliffs. Tips:
Wear sneakers that won't slip off as the hike to Forbidden Cliff is difficult. The jungle has a lot of mosquitos, so in addition to your bug spray, if you want to swim, change into your bathing suit before you go, as there are no changing rooms at the bottom of the cliff. The rocks around Forbidden Island can be dangerous, so watch your step to avoid falling.

A peaceful stretch of Lau Lau Beach.

Lau Lau Beach
To get to Lau Lau Beach, drive back to the main road then go south for about 20 minutes. Once you reach Lau Lau Beach Rd, turn left and drive until you reach the end of the road (the coast). This road is very bumpy so make sure to drive slowly.
At Lau Lau Beach, divers of all levels are welcome, as it is one of the most recognized dive sites in Saipan. There is an abundance of beautiful marine life, including clownfish, as well as saddleback clownfish, and the underwater landscape is breathtaking with varying levels of coral and rock formations.
For those of you who want to dive here, you will need to book a tour for the next day and you will be picked up from your hotel and dropped off after. You will go in the water with an instructor, so you don't need to bring your own gear. You can also enjoy the flat sandy beach.

Inside Carmel Catholic Church.

**Mount Carmel Cathedral** Continue to the main road and go south towards the beach. After taking a left turn at Beach Road, in less than 10 minutes you will see the white Mount Carmel Cathedral on your left. The cathedral was built during the Spanish colonial period, and the white version you see currently is a post-World War II reconstruction. Being one of the largest Catholic Churches on Saipan, it still preserves its Spanish colonial character. There is a statue of the Virgin Mary with baby Jesus in front of the church, and in the garden, there are many colorful flowers. Next to the cathedral are flame trees, which make the area more beautiful when they bloom. **Tips** The main entrance is closed, but there is a side entrance. Because Sunday morning services are held at the church, it is closed to tourists during that time. For a good photo of the cathedral, it is best to take it in the midday or afternoon when the light is good on the front of the church. Mount Carmel Cathedral, Saipan, and the statue located there.

**Ladder Beach and Obyan Beach** Once done visiting Mount Carmel and before going back to the town, one can take alternate and more enjoyable ways to visit Ladder Beach and Obyan Beach: use an ATV. Then, take the Beach Road. You can take a break after visiting the cathedral. Ladder Beach and Obyan Beach are located at the southernmost part of Saipan and are usually deserted. Since there are a lot of corals and very few sandy areas at Ladder, it can be a bit tricky to walk along the shoreline. Ladder and Obyan have incredible waters, both have lots of fish and corals. Riding ATVs can be a fun way to explore the beaches, waterside roads and mountain tracks. Self-drive ATV tours are available online. If you take the southern route to the island, a shuttle will take you to the ATV starting point. **Final Remarks** **For sightseeing in Saipan, 2 days for a self-drive island tour, 1 day for Managaha and 1 day for Tinian are enough.** It is enough time to enjoy everything being explored at a slow pace which feels like a proper vacation. Skydiving is another fantastic experience you can have in Saipan, if you have some extra time.

**Saipan Skydiving** Saipan Skydiving Adventure gets customers as close as possible to the experience of free-falling from thirty thousand feet! Leaving the customer with spectacular views and memories that last a lifetime, and loads of photo opportunities! The views are of the blue ocean and various colored coastlines of beautiful Saipan beaches. They offer shuttle services to and from hotels to make this adventure as easy as possible. The best time to go skydiving is at the beginning of your trip. After you go skydiving, you will want to avoid scuba diving for 24 hours to allow your body to adjust from the elevation and pressure. There are places for snorkeling and places for diving at the same time, but you will want to check in with your guide first if you are planning on skydiving afterwards. At Lau Lau Beach you can go diving with or without a certification. You will not want to go to any other beaches after Lau Lau Beach from your skydiving location.

Saipan also has delicious food! You can take a look at the Chamorro Night Market, which has local delicacies and is approximately a 20-minute walk from the Hyatt Regency Saipan. Look for the DFS Galleria and McDonald's, which are on the way! They have local performances, and while you're there you can check out some clothing and handicraft vendors! Be sure to grab some Hineksa' Agaga (red rice), tuba, and Apiqiqi (which is the leaf wrapped food - so good!). The Night Market is on Wednesdays only, so be sure to check the schedule! Fire show at sunset BBQ dinner at Hyatt Resort.

The next stop is the Yuetai Resort Barbeque Sunset Dinner. This is the same resort where the last episode of Running Man Season 2 was filmed. Barbeque Sunset Dinner is one of Saipan’s best evening activities. You get to experience the Chamorro culture by watching the professional fire dancers and Chamorro dancers while eating Chamorro food. If the performers ask for volunteers, don’t be shy, you may be asked to go up on stage. The BBQ Sunset Dinner begins at 6:00 PM. Since they sell out quickly, you need to buy tickets online and get to the hotel resort early to make sure you get in.
The other activity you need to do in Saipan is the seafood buffet. The seafood buffets at Yuetai Resort and Qingquan Hotel are famous on the island. The seafood is of high quality and very fresh. You can have it daily at Yuetai Resort, but for Qingquan Hotel, it’s available only on Tuesdays and Fridays.  
Yuetai Resort is in the city center at Micro Beach, and it is close to shopping streets and duty-free stores. You can also walk there easily. If you are driving the Northern Loop, you can also easily access Qingquan Hotel, which is located in Northern West Saipan, along Middle Road. It is a convenient stop for lunch or dinner.

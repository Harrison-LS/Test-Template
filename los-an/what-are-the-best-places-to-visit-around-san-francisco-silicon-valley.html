What Are the Best Places to Visit Around San Francisco? Silicon Valley Tech & Culture Day Tour

Stanford University

San Francisco and its surrounding areas are home to numerous top-tier universities, while Silicon Valley (Silicon Valley), just 60 minutes by car from downtown, is synonymous with the high-tech industry. Here gather many technology companies along with world-renowned Stanford University. While enjoying the beautiful scenery, you might as well visit these "high-precision and advanced" spots, adding uniqueness and cultural atmosphere to your San Francisco trip.


Stanford University


Distance from San Francisco: 35 minutes by car


Established in 1885, Stanford University can certainly be considered one of the premier universities in America. For travelers, the Spanish-style buildings with yellow brick and red tiles are the main attraction. Walking among them, you're immediately enveloped in a rich academic atmosphere. As a top-tier university closely linked to Silicon Valley, it's also a pilgrimage site for IT professionals.


Additionally, Stanford University has over 30 restaurants where you can have lunch on campus.


Stanford Bookstore


This bookstore is more like a tourist souvenir shop. The three-story store mainly sells campus-related merchandise and clothing.

Main Quad


Located at the center of Stanford University, Main Quad is the oldest building on campus. Currently it mainly houses humanities faculty, and major electrical events and activities take place in its central plaza. The Stanford Memorial Church to the south is also a popular photo spot for students.


by Yi Xie Jun


Hoover Tower


The 86-meter tall Hoover Tower is the highest building on campus. The first floor can be visited for free, and from the 14th-floor top one can overlook the entire campus, though now only Stanford students and faculty are permitted to reach the summit.


by Yi Xie Jun


Palm Drive


The main entrance to campus, with nearby Palm Drive, and slightly farther Stanford Main Quad, Memorial Church, Hoover Tower, and the small hill in the distance make up Stanford's most classic view.


Cantor Arts Center


This modern art gallery opened to the public in 1894, featuring over 130,000 square feet of exhibition space including a sculpture garden. With 24 galleries and a collection spanning 5,000 years of artistic works.


Arizona Garden


A small botanical garden dedicated to cacti and succulent plants research. Not essential to visit as a must-see spot, but worth stopping by along the way.


Tips:

If you want to learn more about Stanford University's history, you can visit the official website (visit.stanford.edu) in advance to book a free campus tour.

Hewlett Packard Garage


In the history of Silicon Valley entrepreneurship, the garage has always been a magical place.

silicon valley

For Silicon Valley, the garage is synonymous with the birthplace of startups. Travelers visiting Silicon Valley always feel compelled to pay homage to these famous "garages." Among them, the most renowned is HP, which created the "garage culture," and its original wooden garage has become a pilgrimage site for makers around the globe.


1939, William Hewlett (William Hewlett) and David Packard (David Packard), who met at Stanford University, established HP Company in Garage 367. Therefore, this ancient and simple wooden garage with only over thirty square meters is considered the "birthplace of Silicon Valley." There is a sign outside that briefly introduces this history; today travelers cannot enter the garage interior for visits, but they can take photos outside to commemorate their trip.


Google Headquarters Googleplex


About 15 minutes by car from HP's garage, you arrive at Google's headquarters. The office campus is open to visitors; walking around outdoors, you can feel the Google culture atmosphere. Besides strolling and taking photos on the lawn near the main office area—checking in with dinosaur sculptures—you can also go to the Google Visitor Center to purchase limited edition merchandise and enjoy a cup of coffee at Cafe that features the Google logo.

Computer History Museum

About 10 minutes' drive from Google headquarters, you can reach this privately donated Computer History Museum. Founded in 1996, it is currently recognized as the largest and most significant computer museum in the world. The museum contains 20 exhibition zones, divided chronologically, showcasing developments from ancient computing to the CD era, personal computer era, modern software, and future applications, each period with rich collections and explanations.

Here, you can witness firsthand how technology develops rapidly and how it influences and transforms our lives.


Opening Hours: 10:00-17:00 (Closed on Mondays and Tuesdays; no reservation required)

Suggested Admission Fee: $17.5, discounted tickets at $13.5


Intel Museum


The Intel Museum, opened in 1983, is located on the first floor of the headquarters main building and is now one of Silicon Valley's famous destinations. Upon entering, you can pick up a museum guide at the visitor center to follow guided tours through various areas. Learn about more than six decades of Intel's history, the general chip manufacturing process, the company's groundbreaking signature products, and the stories of its two main founders, Robert Noyce and Gordon Moore. Overall it is quite "technical," making it perfect for tech enthusiasts.


Opening Hours: Monday through Friday 9:00-18:00, Saturday 10:00-17:00

Suggested Admission Fee: Free

How to Get There: Navigate to The Intel Museum and Intel Museum Store, 2200 Mission College Boulevard Santa Clara California. There is free parking for visitors in front of the building.


Tech Museum of Innovation


The Tech Museum of Innovation, established in 1990, is one of Silicon Valley's most popular museums and attracts many technology enthusiasts to visit.

The museum is primarily hands-on oriented and can absolutely be called an excellent place to exercise your brain. Here, you can experience various exhibits including the Astronaut Pilot Seat (Jet Pack Chair), hand-eye coordination game to clear online spam (Whack-A-Spam), space disorientation simulation (Mixed Up in Space), Imagination Playground, Energy Harvesting Design (Harnessing Energy), and Red-Green Color Blindness Test.


Opening hours: Tuesday through Friday, 10:00～15:00; Saturday and Sunday, 10:00～17:00; Closed on Mondays

Ticket price: $28


Apple Park Headquarters


Although the Apple Park Headquarters is not open to the public, visitors can go to the nearby visitor center (Apple Park Visitor Center) to purchase merchandise and experience Vision Pro, or visit a nearby cafe for coffee, then head upstairs to enjoy views from the terrace.


Opening hours: Visitor center, 10:00~18:00

The US travel/business visa application process can generally be divided into three stages: document preparation, online application, and interview. Regardless of which stage you're in, there is only one principle that determines whether you successfully obtain a visa: whether the applicant will return to China normally.

What's the difference between 5-year and 10-year US visas? Currently, Chinese citizens applying for business or tourism (B1/B2) to the US can apply for a 10-year multiple-entry visa, while students can apply for a 5-year multiple-entry visa.

Is every B1/B2 US visa automatically valid for 10 years upon application? Not everyone receives a 10-year validity period. Visa officers will make judgments based on each applicant's individual circumstances, but most people do get 10 years.

Does a 10-year validity period mean you can stay in the US for 10 years? No! A 10-year valid visa means you can enter the US unlimited times during those 10 years. Each time, how long you are allowed to stay is decided by immigration officers.

It's said that being single makes it easier to get rejected. Hmm... especially for single women, yes, you do need to prepare more thoroughly than others. However, it doesn't mean rejection is guaranteed! If you can convince the consular interviewer that you have no intention of immigrating, approval will come easily.

Will poor English really lead to rejection? No! Interviewers are fluent in both languages and often proactively greet you in Chinese and ask questions, so there's no need to worry too much. However, knowing some basic phrases would be more beneficial for your success.

How can you improve your visa application approval rate?

The key to a successful visa lies in convincing the embassy that you will definitely not overstay illegally and will return home. All materials prepared are essentially to prove this point; therefore, be clear about your objective and prepare all visa documentation around achieving this goal.

Several Results of US Visa Interview


1) Approved on Spot: Receive a receipt immediately and leave your passport as collateral. This indicates successful approval. After issuance, please visit the designated CITIC Bank to collect your passport.

2) On-Spot Check: During the interview, if the consular officer has questions about submitted materials, they will phone check on the spot. If the investigation goes smoothly, you can still be approved on the same day. Therefore, on the day of the interview, ensure that any unit information or locations with phone numbers provided in your materials have confirmed callers who can answer consistently with the documents to guarantee a successful investigation.

3) Additional Materials Required: In rare cases where the consular officer is dissatisfied with submitted materials but does not immediately deny the application, they will request additional materials within a specified period and reconsider whether to issue a visa. You must make every effort to submit these supplementary materials to the embassy within the required timeframe. The deadline for submitting additional materials is typically by the end of the afternoon on that day.

(4) Visa Rejection: After a rejection, do not be persistent. Ask politely, express gratitude and leave. Never lose your composure or speak harshly. You must create a good transition for your next visa application, and you can even politely tell the consular officer: "I will come again next time."


What exactly is the EVUS system?


Starting November 1, 2016, Chinese citizens holding US ten-year validity B1 or B2 visas must register with and pay for the "Electronic Visa Update System" (Electronic Visa Update System, abbreviated as EVUS) to obtain permission to enter the United States. Each registration remains valid for two years. If either the visa or passport expires, the registered information becomes invalid, and visa holders must log into EVUS again before traveling to update their personal information.


How to use EVUS?


Each EVUS registration must include the visa holder's name, date of birth, passport and other personal data, as well as work information.

The website offers both English and Chinese versions, but all 10-year visa holders must complete EVUS questions in English. Friends, relatives, travel industry professionals, or other third parties may submit the required information on behalf of travelers to EVUS. Currently, the system is still in development. After November 1, 2016, applicants will need to pay a nominal fee for system maintenance; specific fee details will be provided before full activation.

Consequently, even with a valid US 10-year multiple-entry visa, travelers can no longer rely on a one-time solution. For spontaneous travel plans, advance information registration is still necessary to avoid potential itinerary disruptions.

More common EVUS questions Q&A!


1. How long does it take to apply for EVUS registration?

If you have all the required information, it will take about 20 minutes.

2. What is the difference between EVUS and a visa?

EVUS registration is not a visa. Travelers planning to visit the United States must hold a valid B1, B2 or B1/B2 visitor visa when traveling.

As a Chinese traveler holding only a valid ten-year B1, B2, or B1/B2 visa, you must apply for registration on EVUS and possess the visa before travel. If you have registered with EVUS and arrive at the airport, this passenger will be able to enter the United States. Any other non-B1, B2, or B1/B2 visa types with a ten-year validity period do not require EVUS registration.

3. If I'm only transiting through the United States to another country, do I need to apply for EVUS registration?

Chinese citizens transiting through the United States must hold a visa. If travelers plan only to transit through the U.S. to another country, when registering on EVUS, for the question "Are you transiting to another country when going to the United States?" they must answer "Yes".

4. If my visa is still valid but on an expired passport, can I use my visa?

If your visa is still valid but on an expired passport, you should use the expired passport containing the visa along with another new valid passport to apply for EVUS registration. After completing the EVUS registration, you will use the new passport as your primary document. When traveling to the United States, you'll be required to present both your valid passport and the expired passport with your valid visa.

5. If I have planned my trip, when should I apply for EVUS registration?

You may complete your EVUS registration at any time before traveling to the United States. The U.S. Department of Homeland Security recommends that travelers apply for EVUS registration at least 72 hours before departure.

EVUS registration validity period is generally two years from the date of authorization, except in cases where it is revoked, or until six months before your passport expires, or until your U.S. visa expires.

6. What passport and visa requirements are there for applying for EVUS registration?

Your passport must be valid for at least six months beyond your planned visit to the United States, and you must have a valid 10-year B1, B2, or B1/B2 U.S. visitor visa. If your valid visa is in an expired passport, you must obtain a new passport to apply for EVUS registration. When traveling to the United States, you must present both your valid passport and your valid 10-year B1, B2, or B1/B2 U.S. visitor visa.

7. Is there a fee for applying for EVUS registration?

The U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) states that no registration fee will currently be charged. While an EVUS registration fee is expected to be implemented eventually, the specific implementation timeline has not yet been confirmed. So hurry up to complete your registration!

8. Can others submit information for updating a traveler on EVUS?

Yes. Friends, relatives, travel industry professionals, or other third parties can represent travelers by submitting required information to EVUS. Third parties may also pay related fees on behalf of the traveler. However, the traveler remains responsible for ensuring the authenticity and accuracy of all information submitted on their behalf.

9. If I have already registered, does my travel authorization guarantee entry into the United States?

No.

A successful EVUS registration demonstrates your eligibility to travel to the United States using your ten-year B1, B2, or B1/B2 visitor visa, but it does not guarantee admission into the country. When you arrive in the United States at a port of entry, you will undergo inspection by Customs and Border Protection officials, who may determine that you are inadmissible under any grounds permitted by U.S. law.

We recommend that everyone holding a ten-year U.S. B-category visa who plans to travel to the United States soon should promptly complete their EVUS registration. Once you register in the EVUS system, it's not done yet—you must pass an approval process before you're eligible to enter U.S. customs. The review can take up to 72 hours at most, so remember to monitor your registration status. As a newly launched system, EVUS has not yet established a formal fee structure, meaning registration is currently free of charge. This presents an ideal opportunity for travelers to complete their registration immediately!

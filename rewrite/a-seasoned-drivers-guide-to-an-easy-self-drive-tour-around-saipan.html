Saipan

A seasoned driver’s guide to an easy self-drive tour around Saipan

A drive around an island is a fixture on many bucket lists. With so many islands made for road trips, why recommend Saipan for a self-drive loop? For experienced drivers, the answer is simple: compared with other island destinations, renting a car and linking Saipan’s sights into one route is remarkably easy. For most travelers, it is practically the easiest kind of self-drive trip.

Getting to know Saipan

Located in the Pacific Ocean, east of the Philippine archipelago, Saipan is the largest of the Northern Mariana Islands, which are a U.S. territory. It is roughly a four-hour flight from mainland China and, while America may feel distant, Saipan feels very close. Saipan is also more easily accessible than U.S. visa requirements for Chinese visitors. Recently, Saipan became more popular in China because of the variety show Running Man which filmed in Saipan giving the destination the title of \"internet-famous island\". It’s popularity surpasses social media trends and is known for two main attractions: it's rich history from World War II and the Blue Grotto. WW2 wrecks under the sea. (Photo by Pirate King Kidd) During the mid-20th century, Saipan was a major stronghold for the Japanese. It served as a launching point for attacks on Guam. When U.S. Marines landed on the southern part of the island, the Japanese military outpost was turned into a battle ground. The island is filled with WWII remnants. At the northeastern part of Saipan, there is the Blue Grotto. It is a natural limestone cave that is one of the best diving sites in the world. Its stunning blue waters alone attract divers globally, earning it Diver magazine's world second-best cave-diving location.

Saipan’s Three Main Islands

The most exciting islands to visit include Saipan, Tinian, and Managaha (also Mañagaha or “Battleship Island”). For water sports, Managaha is the best spot, while Tinian offers beautiful and tranquil scenery. Ideal for a relaxing self-drive circuit, Saipan has breathtaking views.

Managaha Island is perfect for those who love water activities. Despite its small circumference of just 1.5 kilometers, it has the famous “glass sea” and is one of the best diving spots for wreck and plane viewing. The island is named after the Japanese warships and planes used during the Pacific War. Popular activities include snorkeling, jet skiing, and banana boating. The island is accessible in under 10 minutes via speedboat from Saipan. There is a $5 fee to enter the island, and you can walk around the island in 20 to 30 minutes. The island closes at 4 p.m., so it is recommended to visit for just one day.

To visit the island, a ferry can be boarded from Micro Beach (located north of the Hyatt hotel at the town center). Scheduled times of the day are set for the double-decker slow boat. Round-trip tickets cost $12 per adult, with children receiving a half-price discount. If you plan to snorkel or participate in any water activities, it is recommended to purchase an online day tour package which include equipment rentals, allow you to participate in multiple activities for a better value.

Small planes fly to Tinian. Photo by Pirate King Kidd

Tinian offers a classic beautiful-island experience. Though it is only about one-tenth smaller than Saipan, with a population only one-seventh of Saipan's, Tinian has a strikingly quiet atmosphere. It feels like a truly private island. It is a great choice for travelers looking for a slow coastal getaway. Visitors will encounter old stone pillars (remnants of war), and find modern luxury hotels and entertainment venues. Tinian is great for easy sightseeing and for history buffs.

Tinian is accessible via small planes from Pacific Island Aviation and Freedom Air. The flight is a mere 10 minutes and round-trip tickets are about $100. You can get your ticket at airline kiosks at the airport on the day of travel or they are available online. The best option is a Tinian day-tour package as it is the most convenient and best value, combining all transportation and sightseeing.

A Stylish Self-Drive Circuit: Saipan Island

Sea cliffs are everywhere on Saipan. Photo by Pirate King Kidd

Saipan is the largest, and most populated, of the three islands. It has no public bus system. This leaves walking (which can be quite a long journey) and driving as the only options for getting around quite a large area. Saipan is located right in the middle of the largest, and most populated islands. Taxis are costly and few to come by, so driving is the way to go. Most visitors opt to rent a vehicle to drive around the island. Most of the major attractions on Saipan can be seen in under two days. Its wartime sites and breathtaking marine areas are dispersed along the coastline, making the island a popular choice for an easy road trip. For planning purposes, the route can be split into three regions: north, south, and central.

1. Important Self-Drive Details

Hiring A Vehicle

Renting a car in Saipan is easy. Anyone 21 years and older can rent a car with a Chinese driving licence. However, selection of the vehicle can make a difference.

Roads: The western and eastern parts of Saipan have different road conditions. The west is flat and the east has jungles and mountains. You can have a good drive experience on the western and northern routes with a Mustang or Camaro convertible. But for the central and southern routes, a mountain road driving SUV is recommended because a low chassis can make driving and scraping be an unnecessary difficulty.

If you speak English, you can rent a car at many shops in town (closest to I Love Saipan souvenir shop) for approximately US$100 to US$130. It’s best to compare different shops. However, booking online is better, especially if you need assistance in Chinese. Prices can be lower and you have the option to choose a specific vehicle. Local rentals usually do not have special vehicles such as a Mustang pink.

Parking, Speed Limits, and Other Basics

Traffic drives on the right in Saipan, just like in China. The speed limit on the island is 35 mph, which is around 56 km/h. Most vehicles use miles on their speedometer. And, there are many speed limit signs. Do not exceed the speed limit; the local police are strict on this rule and have fines for it. Fuel is separate from rent and is approximately priced at US$4 per gallon. Most rental companies fill the tank before giving the car to the customers, and the customers are responsible for refilling it before returning the car. Also, there is free parking all over Saipan.
2. Planning the Island Circuit
Saipan has a self-driving itinerary that has been broken down into northern, southern, and central routes. It is recommended to spend a full day on the north route, and for the other days to combine the south and central areas together. At the airport and hotels, you can also find maps of the attractions on the island. It will help to pick one of these maps up and mark all the stops you want to make along the way so you can link them into a route
Before you go, make sure you have Google Maps downloaded because it works for all of the major attractions. It is also helpful to rent portable Wi-Fi because local SIM cards are charged in U.S. dollars, and portable Wi-Fi is usually a better option than mobile data. You can reserve the Wi-Fi online before arriving to Saipan, then pick it up at the Wi-Fi service counter at the airport.

The North Route
Key stops: Mariana Seaside Chapel, Bird Island and the Blue Grotto
Of all three driving routes, the north route is the easiest. The roads are flatter and the attractions are easier to find. There are also clearer signs for where things are. Middle Road is the road that leads to the most attractions, which are all located at the northernmost point of the island. In the months of May and June, the flame trees (which are a globally recognized species) bloom along Middle Road and paint the route with color. If you are there during that time, stop for photos.

The Blue Grotto
The Blue Grotto is located on Saipan's eastern coast, and is near Bird Island. When viewed from the outside, the structure looks like a dolphin with its mouth open, but inside is a huge limestone cavern. The rocky contours help foster a habitat for various marine organisms, such as brightly-colored tropical fish, sea turtles, sting rays, jellyfish, sea urchins, and even dolphins. This place is recognized as one of the best places in the world to dive as it was ranked second best cave diving site in the world by Diver magazine. For snorkelers, the diving experience will be great since this place attracts many visitors because of its stunning unique blue water. Divers of this cave will be able to go deeper than those who are not certified and go snorkeling.

The unique blue color of the Blue Grotto. Photo by Pirate King Kidd

Visitors should keep in mind that because the Blue Grotto is a direct link to the Pacific Ocean, the underwater currents can be quite strong. Additionally, staff at the site are highly focused on water safety. That said, be sure to book a diving experience in order to avoid the hassles of packing your own diving equipment.

What you need to know:

1) Diving is usually scheduled for the morning as the waves tend to be less strong and poses less risk. This way, you can also maximize your comfort.


2) If you want to enter the Blue Grotto, you can do so by jumping into the water with your instructor or via some stairs. However, be warned that the staircase has over 100 steps that you will need to climb again after jumping in. So be sure to bring some stamina. You should eat a small meal in order to have enough energy for the outing. \n 3) Although time underwater is generally only around an hour, diving is physically demanding. Ideally, a morning dive with hotel transfers should be arranged, followed by a rest at the hotel, and then an afternoon drive across the northern route. Note that driving after a dive can be tiring.

Mariana Seaside Chapel

From the town, drive north for about 15 to 20 minutes along Middle Road. On the left, the Aqua Resort Club and Mariana Resort will come into view. Mariana Seaside Chapel is located within Mariana Resort.

Mariana Seaside Chapel is a white chapel built beside the sea and is a favorite among couples for wedding photos. Enter through the hotel gate, go through the lobby, and you'll find the chapel by the sea. Access to the hotel and photos are free.

Second World War all korean remains at Last Command Post. Photo by mayagt422

Last Command Post

After you have gone past the two hotels, continue driving north for about 20 minutes. On the right side of the road, there are two monument-like sites that are a little too close to each other: one with a Korea connection and another with Japan, the Last Command Post.

Last Command Post is a leafy park today. Previously, it was a secret site for storing atomic bombs and was used to load atomic bombers (B-29s) that participated in the bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki during World War II. Throughout the park are tanks, cannons and other war relics that remain rusted and serve as a sobering reminder of history. The park is small and the food stalls located by the parking area serve as a convenient stop to get snacks.

Bird Island

Go back to the junction of Middle Road and Route 322 where Route 322 goes uphill. Continue on Middle Road, and take the right turn for Bird Island and left for the Blue Grotto.

Bird Island gets its name from a numerous bird population that used to inhabit the island. Nature creates two distinct colors of blue for the sea here. During high tide, Bird Island is an offshore islet and is covered by dense green vegetation. It looks like an emerald floating in the dark blue Pacific. During low tide, the island connects with Saipan. There is a lookout point that is located between the road and the sea that provides a view of the island. If you want to see the birds that live there, it’s best to bring a small pair of binoculars. Both sunrise and sunset are stunning views, it is a good choice to end the northern route with the sunset view at Bird Island before a short drive back to the town.

Bird Island

After visiting Suicide Cliff, return back to where Route 322 goes uphill, and continue on Middle Road and take the right for Bird Island and left for the Blue Grotto.

Bird Island is named after the birds that live on the island. There are two different colors of blue that the water is separated into. During high tide, the island is surrounded by water and looks like an isolated emerald in the dark-blue Pacific, but during low tide the island connects with the main island. A great place to get a scenic view of the area is from the Bird Island viewing platform. Bird Island is also a beautiful place to watch the sunrise and sunset which makes it a great place to end the northern self-drive route.

South and Central Route Map

South and Central Routes

Classic stops include: Mount Carmel Cathedral, Ladder Beach, Obyan Beach, Lau Lau Beach, Tank Beach, Forbidden Island, Mount Tapochau, and Santa Lourdes Shrine.

When it comes to the south and central routes, there is a lot more mountain driving than on the north route. Because of this, the overall conditions of the roads are more difficult to traverse and the specific points of interest are more difficult to find. There are also a variety of beaches; Ladder Beach, Obyan Beach, Lau Lau Beach, and Tank Beach. You can enjoy these beaches two ways: You can drive to the beaches and spend the day relaxing in the sun or you can take an ATV and ride along the coast.

Note: If your group has only female drivers, this route is not recommended for self-driving as the mountain roads can be demanding. For safety and an easier experience, look for a suitable day tour. You will still see a lot of scenery without the added stress of driving.

Mount Tapochau

To get to Mount Tapochau, drive north along Middle Road for about 10 minutes and take a right turn onto Isa Drive. Keep driving until you see Tapochau Road and head down that road until you reach the end, which will be the highest point of Saipan which is 473 meters above sea level.

One of the best locations to view Saipan from above is Santa Lourdes Shrine because it also gives a view of the nearby Mariana Trench, the deepest part of the ocean. It also provides a 360-degree view of the island and its large Jesus statue. The height of the view is a rare dramatic vertical scale. \n

Santa Lourdes Shrine

To get to the shrine, return back to Isa Drive and you should see Chalan Santa Lourdes to your left. Turn left there, and in less than 10 minutes the Santa Lourdes Shrine will be on your right.

The Santa Lourdes shrine has the only freshwater well on the island that is said to have healing properties. The enclave in which the well is found is said to be the only place on the island that was not affected by the violence of World War 2 because it was divinely protected by the Virgin Mary. The well is located beside a large tree on a mountainside that provides a natural canopy to the well. Many residents come to collect water from the well and it is said to be drinkable. You should also try some when you come to the shrine.

Forbidden Island

To get to the island from the school, go to the T-junction and turn right. Then go straight. Within 10 minutes you should reach another T-junction and turn left to get onto the mountain road. The road can get steep so be careful. Close to the top, there should be a sign for Forbidden Island. Just before the sign there is a flat area where you can park. From there, it is about a 20 minute walk to the lookout. When you reach the lookout, you will be able to see all of the island.

Forbidden Island is an offshore island that is attached to Saipan at low tide but during high tide it separates. A legend tells of Saipan suffering from long droughts and priests chose a man, beheaded him, and threw his head into the sea in hopes of bringing rain.

Forbidden Island. Photo by mayagt422

Despite the legend, the island is beautiful. In April and May, there are a lot of wildflowers which makes it a lot nicer. People can hike down below the cliff to where they can snorkel and enjoy the view but there is also a lookout from the top. A natural pool surrounded by reefs lies under the cliff; while the sea outside the reef can be rough there is a calm pool that Sea Turtles may even visit.

The hike to the cliffs will take about 30 to 40 minutes. At the Forbidden Island sign there is a narrow path that goes into the jungle. Forks in the path may be encountered but all of them will lead to the cliffs.

Tips:

Wear athletic shoes to avoid slipping because the trails are uneven. Jungle mosquitoes are sure to be bothersome so bring some repellent. You should also consider bringing a swimsuit because there is nowhere to change at the bottom. Be careful walking around the rocks by Forbidden Island as they can be slippery.

Lau Lau Beach

To return to Lau Lau Beach, you will first need to return to Isa Drive, then go south for about 20 minutes. Turn left onto Lau Lau Beach Road and drive to the end of the road. Take it slow as this is a rocky road.

Open water divers of all levels can enjoy Lau Lau Beach as it is one of Saipan's best known diving spots. Abundant marine life and interesting underwater terrain with varying underwater topography including arches make it an ideal diving location. Look out for clown fish and the two-bar anemone fish.

If you arrive by car, you can enjoy the sun on the flat beach. If you want to dive, you will have to book a dedicated day trip for the following day. You don’t have to worry about logistics, as these tours will take care of everything. They provide hotel pick ups, diving instruction, and diving gear.

Mount Carmel Cathedral

Drive to Isa Drive and go all the way down south until you hit the end where the beach is. Take a left and drive for less than 10 minutes, the white Mount Carmel Cathedral will be on your left.

Mount Carmel Cathedral was first constructed during the Spanish occupation of the area. The white building you see today is post WWII and is the biggest catholic church on Saipan. The church has a heavy spanish influence design and at the front is a statue of Mary holding Jesus. The church is surrounded by colorful flowers and flame trees which create a striking contrast when they are in full bloom.

Advice:

Visitors cannot use the main entrance, only the side door is open to the public. The Sunday morning session is closed to tourists. The front of the chapel has the best angle for photos during the towards the end of the day when the sunlight is on the chapel.

Saipan’s coastal waters are different hues of blue, Ladder Beach and Obyan Beach.

After visiting Mount Carmel Cathedral, drive back along Beach Road to the town, have a little break, and then go out again in the afternoon to the last remaining beaches.

Instead of driving to Ladder Beach and Obyan Beach, try taking an ATV! Located on Saipan’s southern end near the airport, Ladder Beach and Obyan Beach are quite the sight to see. Ladder Beach is full of endless coral and is sandy, and the beach is lined with a mixture of small coral fragments and shells. Obyan Beach has beautiful waters with exotic fish and coral. If you’re looking for a unique experience while driving an ATV, the ride is exhilarating. You can find ATV rentals online before your route across the southern part of Saipan, which include transfers to the ATV base and self-guided instructor-led rides. A Few Final Recommendations To experience the destination thoroughly and to avoid making it a rushed checklist, plan for one day on Managaha Island, one day on Tinian, and two days driving around Saipan. If you have more time, there is one more activity that you should consider, skydiving. Skydiving over Saipan. Photo by Pirate King Kidd With its scenic views of the beautiful outline of Managaha Island, seven colored coastlines, and expansive blue ocean, Saipan is in the center of the Pacific. From an aerial view, Saipan is one of the recognized skydiving spots in the world. Skydiving over Saipan starts with a small plane ride from Saipan airport. When booking activities, airport pickup and drop off, hotel transfers, and transport inclusions are provided. Additionally, Chinese-speaking customer service staff are available for questions.

Tips: Book skydiving first and separate it from the scuba diving activities. Both skydiving and deep diving are body pressuring and flying at a high altitude is banned within 12 - 24 hours after deep diving. At the Blue Grotto, visitors who are certified to scuba dive can do so, and those who are not certified can just do snorkeling. Snorkeling has no conflict with skydiving, the activity that needs most caution is deep diving. While at Lau Lau Beach, the diving that is conducted is also considered scuba diving no matter the certification, and the depth is only around 5 to 6 meters which does not affect the skydiving.

Special attention needs to be placed on Saipan’s food scene. Chamorro Night Market is about 20 minutes away from Fiesta Resort. To reach it, walk towards the DFS duty-free shop, go past the gas station adjacent to McDonalds, and walk for less than 5 minutes. The market features food, stalls selling clothes and crafts, and local performers. Hineksa Agaga, Apiqiqi wrapped in leaves, and Tuba are local foods and drinks that can be tried at the market. Please note that the market is only open on Thursday evenings.

Fire show at Fiesta Resort sunset barbecue dinner. Photo by Pirate King Kidd

A very good evening option is the Fiesta Resort sunset barbecue dinner. The Fiesta Resort is the hotel where the last episode of Running Brothers season 2 was filmed. One of the busiest things to do at night in Saipan is the sunset barbecue. Enjoy traditional Chamorro food while you watch fire dancers and Chamorro dancers. This is a perfect way to get an introduction to the rich culture of the island. The performers may even pull you on stage, so be brave! The show will begin at 6pm, and you must book in advance. Tickets can be bought online so make sure to get to the hotel a little bit early. A seafood buffet is another must-do experience in Saipan, and some of the best seafood buffets on the island are at the Fiesta Resort and Aqua Resort Club. They offer a large variety of extremely fresh seafood. Fiesta has a seafood buffet every day, while Aqua Resort Club only has it on Tuesdays and Fridays. Fiesta Resort is located in the center of town on Micro Beach, and it is easy to walk to the shopping streets and duty-free shops nearby. Aqua Resort Club is located on the northwestern coast of Saipan near Middle Road, so it's a good lunch or dinner option if you're driving north. Conclusion Saipan is a great choice for a self-drive holiday. It is easy to rent a car, and the island is easy to drive around.

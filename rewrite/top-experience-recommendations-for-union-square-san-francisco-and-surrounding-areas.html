Top Experience Recommendations for Union Square San Francisco and Surrounding Areas

MOIC Ice Cream Museum

Union Square in San Francisco is the third-largest shopping district in America. Most Union Square stores carry home furnishings, clothing, art, or collectible items. This is an excellent place to browse and shop; standing in the middle of Union Square with Macy's department store directly ahead, downtown financial district and the waterfront are to your left; beyond Macy's (directly facing you) lies SOMA (South of Market) and the San Francisco Museum of Modern Art. Chinatown and North Beach are right behind you, while theaters and art galleries are located to your right.


However, Union Square was once a place locals looked down upon, because shopping there wasn't cheap, transportation wasn't particularly convenient, and there were too many tourist trolley tours or designer stores. If you're more of a traveler than a shopper, you'll likely be disappointed for the most part.


In 2018, the area welcomed a new music venue August Hall, which revitalized a Victorian theater from the 1990s and brought reluctant locals back to Union Square with cool live performances featuring Johnny Marr, former guitarist of The Smiths. Dive deeper into what separates this tourist trap from others; beyond shopping at Sears Fine Food and Macy's, you'll discover much more and truly begin to appreciate its charm!

Pacific Cocktail Haven


Pacific Cocktail Haven, 580 Sutter Street, San Francisco, CA 94108, +1(415)398-0195


Pacific Cocktail Haven is a community bar beloved by San Francisco bartenders; owner Kevin Diedrich was named "Bar Star" by the San Francisco Chronicle, yet he remains dedicated to crafting interesting, approachable cocktails.


Curran Theatre


Curran Theatre, 445 Geary Street, San Francisco, CA 94102, +1(415)358-1220

The 1962 Beaux-Arts Curran Theatre, which welcomed the next major theater sensation before becoming headlines after its major restoration last year.

In a typical San Francisco style, theaters express their mission as "bold, bold change"; currently, this involves Hillary Clinton's yearning for the performances presented in her latest stage production "Soft Power".


San Francisco Art Exchange


San Francisco Art Exchange, 458 Geary Street, San Francisco, CA 94102, +1 (415) 441-8840


It's easy to miss this cool small gallery on Geary, a block south of the square, but rock fans especially should make a pilgrimage to San Francisco Art Exchange. Explore its eclectic corners, where some excellent pop culture photography appears, with subjects ranging from Johnny Cash to Robert Kennedy. SFAE began exhibiting paintings by artist Alberto Vargas as artwork (you can still find some upstairs). On an archway on the ground floor is painted "Storm's Arch," a tribute to Storm Thorgerson, famous for Pink Floyd album covers: in the gallery's early days he asked them to install an arch to help "move" his exhibition.


SFMOMA


SFMOMA, 151 3rd Street, San Francisco, CA 94103, + 1 (415) 357-4000

The San Francisco Museum of Modern Art is a quick 5-minute stroll from Market Street, entering the newly developed SOMA district. After major renovations, its exhibition spaces have recently tripled—including the Pulitzer Center for Photography, which serves as the museum's permanent photography center.


To match the city's renowned culinary scene, SFMOMA also features a Michelin-starred restaurant in Situ, where the kitchen recreates dishes from world-famous restaurants including Eleven Madison Park in New York City and Osteria Francescana in Italy.


Samovar Tea Lounge


Samovar Tea Lounge, 730 Howard Street, San Francisco, CA 94103, + 1 (415) 227-9400

Afternoon tea?

Samovar Tea Lounge is located across the street from SFMOMA, a peaceful oasis above the city center's hustle and bustle, hidden on an upper-level terrace of the Yerba Buena Gardens Art and Cultural Center.

Samovar's small-batch teas come from artisan farmers around the world. You can taste everything from soothing turmeric, ginger, and licorice herbal infusions to comprehensive tea pairings with food.

(Don't miss the mochi cake - satisfying sponge and chewy texture, available in rotating flavors including nutmeg and coconut date.)


Hitachino Beer & Wagyu


Hitachino Beer & Wagyu, 639 Post Street, San Francisco, CA 94109, +1 (415) 624-3580

The Japanese craft beer brand Hitachino Nest has chosen a location east of the plaza to open its first American restaurant. This restaurant pairs pastries and sake with a richly flavored menu - therefore, this name is definitely fitting for Hitachino Beer & Wagyu.

This is an expensive place - high-end Hitachino beef requires costly valet parking - but it's worth sitting down at the bar to try some delicious, unique beers (see the old red rice beer), or yuzu-infused beer.

The backstory here is also top-notch: Kiuchi Brewery, which produces Hitachino Nest, has been brewing sake since 1823.


Bourbon & Branch


Bourbon & Branch, 501 Jones Street, San Francisco, CA 94102, +1 (415) 346-1735

Speakeasy-style bars are everywhere these days, but Bourbon & Branch was an early pioneer of this trend.

During the earlier Prohibition era, it was known as 'JJ Russell's Cigar Shop', but in reality it was one of the city's premier drinking dens - so today it still exists in a new form.

The entrance is hidden behind an inconspicuous door, under a "Anti-Saloon League" sign, requiring a specific code to enter.

No reservation? Try your luck at another unmarked entrance around the corner, where a not-too-subtly hidden bodyguard is lurking. Inside, there's a small bar like a library; the password is "books".


Liholiho Yacht Club


Liholiho Yacht Club, 871 Sutter Street, San Francisco, CA 94109, +1 (415) 440-5446

Liholiho Yacht Club is a favorite in San Francisco's thriving 'Tendernob' district: located between the infamous Tenderloin neighborhood and wealthy Nob Hill.

The menu where California meets Hawaii is an innovative creation by chef Ravi Kapur, featuring tropical influences and fresh ingredients from the Bay Area. Although it has been around since 2015, you still have a hard time getting even a week's worth of reservations before 10pm on any given night.

The basement bar downstairs, Louie's Gen-Gen Room, serves Pineapple Negronis and butter waffles.


MR Holmes Bakehouse


MR Holmes Bakehouse, 1042 Larkin Street, San Francisco, CA 94109, +1 (415) 829-7700


You're holding a cruffin while strolling down the streets of San Francisco: yes, that's exactly right—it's a dessert that falls between croissant and muffin.

Available in limited quantities every day starting at 9am, Mr. Holmes Bakehouse—perhaps the city's trendiest bakery with its stylish white tiles and "I bake in San Francisco" pink neon sign—is all about daily cruffin flavors. Options range from guava cheesecake to lavender mousse and many more delicious varieties.


Hero Shop


Hero Shop, 982 Post Street, San Francisco, CA 94109, +1 (415) 829-3129


Who says San Francisco style means nothing but schlub in hoodies?

Hero Shop is a chic boutique store owned and curated by former Vogue staffer Emily Holt. Her selection ranges from Rosie Assoulin's statement pieces to Stevie Howell's own luxury loungewear brand, SF's very own.


To Union Square


Signs from most freeways lead into Union Square. If you're using GPS, enter 335 Powell Street, which is the address of St. Francis Hotel.


The convenient parking garages under Union Square aren't more expensive than other downtown city garages. Enter Macy's at Geary. If all 985 spaces are full, circle around Union Square and turn right until you're on Powell Street. At Bush & Stockton on Powell, turn right and you'll find the Stockton-Sutter Garage.


From North Beach or Chinatown by foot: walk south along Grant Street through Chinatown to Maiden Lane, then turn right.


SF Muni Bus lines 30 and 45 go to Union Square. At the intersection near Powell Street and Market, you can take the Powell-Mason and Powell-Hyde cable car lines, BART, as well as the historic free "F" streetcar line. All are reachable!

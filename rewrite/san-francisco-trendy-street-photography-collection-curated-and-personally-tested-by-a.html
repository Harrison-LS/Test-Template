Explore San Francisco's trendiest neighborhoods with Color Factory

Having lived in the San Francisco Bay Area for over four years, I still frequently discover fresh and fun places around the city. San Francisco seems like an endless treasure trove, with unexpected small surprises hidden in every corner.


Follow Color Factory to explore San Francisco's trendiest neighborhoods


【City Lights Bookstore】


City Lights is one of San Francisco's oldest bookstores. It's a very sentimental place, with books piled from floor to ceiling in an old house that's nearly creaking apart, and small chairs placed in every corner for people to read.

Although the storefront is small, City Lights has a wide variety of books - everything imaginable, from best-selling novels to religious studies to independently published poetry chapbooks. Just come in with an exploratory mindset.

Address: 261 Columbus Ave, San Francisco

City Lights Bookstore


City Lights Bookstore

City Lights Bookstore is located on Nob Hill in San Francisco, USA, and was founded by Lawrence Ferlinghetti in 1953.

City Lights Bookstore was once the home of Allen Ginsberg and Jack Kerouac, the headquarters of the Beat Generation, a landmark of counter-culture, still standing proudly today even amidst the impact of diverse cultures and information.

San Francisco Chinatown

San Francisco's Chinatown is the only place in western America that can be compared with New York's Chinatown, centered at the intersection of Grant Avenue and California Street. About 80,000 overseas Chinese residents live here; it's truly a city within a city, full of exotic charm. Although San Francisco's magnificent Union Square and Western financial center are located next to Chinatown, inside Chinatown you'll see signs written in Chinese characters, and everything you hear is spoken in Chinese. It's a place that feels very familiar to Chinese people.

After walking around outside in the cold weather all day, enjoying a nice soak in the hot springs after eating was truly relaxing.


The hot springs here cost $35 for two hours and include hot spring pools, dry steam room, and wet steam sauna, plus shower facilities and lemon water. Compared to luxury bathing centers back home it seems very basic, but this is considered quite upscale in the US.


There's a small passageway behind the restaurant leading to the hot spring area, still featuring a mix of traditional Japanese + industrial style decor. The hot springs here are different from Japan - they're co-ed bathing and require swimwear. There are two days each week designated for men-only or women-only where swimwear isn't required, but American culture probably wouldn't accept a group of strangers soaking naked together in a small pool.


When we first arrived the hot spring pool was empty, and the staff kindly allowed us to quickly take two photos~

Mosaic Art Staircase

Located at the intersection of Moraga Street and 16th Avenue, this stairway features 163 vibrantly colored mosaic steps as a tribute to Brazil's famous Escadaria Selarón. Ceramic artist Aileen Barr and mosaic artist Colette Crutcher collaborated with over 300 neighborhood volunteers to complete this project in two and a half years. Using more than 2,000 handcrafted tiles along with 75,000 tile fragments, mirror pieces, and colored glass, they created elegant patterns. The central vision features colorful ocean waters flowing upward into the sky, rich with flowers, plants, and animals. Stars and a giant sun are included among the designs. This beautiful result embodies community power, leaving a lasting impression of significance.

【Golden Gate Park Golden Gate Park】


Golden Gate Park is also quite a well-known attraction, but most tourists will visit the California Academy of Sciences, de Young Museum, and at most one Japanese garden.


Golden Gate Park

Golden Gate Park stretches from the Pacific Ocean coastline where waves crash along the shore all the way to downtown San Francisco, magnificent. The park spans 53 streets and is composed of over a dozen small parks each with unique charm, making it the world's largest man-made park.

There is always a chance of getting lost while exploring. The park features golf courses, tennis courts, children's playgrounds, museums, art galleries, and botanical gardens, including three popular city museums—the California Science Center, the Asian Art Museum, and the M.H. de Young Memorial Museum. It's an ideal destination for family leisure travel.


1. Dutch Windmill Holland Windmills

The tulip season (mid-March to mid-April) is especially beautiful. The windmills are at the westernmost tip of Golden Gate Park, right next to Ocean Beach. No admission fee!

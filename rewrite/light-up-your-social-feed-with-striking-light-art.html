Los Angeles

Light up your social feed with striking light art!

The Bay Lights

Every evening when the city gets lit up, artwork on the street and the waterfront glows. The installations of Illuminate SF shine while they draw people to explore different neighborhoods of San Francisco after dark.

You can see most of these art displays for free, and many are along public transport routes. Check the current displays because some are only up temporarily or they are under maintenance.

The Bay Lights

Location: San Francisco—Oakland Bay Bridge, Western Span
Artist Leo Villareal created The Bay Lights using 25,000 white LED lights. The lights formed patterns that changed all the time. The lights were first turned on in 2013 as a two year project but they kept getting turned on for many more years. The lights were turned off in 2023 because the changing patterns had caused the wires to break in the water. The light show was planned to be redone, but the bridge has not been lit up for many to see.

One of the most recognizable locations in San Francisco is the Bay Bridge. Its illuminated spans photogenic shots at sunrise and sunset. Other good shooting locations are the Embarcadero waterfront and Pier 14. The Exploratorium is also at Pier 15. Depending on the current operations, the span lighting may also change.

Day for Night

Location: Salesforce Tower, 415 Mission Street

Jim Campbell, a light artist, created “Day for Night,” located at the upper section of Salesforce Tower, and it is an iconic addition to the city’s skyline.

Approximately 11,000 LED lights are used for the installation, and it depicts low-resolution moving images of scenes captured in San Francisco. The display is visible from a distance throughout the city and Bay Area, as long as the tower is not covered by dense fog.

Tara Mechani

Location: Patricia’s Green, Hayes Valley

Illuminated sculptures are a new form of art that combines light and design. One of those sculptures is Tara Mechani, a 17-foot tall, all illuminated sculpture designed by Dana Albany. Albany uses Tara, the name of a female deity in Buddhism, and combines the sacred female image with the figure of a robot. Albany's sculpture was designed using reclaimed materials and has a glowing chandelier inside the sculpture's abdomen.

Language of the Birds

Location: Broadway and Columbus Avenue

Language of the Birds is a public light installation created by Brian Goggin and Dorka Keehn. It consists of 23 solar-powered books that are light installations. The books are designed to hang above the street, and the books are designed to give the illusion of a flying flock of birds.

Located beneath the installation, words and phrases in three different languages can be found on the ground in front of the books. The words and phrases in the books are in English, Italian, and Chinese.

Love Over Rules

Location: Annie Alley at Mission Street

American artist Hank Willis Thomas' first permanent public art piece is the illuminated text installation Love Over Rules. Thomas has ties to the San Francisco arts community in addition to being based in New York.

Bayview Rise

Location: Pier 92, at Third Street and Cargo Way

“Bayview Rise” is an illuminated mural created by artists Laura Haddad and Tom Drugan that is 187 feet tall and 70 feet wide. Its layered imagery reflects the history, character, and transformation of San Francisco’s Bayview neighborhood.

To get to the artwork from downtown, take the Muni T Third line southbound to Cargo Way and Bayview Gateway Park. The artwork is particularly captivating at night when the changing colors of the lights illuminate different sections of the mural.

On your way, or on the way back, you can stop and explore the independent galleries, restaurants, bars, design shops, and local businesses in Dogpatch.

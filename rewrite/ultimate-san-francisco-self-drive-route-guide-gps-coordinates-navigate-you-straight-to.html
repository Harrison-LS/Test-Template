How to enjoy San Francisco?


San Francisco is one of America's West Coast most beautiful cities with rich cultural atmosphere. There are many places worth visiting, mainly: Golden Gate Bridge, Nine-Bend Hill (Lombard Street), Fisherman's Wharf, Union Square, Chinatown, and Twin Peaks for panoramic views of the entire city. None take much time; a half day plus the morning of the second day is enough to complete them all.

For families with children, I recommend visiting Exploratorium museum, as it is one of America's better science and technology museums.

Downtown San Francisco isn't very large, comparable in size to a major county town in China, but city roads are complex with many steep hills. I suggest combining car driving, public transportation, and walking for touring. This city is quite small - only 4 kilometers from Fisherman's Wharf in the north to Union Square or City Hall in the south. The only attractions slightly farther away that aren't convenient by walking are Golden Gate Bridge and Twin Peaks.

South of Market Street, there are not many tourist destinations, and it's also not particularly safe, so I don't recommend going there.


Best GPS coordinates for driving to Twin Peaks


Bird's-eye view of San Francisco from Twin Peaks is absolutely recommended. But here, there are occasional thieves around; be careful your car windows get smashed. If you only search for Twin Peaks in navigation, it will just take you to the vicinity: first, you might not find a good spot to see San Francisco's full view, and second, parking is too far away to keep an eye on your car.

I recommend not parking your car too far away; try to keep it within your line of sight. So I'm recommending a navigation address that is the best viewpoint, with parking right next to it.

Navigation address: Christmas Tree Point Rd, San Francisco, CA 94131

GPS coordinates: 37°45'17.0"N 122°26'47.4"W

Google Maps coordinates: 37.754720, -122.446487

Recommended visiting time: 30 minutes.


Best navigation coordinates for self-driving to the Golden Gate Bridge


The Golden Gate Bridge is an absolute must-see stop on a self-drive trip through Western America. There are many navigation points for it; if you only use Google Maps and search "Golden Gate Bridge," it will just take you onto the bridge itself, with no place to park.

The scenery here is excellent, and parking is free. After parking, you can take photos at the spot or walk to Torpedo Wharf for more photos, or follow the Battery Trail to walk up and visit the Golden Gate Bridge. Recommended visiting time: 1 hour. Techniques for driving through Lombard Street in San Francisco


San Francisco is a hilly city, with over 40 small hills in the downtown area. Driving around San Francisco is very enjoyable, and of course, the most enjoyable experience is navigating the world's most winding street - Lombard Street.


Nine Curves Flower Street is the section with the most curves on Lombard Street, which runs east-west across San Francisco, and is a one-way street from west to east. If you want to drive through this section of Nine Curves, it's difficult to search for only Lombard Street (navigation will default to bypassing this section). So what should you do?

You need to start from any point on the west side of Nine Curves street, and navigate to one of the following points: 37°48'07.9"N 122°25'07.4"W (37.802194, -122.418722) or 1025 Lombard St # 6, San Francisco, CA.

After driving through Nine Curves street, you can park in free roadside parking and get out briefly to take photos (roadside parking is limited, so spots aren't guaranteed, and there are reports of cars being damaged or items stolen while people were taking pictures).

Alternatively, after completing the drive through Nine Curves street, you don't need to park—just turn left directly to Fisherman's Wharf. That area has abundant parking options and is notably safer.


How to park at Fisherman's Wharf?


Recommended visit duration: 2-4 hours

If you're staying at a hotel in Fisherman's Wharf, simply park your car at the hotel and then walk around to explore. Parking at this hotel costs $50 per day including tax, which is indeed quite expensive. But it's convenient for those who want to drive around the American West Coast - besides here, there are basically no parking fees elsewhere. If you're not staying at this hotel, I recommend the following parking lot: Address: 400 North Point St, San Francisco, CA (Outdoor open-air parking lot, convenient for entry and exit, and very close to various attractions and restaurants at Fisherman's Wharf.) After parking, please pay at the automatic payment machine at the exit ($25 for all day), cash or card accepted. After paying, be sure to place the receipt on your car's windshield so the parking attendant can check if you've paid. If not paid, the car will be towed away. Parking here allows you to walk around various places at Fisherman's Wharf. For exploring Fisherman's Wharf, I recommend first finding the large crab sign of Fisherman's Wharf and taking a photo to mark it. This sign is located at the intersection of Jefferson St and Taylor St, near Pier 43. The bustling area of Fisherman's Wharf spans primarily from Pier 43 to Pier 39. This area features numerous renowned seafood restaurants, night markets, shops, bars, and street performer shows. Here are some popular restaurants that locals recommend: The fan favorite at Fisherman's Wharf is the Crab House, which specializes in a variety of crab dishes. Their signature offerings include the famous "Killer Crab," sizzling crab legs, rich crab bisque, decadent crab Caesar salad, and delicate crab cakes, among others.

Walking + Bus Tour of San Francisco City Center (approximately 3-4 hours)


Walking, bus, or streetcar: First, you can slow down and carefully savor the city's charm; second, it eliminates the hassle of driving through complex areas.

Here is my recommended walking tour route:

Fisherman's Wharf as the starting point, then visit the Nine-Bend Flower Street, Washington Square with St. Paul Peter Church, Pan American Pyramid, Exploratorium Museum (requires 2-3 hours; if you plan to leave San Francisco by noon, this may not be ideal), Ferry Building, San Francisco CBD, Chinatown, Union Square, Macy's Department Store, Apple Store, streetcar station (where you can take a streetcar directly back to Fisherman's Wharf), City Hall, and finally return to Fisherman's Wharf via bus route 47. This forms a complete loop, allowing flexibility to start from any point.


Where to stay in San Francisco?

How to choose a hotel?


San Francisco hotels have the highest average prices in the world. Off-season at 1300 yuan per night, peak season at 2500 yuan per night, still for ordinary 3-4 star level accommodations. Parking is also extra expensive, 25 US dollars - 50 US dollars per night. If your budget and time are not very flexible, staying here for one night would be enough.

San Francisco's first choice for accommodation is Fisherman's Wharf, right outside you have the seaside and busy shopping district. Fisherman's Wharf and Nine Bends Flower Street are both here. When playing around, no need to drive; walking, cable cars or buses will handle it because the city is so small. This area is also the safest zone (San Francisco overall isn't chaotic, but there are often cases of thieves smashing car windows and stealing things). The disadvantage of staying at Fisherman's Wharf is that it's expensive.

Another recommended place to stay is near Union Square, but don't go south of Market Street. Hotel prices here, compared to Fisherman's Wharf, are slightly cheaper, convenient for going everywhere. Surrounded by shopping malls and stores, Chinatown is also nearby.

Then there's accommodation near San Francisco Airport. First, the prices are much lower with more choices. Getting to downtown takes about 30 minutes of driving time, parking usually doesn't cost anything, especially convenient for friends whose flights arrive in the evening. When going downtown for fun, park your car at Fisherman's Wharf and then walk or take buses around, completely no problem. There are also shopping malls, restaurants, and large supermarkets nearby.

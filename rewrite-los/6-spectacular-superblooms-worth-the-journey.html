6 spectacular superblooms worth the journey

Do you think you know what a desert looks like? Well, you haven't seen a super bloom. In a desert super bloom, with enough rain, seeds that have laid dormant for months or even years suddenly burst into life creating a beautiful display of color across the vast, dry desert. While Death Valley gets the most attention for its super blooms, it is not the only place that the deserts have this spectacular display. If you are not someone who is drawn to the basic tulips and cherry blossom displays, then these are the incredible hot-weather deserts you will want to see this spring season. Atacama Desert, Chile: Man posing for photos in purple flowers in the Atacama Desert, Chile. Image: mcodern/Tripadvisor Most of the Atacama desert is incredibly dry and barren, so much so that it looks almost like a moon-like landscape, but every five years, a few inches of rain will fall and the plants that have been long dormant will come alive drawing red and yellow añañucas and other flowers from the soil. When to go: You will want to watch the weather closely during winter months. If there is above-average rainfall during the winter months, a desert flowering event will happen during spring (September - November). Where to get the best photos: About a two hour drive from Copiapó, you can find flower filled valleys that have a stunning rugged coast, this is the Pan de Azúcar National Park. Also, Llanos de Challe National Park is another great spot. While you're there: Don't call it a day when the suns sets. In the world, the Atacama is one of the best places to view stars because of its elevation and clear skies, along with having almost no light pollution. Here at the Observatorio Etno-Astronomico Licanantay or Observatorio IOcrux, the view is spectacular with a clear view of the Milky Way Galaxy. The Atacama Desert is a must visit!

Antelope Valley Poppy Reserve, California Visitors walking through path amid orange poppies in the Antelope Valley California Poppy Reserve The Antelope Valley California Poppy Reserve.Image: traveler21132655/Tripadvisor Cinematic doesn't even begin to describe Antelope Valley. Just under 2 hours North of Los Angeles, you can experience a massive display of poppies and golden yellow flowers. Just make sure the weather is nice since the flowers will close up when it's cloudy. When to go: After a good rainfall in the fall/winter the poppies bloom in the spring. Most years, the poppies bloom from mid-February to May. Photo Opportunities: The Antelope Valley Poppy Reserve has 8 miles of walking paths through the reserve. Rest you legs and take the easy way to the hilltop flower ring. The Atacama Desert offers more than just poppies: Arthur B. Ripley Desert Woodland State Park is home to one of the last untouched California juniper forests. The next time you find yourself in Western Australia, you need to visit Fitzgerald River National Park, as it is the best flower destination in the country. Western Australia is home to many coastal reserves as well as the longest wildflower stretches, which makes it incredibly practical. It might be remote 6 hours from Perth, but the isolation of the area and flower fields versus the alternative of invasive crowds makes it worth it.

The best time to see the flowers is when the Royal hakea, which can grow 10 feet, is in bloom, so from late August until November. It might not look like much, but the Western Aus coast is distinctly beautiful, and when the flowers are in bloom, it is even more so. The East Mount Barren Trail is the best place to see it and keep an eye out for pink fairies. While you're in the area, visit Stirling Range National Park, which is less than two hours away. The hike up Bluff Knoll is challenging, but the path provides opportunities to see the highly endangered Queen of Sheba orchid.

Joshua Tree National Park, California

Close-up view of tiny yellow wildflowers

Yellow wildflowers in Joshua Tree National Park. Image: ImmerWandern/Tripadvisor

Joshua Tree National Park is famous for its enormous twisted trees, but in late winter and early spring, a new kind of beauty emerges. Colorful flowers grow in the desert and spread wild under the trees. In some fortunate seasons, the park even experiences a rare wildflower super bloom.

When to visit: Before spring even begins, this park is the perfect place to escape the winter blues. January and February bring early blooms near the southern entrance of the park, including beautiful Canterbury bells. Come back in March and April for the pineapple shaped Joshua tree flowers.

Where to take the best photos: Southern entrance of the park is best for wildflower viewing as it is lower in elevation than the rest of the park. Near the Cottonwood Visitor Center, there is a quarter mile Bajada Nature Trail that is flat and great for families. If you want better views take the 2.5 mile Mastodon Peak Loop trail.

While you are there: Be sure to spend some time in Palm Springs, which is the gateway to Joshua Tree. Go on the Palm Springs Aerial Tramway, which is the largest rotating cable car in the world and provides 360-degree views on the way up. You’ll have access to Mount San Jacinto State Park once you reach the top.

Visit the Palm Springs site to make your itinerary.

Namaqualand, South Africa

Clusters of flowers

Purple flowers in Namaqua National Park. (Image: DebbyKZN/Tripadvisor)

For most of the year, Namaqualand in Northern Cape, South Africa is a rocky, barren landscape. Thanks to some late August rainfall, it can transform into a place locals call a “sea of flowers”—a very apt description.

When to go: It’s largely a guessing game based on regional rainfall since the superbloom can occur any time between late July and early October. If you're hoping to catch the bloom, plan your visit sometime between mid-August and September.

Picture-perfect views: For some of the best views of the display, visit Skilpad Wildflower Reserve, part of Namaqua National Park, which is home to the celebrated “carpet of gold,” the hills covered in clusters of orange and yellow daisies. Goegap Nature Reserve has a wider color palette, featuring clusters of vivid purple vygies as well as a rocky area to balance out the landscape.

While you're there: This is a remote area so amenities are limited to a few communities. Hondeklip Bay and Kamieskroon (both near the reserves) are good options to stop and eat. Die Kliphuis Kombuis has been recommended by reviewers on Tripadvisor, and they rave about the owner's homemade bread.

Saguaro National Park, Arizona.

This is a close-up view looking up at a flowering cactus.

There are people riding on horses through the desert landscape.

Flowering Saguaro cactus (L); Horseback riding through Saguaro National Park (R). Image: 440ah440/ Tripadvisor (L); kayak62/ Tripadvisor (R).

Superblooms at Saguaro National Park are exceptionally rare, occurring only once every 10 to 15 years. This makes them extremely beautiful. After an unusually wet winter, some saguaros that are 40 feet tall will bloom at the tips and those creamy white flowers may emerge along their full stems.

When to go: The very end of May and the first week of June is prime time for flowering cacti. If that short window doesn't work for you then go between late February to mid April, as that is the time that the yellow brittlebush and many other wildflowers will bloom and cover the desert.

Best picture spot: Cactus Forest Drive is the first place you should go. There you will find places to pull off the road to take pictures of the blooming giant saguaros with the Rocky Rincon Mountains in the background. Be mindful of the wildlife, like the javelina, which are pig-like. If you want a walk that has more history, take the Signal Hill Trail, which passes ancient petroglyphs.

Last Minute Hotels in New Zealand: Smart Stays Without the Long Lead Time

Last Minute Hotels in New Zealand: Smart Stays Without the Long Lead Time

Last minute hotels in New Zealand are not just for travelers who failed to plan. For changing travel plans, unpredictable transport, and the travelers who prefer flexibility to have every night of the trip booked, smart last minute hotel stays can be a great option. The distinction is this: last minute bookings can be smart, but assuming planning last minute hotel stays will always be the cheap option is a bad assumption.

Last minute bookings should be treated as a smart value choice, not as a discount hunting frenzy. A slightly more expensive room that gets the traveler closer to the airport, the town center, or trailhead may actually be the value choice if the traveler considers time, transport, and parking. In New Zealand, where travel is often long, and plans are often subject to last minute changes, location and booking conditions can be more important than the \(publicly\) advertised room rate.

Last minute travelers should be decisive and selective. Flexibility creates opportunity, but it does not eliminate demand. The best last minute hotels are the ones that fit the actual trip, not the ones that seem to be the cheapest hotels on the booking list.

The case for booking hotels late

There is a widespread belief that all hotel stays need to be booked well in advance. This advice is quite sound for travelers with fixed itineraries or particular hotels in mind. It's rarely the best option. Traveling to New Zealand requires a considerable time commitment, which makes it less flexible, especially for travelers who plan to reach the country by car. It is also less flexible for travelers who plan to leave the country at their own pace, based on changing weather patterns. Travelers may reach their destination and learn that one night is enough, or that the stay may be worth an extra night. Travelers may plan to stop early, may not continue with a long drive in poor weather, or may later plan to take a different route if an activity is unavailable. Travelers who pre-book their stays may end up accommodations becoming obligations, rather than useful assets, on their journeys. Their major benefit is offering travelers flexibility on their trips. If travelers do not pre-book their stays, travelers may choose to stay in a different location of interest or accommodate travel day stays in a larger city, rather than staying in a smaller city with limited travel accommodations. Last minute accommodations can be a better option to travelers who value flexibility, rather than travelers who pre-book accommodations. Only flexibility can do wonders, while the rest requires a compromise. Travelers who compromise with room types, room flexibility and check-in hours, have many more options than travelers who are particular about a stay, which is not really a last minute booking. Last minute bookings are never cheap. 'Waiting' is the worst accommodation advice. Hotels may also increase prices when room availability is low. Booking late may yield excellent deals, but may also show that the convenient options are gone and only the higher priced rooms are available. The decision to book late should always be contingent on demand and what the alternatives are.

Demand is what really matters in the end. When a lot of people book the same night to stay in the same place/area, hotels have no reason to discount. This can happen in and around popular attractions, transportation hubs, during school holidays, on weekends, for special events, and on days when people's travels tend to coincide. Travelers should not believe the last minute deals are the way it always is.

A better principle is to book late only if the traveler has alternatives. Those alternatives may include shifting the destination, changing the type of accommodation, accepting a less desirable room, staying for a shorter time, or rescheduling the day's journey. Without alternatives, waiting is not a good strategy; it is a gamble.

Always consider the total cost rather than the advertised nightly rate. A room farther away that may appear to be cheaper might actually be more expensive due to the extra cost of traveling, such as gas, paying for parking, and/or taking public transportation and/or a cab. Also, a room with a cheaper rate may appear to be a good deal, however it may have restrictions that make the deal less valuable compared to more convenient options, such as a rooms with a flexible cancellation policy or a more convenient check in process.

Location should be the first filter

For last minute hotels in New Zealand, location should usually come before hotel brand, star rating, or promotional language. The right location is determined by the purpose for the stop. When deciding the best location for a hotel on a road trip, the most attractive hotel probably is not the best location. The purpose is to minimize the drive for the next day and, therefore, the best location is the hotel that gets you closest to your final destination. The same applies for the location of a hotel if you have an early flight. If you have an early flight, an attractive hotel with a large room that is a long distance from your final destination is not going to be the most convenient. When traveling for a short city visit, staying in the center of town may mean you don’t have to worry about finding parking. It’s also probably going to be easier to get to restaurants, tourist spots, and your appointments. When you’re going on an outdoor-centered itinerary, an ideal location may be a hotel that gets you as close as possible to your starting point for the tour, hike, or whatever activity you’ll be doing. Booking airline tickets and hotels is where many people fail. People sort by price, book a hotel and find out they have to take a long and stressful trip just to get to their hotel for the night. The best way to avoid this is deciding on the map where you want to be and looking for hotels within that area. A location that is cheaper is typically a longer drive and not worth saving money on. Last-minute plans don’t mean an extensive search. You just need a list of priorities to search by. Priority lists include dates, number of guests, and bed types. Also include when you want to check-in and a preferred location. Now identify the practices that are non-negotiable. These may include accessible accommodations, parking, kitchen accessibility, pet friendliness, 24-hour reception, or the ability to leave early. Most everything else is something to consider at best. A traveler who refuses to stay at a property without a certain feature may be eliminating a perfectly acceptable room. The priority is not to fully satisfy a traveler at the expense of the overall accommodations and experience. Check all the major booking sites and the hotel's webpage if possible. We are not stating one approach will always be better than the other. There is a lot to consider when comparing availability, room types, booking inclusions, terms for cancelling a booking, and payment methods. A booking may be quicker via a booking site, but a direct booking may be better for communicating with the hotel. What is important is understanding the terms and conditions before purchasing anything. Sometimes things may be unclear, so reach out to the hotel, especially for late bookings. Just because you have a confirmed booking does not mean that the front desk will definitely be open. Do not assume that every hotel has the same check-in procedure. A quick, confirmatory response may be all that is needed to resolve an issue, especially if you may encounter delays while traveling. Reading the guest comments provides valuable information for the traveler. Review scores are important, but not as important as existing relevance at the last minute. Determine how clean the rooms are, how noisy the room is, how easy the check-in process is, how the rooms are parked, what the temperature of the room is, how reliable the Wifi is, how accessible things are, how reliable the breakfast is, how accurate the room descriptions are, and how easy it all is. Don't let one bad or one good review consume you. Look for a pattern. More importantly, read the conditions of the booking. See if your rate is refundable, if payment is taken, if taxes and fees are accounted for in the total, and if the room can be changed. An unrefundable room can be perfectly reasonable depending on the travel schedule, as long as the value is there. It is not smart to get an unrefundable room if the travel schedule is uncertain. The same is true for room types. Terms like "compact", "interior", "shared facilities", "limited view" and many others are relative. Know what those terms mean before booking. Most of the time dinner comes as a surprise, not from the hotel, but from the traveler's own unmet expectations. There can be a hard and fast rule as to how far in advance you need to book a last minute hotel, but it largely depends on availability, how flexible you want to be, and how set in stone your plans are. Answering the question of, 'if this room goes, do I have a backup?'

can help figure if you need to book. If the room goes, a good answer would be yes. Of course, if the location, the price, the conditions, and the room fulfill a need, then it's worth booking Waiting for price drops is a gamble you can only afford to make if you’re confident you’ll still have a shot at booking your preferred choice. If you are not booking a last minute option, you may have some more time to make up your mind. Consider how flexible you can be and how much time you are willing to wait. For some travelers, having more time may mean less stops, however for others, available time may help you decide between multiple stops. Ultimately, there is no benefit to getting weary and booking it last minute. One of the better ways to go about planning a last minute trip is to have multiple good options available rather than focusing on the best. Compare your options based on their price, proximity and policies. Rather than trying to outsmart the market, it’s better to book an available good option while you still can. ### Different travellers need different last-minute strategies As a solo traveler, you can afford to be flexible and wait for the best available option. However, you still need to consider your safety and how easy it will be to get to the location once you’ve booked the room. Couples get to be more selective with their bookings. The most important consideration should be your overall comfort for the length of your stay. Sometimes convenience should dictate your booking, rather than splurging for the Instagram worthy hotel. Families and groups traveling together have the most strict booking look outs due to their number. However, there are great last minute booking deals depending on when you book. Rooms that accommodate several people, accessible rooms, and property facilities tend to be limited. Travellers that require these features should book the moment they find their dates. While less-planned travel may still be worth a try for reasonable alternatives like apartments, motels, and booking multiple rooms where needed.

Business travelers prefer the consistent options. The convenience of a good location, clear cost breakdowns, consistent internet access and tried-and-true check-in process usually beats a lower rate. Leisure travelers would probably rather take the character and charm of a guest house over the convenience of a business hotel. Those attending a morning meeting need to stay at a convenient location; others might not care as much.

Hotels are not the only options, but they are usually the easiest. If you are not planning on booking ahead of time, you really should look beyond traditional hotels. Traditional last minute booking accommodation usually ends up including motels, serviced apartments, lodges, holiday parks and hostels with private rooms. The best question is, "what type of accommodation is going to cause me the least amount of hassle for tonight?" Even though there are a lot of convenient accommodations that cater to longer stays, it does not make sense to trade up to one that has extra self-catering amenities for a lower base rate.

On the flip-side, if you're planning on staying more than a night, it probably makes sense to switch accommodations to one with more self-catering amenities and an improved base rate since those amenities offset the cost. The best last minute choice may actually be more than just a hotel, even if the search starts with “last minute hotels.” It is the accommodation that will best facilitate your trip. Common mistakes to avoid Try not to assume the best deal is the cheapest. Think about location, transport, fees, room type, and conditions. Don't reserve a non-refundable rate just because it’s a special offer. Just because it is a special offer, doesn't mean it’s a good deal. Don't ignore arrival logistics. Find out the check-in time, the parking situation, and the driving and/or other arrival instructions. Find out what the policy is if you arrive after a certain time. Do not just rely on photos. Inspection points include room conditions and reviews. Do not overlook the value of last minute availability. A “last room” message may be accurate, but don't assume it is the best room for you. If the policies are bad or the total price is too high, don't book. Urgency does not mean a bad deal. The best last minute hotels in New Zealand are often balance rather than the most luxurious or the cheapest. They are located in the right place at the right price and are flexible enough to support a traveler's priority. Travelers should reject two bad extremes: booking every night far ahead out of fear, and waiting until the final hour to get a great deal. The smarter option is right between the two. Keep your booking schedule as flexible as it can be, so long as being flexible will save you money. Book the accommodation that is quickest to sell out first. Form your later decisions based on logic and data, not feelings. Approach last-minute bookings with this mindset, and the fear you have often associated with doing so will disappear. You will learn how to travel through New Zealand and be able to adapt to the completely unknown, while still considering the elements of travel that justify an overnight stay.

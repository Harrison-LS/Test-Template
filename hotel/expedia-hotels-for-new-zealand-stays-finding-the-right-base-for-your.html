Expedia Hotels for New Zealand Stays: Finding the Right Base for Your Trip

Expedia Hotels has the ability to help users compare hotels in New Zealand based on the specifics of their trip plans (be it stopovers or road trip base locations). Usually, the “best hotel” isn’t the most central or the one with the lowest price. The location of the hotel and how much time you’ll save between locations can greatly impact your trip. Many travelers miss this point. New Zealand looks like a small country on the map and most travelers tend to choose hotel accommodations as a simple cost saving measure, selecting the cheapest hotel that’s relatively near the center of the city, and will most likely plan the rest of their accommodations in the same way. For a country that has long, winding drives, constantly changing weather, early morning activities, and frequent ferry crossings with numerous remote and dispersed natural areas, this is a poor strategy. A lower room rate can easily result in significantly longer driving time, a more rushed trip, and putting the traveler further away from the transportation and services they need. Expedia Hotels can shine in New Zealand when it’s used for planning instead of being a last minute pricing app. The goal is to predict how much easier each leg of the trip will be from a particular hotel choice. This means looking at more than just photos, star ratings, and promotions to determine how each hotel will impact the itinerary. The “best” New Zealand hotel is the one that preserves the most of your time. While centrally located accommodations may be advised in other countries, this is not the case in New Zealand. A hotel in the busiest part of town is perfect for a car-free overnight stop, but less convenient for someone renting a car, catching a flight, or leaving for the next leg of a road-trip in the early hours. The right question is not, “Is this hotel central?” It is, “Central to what I will actually do?” That question can be used when comparing listings on Expedia Hotels. Staying near a waterfront may be convenient for travelers taking the ferry, or for those who need to access a compact urban area; a stay near the airport may be preferred for early travels. Choice of accommodation on the outskirts of town may be more convenient for the traveler who wishes to access road transport and parking easily, rather than dealing with city traffic. This is not a critique of accommodation in a city center. It is a critique of the reflexive choice of such accommodations. Travel itineraries in New Zealand are stepped between travel and stay in urban areas and travel to outlying areas. The accommodation choice must match the stage of travel. A centrally located hotel may be convenient for the first stay in Auckland, Wellington, Christchurch, Queenstown, or Dunedin; staying in such accommodations may be a poor choice if the day following the stay is a travel day spent driving to a destination in the opposite direction. Use Expedia Hotels to Compare Accommodation Bases, Not Just Rooms A mistake made in booking is to search for one location and choose a hotel, then proceed to book other travel plans. This setup works for a short city break. One of the best things about a New Zealand vacation is getting to customize your trip through variations in overnight stays, but two stops that are too close together can result in a same-same day trip. In that case, consider how each overnight stop will affect the trip as a whole.

For instance, while planning a South Island road trip, travelers will often find a few lodging options priced the same and offering comfort, but one of the lodging options may be located in the middle of a larger town, another may be more rural and have less dining options, whereas the third may be located closer to the main route heading towards the next destination. There is no right or wrong choice. This depends on how late travelers arrive, whether travelers have a car, how convenient travelers want their meals to be, and how long travelers will have to drive the next day.

Expedia Hotels helps travelers with this type of lodging decision when travelers review the lodging location on a map and examine the lodging options and amenities offered, as well as guest feedback, before booking. The most important aspect is to compare the lodging options across the entire trip rather than on their own. A small savings on a night's stay is not a win if it makes the rest of the trip more difficult.

Think of each lodging stay as a logistical base with five questions:

How easy is it to get to this stay to end my travel day?

How easy is it for me to get from this stay to my next destination or activity?

Do I need to have parking, be near an airport or a ferry, or have easy access to services?

Does the surrounding area fit the kind of evening I want to have?

Does this stay minimize unnecessary trips?

These questions are way more important than if a stay has the lowest price. Hotels serve more than just basic lodging. They act as starting points and end points of the days of travel. \ A different strategy is required for booking hotels if you wish to go on a road trip to New Zealand. Road travel in New Zealand is rewarding. Many traveling visitors make the error of thinking that they must reserve a hotel for every night in the major traveling centers. This strategy results in the traveler taking a more circuitous route to their destination, and even leads travelers to make detours simply in order to visit their ‘purchased hotel.’ This strategy often results in travel visitors booking the more convenient hotel, thinking that they will take the longest travel routes. In contrast, the most effective way to book a hotel is to plan travel stops based on the most logical stopping points along your planned travel route. This may mean that you stay in a smaller town with a hotel that is easier to get to with your vehicle, a motel that is less formal out of the busier part of town, or a guest accommodation that is apartment style so that you can stay for a longer period. For some travelers, the availability of parking may be a major consideration, even more so than the location of the hotel in a central area. If hotels do not have adequate parking, travelers may be disappointed to find that a hotel really is not central as it first appeared. Also, travelers may not realize that the hotel may have parking, but it is likely to cost them extra or they will have to book it in advance. It is important to look at what the hotel says, and not what you are assuming it will be like. Self-catering facilities are probably of more importance than formal hotel amenities. A multi-stop trip values features like a kitchenette, laundry access, or room layout over a chic lobby and prime nightlife address. Expedia Hotels offers users more than just a conventional hotel, and for New Zealand travelers, a lodging option that is an apartment, motel, cabin at a holiday park, or a lodge may be more appropriate than a traditional hotel.

Prioritize Convenience on a Short City Stay

For short stays in urban centers, hotels with good walking proximity to key city attractions are ideal. Long days of travel can be tiring and a convenient location can help guests save time and maximize their sightseeing time.

A convenient location is justified only when a traveler is maximizing its use by visiting or staying in the area. Staying at a pricey accommodation and leaving early to drive to an out of town picked-up rental car is just a waste of money. Especially if you're driving to and from an airport that is a good distance away from your hotel. If you are taking a full day city tour that picks you up outside the city, or you're just making a lay over at the city, then booking accommodations is not worth the stay.

When booking a stay on Expedia Hotels, look at the amenities first, then book based on what activities you have planned. If you're staying for the night, look for hotels that put you close to what you'll be exploring the next day.

For transit nights, the most important thing is convenience for getting to and from the hotel. For recovery nights after driving, think about comfort, peace and quiet, easy access to food, and ease of use. These use cases should drive different hotel choices. Don't Let Ratings Replace Reading Guest reviews are important, but they should not replace critical thinking. A good overall rating may mean that a property fulfills its promise for that guest, but that rating may not indicate to every traveler that the location and accommodations are right for this particular trip. A hotel praised for its proximity to the party scene may be perfect for one guest and totally wrong for another guest trying to rest up for an early morning drive. A praised Remote location may be great for some travelers, but it be inconvenient for a traveler that needs to have restaurants within walking distance. When reading Expedia Hotel reviews, look for comments that relate to your priorities. For travelers with a car, pay attention to comments that relate to the access and parking. Unfortunately, for light sleepers, most cons will be lumped into the general comfort score. For families and travelers on longer stays, the room size and the availability of a kitchen and a laundry may be of more importance than simply saying that the hotel was 'great'. Recent reviews are very important so long as they describe the practical aspects. Each review, no matter how extreme, should be considered individually. Flexibility is a Must in New Zealand Itinerary Design

Most New Zealand vacations have a host of outdoor activities and scenic drives and may include some lengthy treks. New Zealand trips involve planning that is sure to change due to weather, disruptions, fatigue, or the decision to linger someplace awesome. Thus, booking terms deserve just as much attention as the photographs of the lodging.

Expedia Hotels provides alternative booking options for different cancellation, payment, and flexibility terms. Travelers must evaluate these terms for the anchor flexibility nights of their trip, and most importantly, for the cheapest option, the best flexibility may not be the best overall savings.

This advice doesn't mean travelers should always choose the most flexible option. Non-refundable rates may provide the best savings for a traveler that fits a firm set of itinerary plans. The defense is simple: decide for yourself. Don't lament restrictive terms due to an attractive price and learn the hard way that a change to travel plans, ferries, or activities will affect others you have pre-paid.

Read the policy for the room selected and pay particular attention to the cancellation terms, payment date flexibilities, charges and taxes that you see listed. Consider the various policies of the accommodation for the selected dates. Think of the given policies as the true cost of staying at the accommodations.

Why “Book the Cheapest Acceptable Room” Is Poor Advice

Budget is important, and Expedia Hotels can be helpful in finding options that you can afford. But budget travel does not mean choosing the lowest price room and ignoring the consequences. Cheap prices can come at a cost, and sometimes the first price is the last price. A cheap room might be out of the way, which will cost more in gas and possibly paid transport. Dealing with a poor layout might cause discomfort for family stays. So, Limited facilities ultimately lead to higher food costs. An ill fit in the itinerary might cause you to waste a valuable day. A better way to operate is to set a total-value threshold. Choose what you cannot live without, such as parking, a kitchen or even food within walking distance, an early morning connection to the airport, a quiet location, and a room that suits your party. After that, you should choose a property that meets your needs. In the end, the most affordable option among this narrower, realistic set is usually the better choice. Counter arguments are reasonable. Some travelers are happy to trade convenience for savings. Basically staying at the edge of the city or selecting a less amenity-heavy room might be exactly what they want. The problem arises when travelers don't calculate the impact that would have on their overall itinerary. There is no one recommended Expedia Hotels search result for New Zealand because different travelers need different bases. For example, couples on a short city break need a resturant within walking distance, and perhaps a room with a good layout. Families need space, practical bedding, and laundry, and parking along with self-catering facilities. Solo travelers need easily accessible public transport and an easy to use base with good facilities. Groups on a driving holiday will often find that an apartment or multi-room accommodations will save them money versus reserving standard rooms at a hotel. Vacation travelers who prefer to be outdoors should take care to not be fooled when it comes to booking. Appearances can be deceiving. A beautiful property that is in a great location may be an awful experience unless the property is in a location that is convenient to the activities you are planning. This can be a beautiful location that is convenient to access the start of a trail or a beautiful location that is closest to the meeting point for a tour. The same logic can be applied to someone going to a business event or conference. Just because a city name is listed, don't think you have the event location nailed just by that. A hotel that is listed in a city may still be far from the venue or how you will get to where you will be staying. Use all of the information that is available to you and see where the hotel is located. New Zealand Vacation Notes A better method is to set a disciplined travel booking process versus checking to see if there are any last minute hotel deals. Create your itinerary and divide your travel plans into purposeful stops on your way to your final destination. 1. Decide what the purpose is for each stay. Research and compare all of your hotel options before making a choice. 2. Check the hotels that are located conveniently to your activities for the next day, rather than just keeping with the hotel options in the same town. 3. Use the filters that will help you make your decision rather than selecting your hotel based on which one you like the best. 4. Check the hotels compared to others on the map and how they coincide with your travel plans. 1. Read room descriptions and take note of bed configurations and amenities. 2. See what other guests have to say, and relate those comments to your needs. 3. Make sure to check rate cancellation and payment policies to see if the bargain is really worth it. 4. Save your confirmation info and verify instructions for getting to your destination before leaving. This will take more effort than just picking the first room, but it will create a more organized trip. In New Zealand, where the best of the journey often comes with amazing drives, the accommodation should help enhance the approach to a destination, not detract from it. If you use Expedia Hotels to compare rooms, you have to do better than just choosing the cheapest room, highest rated room, or room in the most central location. You also have to consider your itinerary for the trip. To assess the best location, consider the transport you'll take to the room, how long it will take to drive there, the amenities available, and the flexibility of the booking. The best accommodation will allow you to save time and plan your trip without last minute changes. Sometimes that will be a hotel, but it can also be a motel with easy access and a quick departure. It can also be an extended stay accommodation. Expedia Hotels really shine when you can easily see the differences between them. Choose the accommodation that serves your journey, not what you imagine is the best deal or looks good in a photo. This is how accommodation fits into a better New Zealand itinerary instead of an obstacle of it. Your trips need the right balance of travel, activities, and relaxation to keep you recharged while away. New Zealand has it all.

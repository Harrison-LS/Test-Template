Kayak Hotels: A Smarter Way to Compare Stays in New Zealand

Kayak Hotels: A Better Way To Compare NZ Accommodation

Comparing prices of accommodation for New Zealand trips is usually a simple pricing comparison based on the first relatively acceptable room available. However, this approach is unsophisticated. New Zealand has long stretches of drive to get to attractive activities or destinations that can be dependent on the weather. The first (and cheapest) room may end up costing you the most in the long run.

Kayak Hotels works best for travellers who use it as a comparison system rather than a way to find the best (potentially the cheapest) deal. Value based on location, bedding, amenities, reviews, booking terms, and the final cost listed by the provider should be the focus. For New Zealand travel, that comparison should not be a luxury. It should be the focus.

Comparing value instead of price is a strong position. A deal is only a good deal if the room fits your budget, your comfort, and supports your plan, and if it accommodates your travel flexibility.

Why Headline Price is a Poor Way to Choose a New Zealand Stay

New Zealand requires a well thought out itinerary based on driving, weather, and activity timing. A room that is cheap may turn out to be far from where it is actually needed. It may lead to more driving each day, a longer trip, fewer dining options, and less flexibility with plans. These costs should be factored into the nightly price. Think about a couple of lodging options. One of them may seem more expensive, but may be the better option because it decreases some of the burdens that come with traveling, like parking hassles, by requiring fewer trips and making better use of time overall. For example, it may provide more convenient access to restaurants, transportation, and other things you’ll need to do during the day, like go out on the water, take a hike, etc. This is where Kayak Hotels has the opportunity to help travelers make a better choice. Instead of showing travelers that all choices are equally valid, they have the opportunity to present travelers with the practical elements of each choice. Location, type of lodging and amenities should all be considered. Price should only be the determining factor after all these elements have been considered. Most travel advice will say that the cheapest, well-reviewed accommodations should be booked. This advice is misleading. Booking a low cost accommodation is only valuable if the location is good, the booking arrangements are good and flexibible, etc. There are a lot of costs that can add up, like resort fees, transportation costs that could be eliminated staying at a more conveniently located accommodation, or the cost of parking. The best lodging choice should be the one that frees up the most time and gives the most flexibility. We should always ask ourselves, "What will this cost me in the end?"

How Kayak Hotels Works

When most travelers start looking for accommodations, they search for hotels and look for the hotels in the city or region they are going to. That search can limit choices. The accommodations in New Zealand can vary and can be a better fit for your trip than just a hotel.

There are many different styles of accommodations that might be better suited for your trip than just a hotel. A central location hotel might be convenient for a quick city trip. For a longer trip, a motel might be a better option because it might have a kitchenette or parking on the ground. An apartment might be convenient for staying multiple days with family or a group.

Kayak Hotels makes it easy to compare accommodations for your trip. Travelers should try to resist thinking that one style of accommodations is better than the others. There are facilities that might help you enjoy your city trip like having a hotel nearby with a kitchenette.

The point of a trip is to make a plan and pick from available accommodations rather than just thinking about location first.

What's your end-of-day destination? Do you have a rental and how families/tools friendly is your mode of transit? Even if your travel is flexible, how scheduled do your start/end times need to accommodate work/life balance? When finding the best rate, do you need your stay to be WiFi equipped? All of these questions matter. 'Lowest price' does not. Location often outweighs a small rate difference. Location is often overlooked, but New Zealand trips consist of driving, sightseeing, walking, and any number of activities. Selecting a property purely based on price can add frustrations and complications to your travel day. When considering stays on Kayak Hotels, use the map as a decision tool and not as an afterthought. Consider the relationship between the property and the important places such as the airport, the town center, the ferry terminal, the activity departure location, the trail access, the beach, the restaurant area, or the road you will drive the next morning. A property’s noted destination may not tell the complete story. The map shows the needed context. For a short stay, centrality may be even more important. Saving a small amount of money on a stay may not be worth the time consuming activities of parking, waiting for transport, or returning to a stay that is located far away after dinner. For a road trip, the best location may be one that reduces backtracking and makes the trip easier the following day. Needs are different and there is no one size fits all solution. There is a reasonable counterargument: staying farther out can deliver more space, quieter surroundings, or a more scenic setting. That is, of course, valid if travelers are okay with the time commitment and if they want the things offered at the remote stay. The mistake wasn't with the remote stay. The mistake was with the stay itself. Kayak Hotels excels at bringing the potential trade-off into view. Travelers need to compare room details, not just property pages. It is easy to book a room with a bad property, even though the property seemed to offer the best price. The lowest priced room may have poor layout, a poor view, limited or restrictive amenities, no bed that fits two people, or a host of other things that do not fit the needs of the guest. Research the room details when booking through Kayak Hotels. Is that supposed to be a double bed or a twin bedroom? Remember to look if the room has all the amenities listed. Is there really a kitchenette, air conditioning, an accessible room, private bathroom, parking, breakfast, and laundry? This is especially important for travelers using New Zealand as an active holiday destination. Wet clothing, hiking gear, travel harp, ski equipment, and/or a long driving itinerary can all change what comfortable means. A compact room may be just right for a city stopover and a complete nuisance for the extended stay. The small additional cost to have a properly sized room for the trip is totally justifiable. It is usually a smart choice.

Don't mix long amenities lists with helpful value. A property may have many services that are essentially useless for your purposes, and lacking the services most important to you. When guests depart before breakfast, they don't care about the property's breakfast. In fact, guests with a rental car probably don't care about the property's breakfast, they probably care more about the property's parking. Guests on a family trip probably don't even care about the fancy lobby, they care more about having separate spaces to sleep. Comparison is more useful when your criteria and standards are specific and personal.

Deals always have conditions

The rate and booking terms together inform the value of a booking. Since New Zealand itineraries are so flexible due to changing weather, there is a strong desire to spend more time at a destination that's enjoyable. A hard booking restriction may be ok if the time at the destination is already set, but that booking could be poor value if the time at the destination is still flexible.

Kayak Hotels operates a product that helps travelers to navigate offers and booking conditions, but the choice should not be based on this product alone. Once you know if a booking is refundable, flexible, or cancels after payment, if the booking terms and taxes are clear, and if the offer shown is a final or best offer, you can make your choice. Read the terms of the booking offer before confirming. Making the best choice should not be based on the assumption that a flexible booking is always better.

There are no universal travel standards. It is always better to deliberate the choice of a non-refundable booking if a substantial discount is offered. Plans need to be certain and the saving needs to be substantial.

Booking an item in an ordered list should not be an unexplored strategy when a better option is available in the rest of the list. The same goes for breakfast, parking, and other perks. Included breakfast can be a game-changer for travelers going on an early morning trip, but may not be helpful for travelers who plan to make use of the local cafés. Similarly, a paid parking spot may be at a reasonable price when it eliminates the burden of an inconvenient location. Reviews on Kayak Hotels are useful, but one should not blindly trust them. An overall score should never be the end answer. Travelers should focus on what will actually impact their trip the most. This includes feedback on the cleanliness of the room, responsive staff, available parking, room maintenance, working Wi-Fi, comfortable beds, and the rest of the amenities, along with the hotel’s overall check-in process and description of the hotel's location. Patterns in feedback should be used to best describe a hotel. A repeated negative feedback may be constructed around a unique situation for a single traveler, but feedback about noise, unreliable maintenance, and a lack of available parking should be concerning. On the other hand, feedback about a hotel's convenient location, helpful staff, comfortable beds, and spacious rooms is a positive testimony. Past feedback about a hotel should be considered when determining how to spend your money in the travel market, as things may have changed. This takes a few minutes, but it is far more reliable than assuming a high score is the best match.

Travelers should avoid overreaction to cosmetic issues when the fundamentals are strong. Just because a property lacks style doesn't mean it lacks practicality. It doesn't matter how aesthetically pleasing the photos are if there is consistent negative feedback because they would ultimately disrupt a stay. Find reviews with problem/solution frameworks that address your problems.

An example for Kayak Hotels for New Zealand

Leave the searching to the disciplined. Start by searching the correct dates and the correct number of guests. For the geographical location, this means doing a little more work than the average searcher by looking at your itinerary and not just the name of the broad destination. Use the map and draw a reasonable zone.

Filtering is the next step. What matters most to you? Examples of common filters are guest rating, parking, breakfast, kitchen, air-conditioning, ADA compliance, and a pool. Don't be so aggressive that you lose great results, but do not be so general that your result is useless.

Avoiding going all in on one result is important. What matters most? Next what is convenient? What is comfortable? And finally? Keep comfort last!

When it comes time to book, verify the details and booking price. Answer questions about prices, dates, guest counts, room descriptions, final amounts, conditions, and provider info. You move on to compare offers and choose the option which seems the best. The final booking phase verifies that the offer is the same as what was compared and booked as such.

The best deal every time?

Deciding which option costs the least cannot always be the deciding factor. For instance, if a traveler needs a quick and safe place to stay for the night to enable them to continue their journey, it may not be worth it to stay at an expensive location. Traveling can be very expensive, so a cheap stay is fine as long as it has the basic necessities. Spending money to upgrade would be a waste.

"Suitable" is doing a lot of work in that sentence. The lowest cost wins only after the location, room, and required amenities are met. This is a much more reasonable approach compared to choosing the lowest number, and deciding that other things will have to sacrifice from there.

Kayak Hotels encourages this type of approach in that it provides a basis of comparison. The real value, however, is when an apparently cheaper option is not the better option. In New Zealand, where the quality of the stops can impact the whole journey, this is relevant.

The More Thoughtful Conclusion

Kayak Hotels is not useful to travelers because it guarantees the cheapest stay. It is often best utilized because it gives travelers a chance to compare stays before they settle on a bad deal.

In New Zealand, the best stay is rarely the cheapest.

It is the property that has the correct combination of location, price, amenities, space, and flexible terms. Use Kayak Hotels to analyze all the factors. Consider the location to cost ratio, the amenities to the room value, and the booking conditions to the decision. Using this method, travelers learn how to not make costly trade-offs and choose accommodations that enhance the overall trip experience rather than just meeting basic trip needs.

Expedia Flight Search for New Zealand: Compare Options Before You Book

Tickets for Expedia flights to New Zealand do not need to be booked with the goal of finding the first number that is the least among all search results. For most travelers, flights to New Zealand are long haul and a flight isn’t just about the trip’s transport. Flights shape the first and last days of the trip, manage jet lag, determine if a rental car can be collected, and become a risk if connections are too tight or if tickets have poor structure.

The easiest method is the best method: use an Expedia flight search tool to explore an entire itinerary and select the option that is the best value, not the lowest fare displayed. That means reviewing departure and arrival times, total trip time, how many stops and what the stopover locations are, how many airport changes are involved, fare restrictions, baggage restrictions, and what it means to arrive in New Zealand at a given time. A fare that is considerably less may be a good deal if it doesn’t result in costs, stress, or lost vacation time that is more than the cost savings.

Why the Cheapest Expedia Flight Is Often the Wrong Choice

Price is an important consideration for a journey of New Zealand’s magnitude, but a headline price is a poor decision-making lens in isolation. Something billed as a cheaper fare may include a much longer layover, an overnight wait in an airport, an arduous self-transfer, restrictive changeability, or an arrival time that creates little time for a smooth first day.

Two almost identical Expedia flight results might be what you’re seeing.

One could be less expensive, but may have additional stops and a late night arrival. The other, more expensive, could have less overall travel time, have a more usable connection, and potentially an early enough arrival to allow for luggage pick up and car rental, check in, and a rest before the trip. The second option might actually have a better value even if it was the more expensive option. It saves time and energy which are extremely important with a New Zealand holiday as both are limited resources.

This is why the saying "always book the cheapest flight" doesn't apply. Cheapest doesn't always equate to best. A short domestic flight and a long, international flight have varying priorities. New Zealand's flight routes are worth the consideration when examining cost.

Expedia Flight Search: Search With Intention or Leave it be

The key to using Expedia to search for flights is to have a plan for your actual trip. After you have that plan, filter the results for your desired landing spots which would be Auckland, Wellington, Christchurch, Queenstown or anywhere that may be a good starting spot. A lower fare doesn't always mean a saving if you are forced to back track.

Someone planning a North Island trip would want to focus on a landing spot that gives a good amount of time to check out Auckland, Rotorua, Wellington, or surrounding areas. Starting your trip in a South Island airport may cost less. Travel time to destinations in the South Island is significantly shorter with this option. There is no best airport. The right one is the one that minimizes wasted travel time and helps you optimize your route. Consider flexible multi-stop searches vs. two-way searches. A two-way search is efficient for a trip that starts and ends in the same place. One-way flights departing from one island and arriving on another, while more expensive, may actually save money in the long run by eliminating the need for a return domestic flight, accommodation, transportation, and fuel since it may allow you to avoid a last minute return trip to your destination. It really makes more sense to search for flights surrounding your route rather than a single route to and from an airport. As with a longer connection, a slightly longer connection can turn an otherwise dreadful journey into a calmer and more reliable experience.

When evaluating Expedia flight options that have a tag of “one stop” or “two stops,” it is important to look past this tag and analyze the total flight time, the connection spacing, and connection times. What looks like a short connection actually adds stress and time pressure. Any number of outside factors like flight delays, long treks within the airport, security, time spent in immigration, and even connection time can ruin an otherwise efficient connection.

The other side of the coin here is an extended layover. An extended layover means that you could potentially lose a day by spending it in a hotel instead of on the road at your travel destination. It may seem like, because of the length of the layover, it’s actually desirable, but you need to look at the total time you lose due to the effects of travel.

In order to make the best of a flight booking comparison on Expedia, there are three questions that you should look to answer in order to justify the booking. First, is the connection length viable (from a time and stress perspective)? Second, is the length of the connection sufficient for normal disruptions? Lastly, is the total estimated time of the connection reasonable? The best booking will come down to the traveler’s preferences, but evaluating these questions is a must for any booking.

Arrival Time Changes Everything

Arrival time is an important travel detail that is overlooked during booking.

New Zealand's travel rewards slow, deliberate vacation-planning. Forcing an ambitious itinerary after landing is a mistake. Plans ought to slow down once you land and are most rewarded when they accommodate flexibility. A slow plan has a lot of benefits, including the transfer to your hotel, car rental, getting something to eat, and minimizing the risks of getting into an accident while driving after a long, exhausting trip. If you don’t have to book a flight that arrives overnight, late night flights are a bad idea, and driving after a no-sleeper of a flight is just as bad. An arrival that dictates you have to spend the night in an airport hotel is also a bad idea. Although, a bad arrival isn’t automatic, it all depends on what you want to do during your first night. You can book your first hotel and plan the start of your trip before you book your flight, as long as it’s with Expedia. If you book a flight that lands after nightfall, book a first hotel that’s not too far away, as driving during the night will not be an enjoyable experience after you’ve already had a tiring trip. If your flight lands in the morning, book a hotel that allows for a relaxed first day. New Zealand trips are memorable because they are flexible enough to accommodate changes in weather and slower, with lots of impromptu breaks. These types of trips are the best way to start a vacation NEVER with exhaustion. When dealing with low flight prices, read the fare conditions carefully. Not all fares are created equal and international flights may have to be changed for a variety of reasons, including resulting changes to a work trip, a personal trip, connectivity, illness, or revised routes. When booking, make sure to review fare rules and terms outlined in the checkout process. Note if baggage, changes, cancellations, and segments are included and if they differ between segments. Buyers mistakenly think a ticket is a ticket. A lower price fare may come with restrictions, while a higher fare may offer more freedom and flexibility. The choice may even come down to how confident the buyer is with the itinerary and how much risk the buyer is willing to take. Someone who has firm travel dates and a set itinerary may choose the restrictive fare, whereas someone traveling on a longer profile, family, or group itinerary, with onward travel or connecting flights, may find a more flexible option more valuable. Flexibility in fares does not mean flexible fares are always better. Restrictive fares save a lot of money, but they also mean a buyer has to understand the baggage and cancellation rules. Expedia has a flight option which allows a traveler to compare options, and travelers have to compare rules, not just fares. Sometimes an Expedia flight search may present an option which combines multiple separate flights, or which includes some self-transfer leg. These options can work, but have more concerns compared to a typical direct flight itinerary. With a single connected booking, the multiple flight sections are generally presented as a single continuous journey making the itinerary easier to understand and manage. Your separate flight booking allows for some flexibility at the check-in and bag drop counter, which saves you time and money. However, if a delay on the first flight means you miss the connection, you'll have to book a new flight and a hotel, which will cost you a lot more than the time you saved on the counters. In these cases, separate tickets may be a better option for more travel-savvy folks who have long layovers on their flights and no rush to get to their New Zealand holiday destination. That said, flight connections cannot be trusted, so don’t take the risk with a short trip to New Zealand because a cancelled flight comes with troubles such as missed connections on your rental car, accommodations, booked tours, and your domestic travel bookings. If you are not sure about the terms of a certain flight deal, don’t just assume it is better to book that deal just because it is cheaper. I strongly recommend the use of flight filters to eliminate options, but not to totally eliminate your decision-making process. For most searches, filtering the number of stopovers to one will reduce the results by a substantial amount, but it may also eliminate better options such as a two-connection flight that arrives on time and is cheaper. It is better to filter options much more conservatively as filtering by time of arrival could give you a schedule with bad connections. Pricing is also a sticky filter that should be used carefully, as it could place a cheap, long flight itinerary at the top. It is much better to filter progressively. First, eliminate options that are clearly unrealistic by removing long itineraries, departure windows that don't work, etc. Once those are removed, compare plan candidates one by one, and then dive into the details: the length of layovers, order of airports, time of arrival, baggage allowance, and fare terms. It may be faster to click the first cheap flight, but doing this actually turns an Expedia flight search from a pricing option to a flight planning option.

Consider All Costs Of Getting to New Zealand

To understand the true travel budget, a flight ticket cost is just one of the factors that needs to be accounted for. Inconvenient travel schedules will bring added costs. For example, flight tickets that have long layovers or a connecting flight that is not near the intended city will mean added costs of accommodations and rental cars. The actual travel budget will go up even more if the flight is a lot longer than a day.

Baggage costs can change the overall cost of the ticket as well. Any passenger should look into the baggage policy as the ticket cost could be much more, especially for longer trips. seat selection costs should be looked into as well as other services that are optional, but could be necessary depending on the trip.

It's generally not recommended to buy every extra offered for a flight, but it is advised to compare the breakdown of added costs to each offered flight because the best option offered by Expedia is the flight with the least overall travel time, cost, and added inconvenience.

Book With A Disruption Travel Plan

Long distance travel means unpredictability. Booking a flight means you accept the variables that you cannot plan for. Itineraries can be flexible… up to a point. Every itinerary has risks, but designing an itinerary that is overly sensitive to disturbances promotes unnecessary stress. Instead, add some leeway to your booking to account for unexpected delays. Also, don’t book international flights that lead to tight domestic connections.

Make last minute confirmation calls to verify the flights and take the time to review the information posted on the airline and Expedia websites. Check and make sure you know the passport and visa requirements. Take out travel insurance if your itinerary is complicated and you want to protect yourself from last minute flight cancellations, but realize that travel insurance is no substitute for reasonable itinerary design.

Final Verdict: Choose the Expedia Flight That Protects Your Trip

An Expedia search pulls a lot of fares, allowing you to design the 'best' itinerary, but you probably won't win any awards for the lowest fare. A long international flight deserves a reasonable itinerary.

Taking all these factors into consideration, choose your flight carefully and be aware of your connections. Be willing to pay extra for a reasonable flight that gets you to New Zeland quicker. Get the deal when cost really does mean quality. Don't just book the first option that seems the most affordable.

The best way to explain this is by example. Let’s say you found an Expedia flight and that flight starts off you best New Zealand trip possible, rather than the worst option, because you did your research and compared your options before booking. Rather than settling right out of the gate for the first deal you see, the better option is to use the entire trip as the totality of your research and your starting point.

Taste Test—The Ultimate Guide to Michelin Restaurants in the San Francisco Bay Area!

This post will be very very very very long,

and it's all about food,

and it's all about Michelin restaurants,

and these Michelin restaurants are all in the San Francisco Bay Area of America.


Eat? Or not eat?


I very strongly believe "you need to experience eating, drinking, playing and having fun yourself to know whether it's truly good or bad", so when hearing people around me who haven't personally been to a Michelin restaurant but keep saying this about how Michelin is bad and that about how it's good, a huge doubt arose in my heart. So, very decisively! I wanted to go try for myself, whether Michelin is worth eating at!


American Restaurants


The first Michelin restaurant from which you walk out leaning on the wall: Solbar


Restaurant Type: New American

Michelin Stars: One Star🌟 (de-starred in 2017, no longer a Michelin restaurant now)

Dining Time: 2015.9.19

Restaurant Address: Solage, 755 Silverado Trail N, Calistoga, CA 94515


In a strict sense, Solbar was the first Michelin restaurant of FF, a small star.

Besides, it was significant - we went on FF's birthday. (flippant toss~) For readers who have seen FF's Calistoga travelogue, this restaurant shouldn't be unfamiliar,

This time I'm bringing it up separately just to enrich the [food diary] content (so stubbornly arbitrary as I am!

Calistoga isn't a big city, just a small town, famous for its hot springs. People come here to relax and vacation.

Solbar is the restaurant section of Solage hotel. Solage's overall style is American modern, rated by stars - I'd say five-star (pure speculation based solely on FF's intuition).

Most visitors are Americans; there are very few Chinese or Asians in sight.

Solbar was decided upon the day before arriving at Calistoga. Didn't expect there would be a Michelin restaurant here, slightly surprised but immediately chose to try it.


📢Overall evaluation:

Contemporary American style decor, abundant natural light, expansive views;

Reasonable spacing between adjacent seats, not crowded; overall environment is excellent.

Food tastes great, fresh ingredients selected, appropriate cooking methods used.

Adequate parking available, no worries about finding a spot.


📢Service evaluation:

Excellent service! Extremely excellent!

You can request special needs when making reservations - whether you're vegetarian, have food allergies, don't eat spicy foods, etc., all can be clearly communicated.

Micelin restaurants truly deliver undeniable excellence in service, fulfilling every request.

The restaurant's waiter service is excellent!

After sitting down while waiting for the menu, different waiters will serve you. Once the menu arrives, explanations for each dish are extremely clear, and they will ask if anything is inappropriate or needs modification, etc. You can explain any modifications needed based on your own preferences to the waiter, or express absolute satisfaction and have the dishes served directly.

Subsequent serving service has dedicated staff explaining ingredients and cooking methods for each dish.


📢 Environment Evaluation:

Furniture selection is very meticulous, all designer-style furniture chosen, and the arrangement after careful design displays elegance prominently.

The color scheme leans cool, yet appears refreshingly comfortable during hot seasons.

Outside the courtyard there are fire pits, supporting parties, group gatherings, etc.


📢 Price Evaluation:

Reasonably priced, falling within normal range for a one-star Michelin restaurant.


📢 Surrounding Area Evaluation:

Near Calista Springs town, famous hot springs. Solbar is located near where hotels are concentrated in Calista Springs, about 15 minutes driving distance. The area around is slightly open with no residences and scattered hotels, this is the only one. So you must drive, walking at night may not be safe friends!


Solbar indoor seating


After sitting down, as per tradition the waiter served a normal menu, since it's Michelin, of course I wanted to try the tasting menu.

After expressing our interest to the waiter, he went to ask the chef about today's menu and printed it for us.


Before starting the meal:

Specially ordered lemon juice - FF's habit when eating Western food, if not drinking alcohol, must order lemon juice, because FF is sensitive to oil, lemon juice helps cut the grease!

There will be small muffin bread before the meal (unlimited supply, the waiter keeps asking multiple times if you need more). The texture is slightly strange, with a bit of spicy feeling inside. Overall it's still sweet~ 😂 Don't have weird thoughts


The first course:

Yellowtail Crudo - Raw yellowtail fish slices

23 olive oil paired with Italian herbs, organic cherry tomatoes, steamed eggplant and local peppers.

The taste: Of course my favorite raw fish was finished in two bites! American-style raw fish is very thick, with solid ingredients and fresh texture. The vegetables have a sweet flavor - excellent combination!


The second course:

Heirloom melon and duck prosciutto

Very thinly sliced duck meat paired with honeydew melon slices that have a pure, naturally sweet flavor

The taste: A nut sauce made from crushed pistachios complements the melon's sweetness and duck's smoky flavor, creating a rich texture that isn't greasy - delicious +1


The third course:

Alaskan halibut - Large Alaska flatfish

Steamed Alaskan halibut with lemon, paired with cherry tomatoes, dried olives, and a broth made from orange peels. Served alongside red finger potatoes as the main side dish.

The texture is soft and slightly sweet - an excellent pairing with the fish!

Taste: Alaska sole has a slightly firmer meat texture; however, the sauce preserves its natural freshness. It was steamed with lemon, so there is absolutely no fishy odor.


Fourth dish:

Mary's Chicken Smoked Roasted Chicken

Roasted chicken cooked in a flat iron skillet, served with black truffle, sweet corn kernels, thyme potato mousseline, and small red pepper sauce.

Taste: Smoked roasted chicken—no joke! Two solid pieces of meat for sure! I felt the urge to leave with my hands on the wall halfway through... But the meat was tender and juicy; the texture when you bite in doesn't feel like chicken at all. The chicken skin is very crispy and not greasy!!! (FF's least favorite thing ever is greasiness, so this deserves praise) The side dishes matched perfectly with the tenderness of the chicken; salt level just right.


Fifth dish:

Flatiron Steak Iron-Grilled Steak

Snake River farm-raised beef, boletus edulis green vegetables, paired with Bordeaux wine-infused brown sauce.

Taste: Setting aside the feeling of being about to burst, this steak's texture is truly excellent! The server kept raving that it was his favorite and recommended it constantly. The beef's flavor paired beautifully with the sauce's richness—extremely authentic. The meat was deliciously tender and juicy; the cooking technique perfectly preserved its natural tenderness, freshness, and delicate quality.

After meal dessert: Grilled pineapple with durian ice cream Grilled pineapple served with durian ice cream, cinnamon cake, sprinkled with crushed nuts around the edges, and drizzled with jackfruit nectar sauce. Taste: When the dessert arrived, I was already shocked. I didn't expect desserts to be this substantial! There was actually a small cake included!! While grilled pineapple isn't my usual favorite since every time there's an odd burnt flavor mixed in, this one did well. Besides the grilled aroma, there wasn't that burnt taste. The cinnamon cake was abandoned because I was already too full 😂 Solbar at night view, typical American modern style The Solage sign photographed along the road when driving out is the entrance to the area near the highway, so seeing all this darkness around really shows it sits in a very "secluded" location! If you come here, definitely drive. Don't dream about walking back after dinner and coming back that way, thank you!


📢Price Review:

A normal Michelin one-star price, $100 per person (before tax and tip)

Service is good; even when busy, you won't feel neglected.

Dishes are served individually - the favorite service style (some Michelin restaurants serve everything except dessert all at once......)


A one-star gem in scenic surroundings: Auberge Du Soleil


Type: New American

Micelin star rating: One Star 🌟

Dining time: 2016.12.

Restaurant address: 180 Rutherford Hill Rd, Rutherford, CA 94573


Auberge Du Soleil is tucked away deep in Napa, inside a hotel as an attached restaurant. Don't underestimate attached restaurants - they can be extremely upscale. First, the jungle scenery along the way wins hearts immediately; turning into what seems like a very private space leads to the front of this hotel.

A quite comfortable resort destination. If you have the opportunity to stay overnight, you can experience the beauty of life.


The entire restaurant appears in an attic style; the second floor is the dining area, while most of the first floor serves as a relaxation zone prepared for guests staying at the hotel. Standing on the second floor admiring the view, one cannot help but praise the beauty of Napa.


Kona Kampachi Crudo, Sesame Crème Fraîche, Cucumber, Radish, Dashi

Tuna sashimi from Kohala Island topped with milky white cream sauce, garnished with thin translucent radishes, complemented by small cucumbers and bean sprouts to add color. The appetizer portions are on the lighter side, but the flavors are excellent; since everything ordered is seafood, the ingredients are particularly fine.


Kona Kampachi Crudo, Sesame Crème Fraîche, Cucumber, Radish, Dashi


Day Boat Scallops, White Corn, Romano Beans, Crispy Onions, Pea Coulis

The seafood battle continues to the main course; a scallop dish that looks so appetizing is truly deserving of being my personal favorite.

White rice, Romano beans, crispy onions and crisp vegetables as accompaniments, highlighting spring in winter. Delicate flavors without being overpowering, sauce rich but perfectly balanced, complementing the freshness of seafood. Perfect for lunch—filling yet guilt-free.


Day Boat Scallops, White Corn, Romano Beans, Crispy Onions, Pea Coulis


Cabernet Braised Short Rib, Heirloom Tomatoes, Watercress, Bordelaise Vinaigrette

How can meat-lovers skip steak? Heirloom tomatoes and watercress with Bordelaise red wine vinegar dressing to balance the beef's richness. Small steaks roasted to melt-in-your-mouth tenderness—soft, tender, rich in texture.


Cabernet Braised Short Rib, Heirloom Tomatoes, Watercress, Bordelaise Vinaigrette


Heirloom Tomato Risotto, Wild Shrimp, Bacon, Tarragon, Parmigiano Reggiano, Yuzu Emulsion

If you enjoy Italian-style rice dishes, don't miss this one. Generous portions—absolutely filling for one person.

The reason I enjoy seasoned rice is because of the ingredients inside; the seafood portion is perfectly balanced, and almost every bite delivers a satisfying flavor.


Heirloom Tomato Risotto, Wild Shrimp, Bacon, Tarragon, Parmigiano Reggiano, Yuzu Emulsion


Coastal Huckleberries, Brown Butter Pound Cake, Douglas Fir Ice Cream

Dessert after the meal is a standard for evaluating a restaurant's overall quality 😂 The first one features brown butter pound cake as its main element, with Douglas fir ice cream as the second ingredient. A sauce made from huckleberries (dark berries) is drizzled on the side as decoration, complemented by whipped cream and flower petals in corresponding colors to enhance it, giving this dessert an artistic presentation.


Coastal Huckleberries, Brown Butter Pound Cake, Douglas Fir Ice Cream


Candied Pineapple Quince, Fromage Blanc Mousse, Granny Smith Apple Sorbet, Almond Crumble

The second dessert has a more delicate and refreshing character compared to the first one.

Micelin family's classic ice cream shape, pointed at both ends with a round middle in between. Using sweet small pineapple balls and fromage blanc mousse as base, almond crumbs mixed with ice cream is the favorite way to eat this dessert.


Candied Pineapple Quince, Fromage Blanc Mousse, Granny Smith Apple Sorbet, Almond Crumble


Classic Apple Tart, Calvados Caramel, Vanilla bean Ice Cream

Classic apple tart plus classic pairing vanilla ice cream - at a glance it's an average dessert. The shape requires some minor effort to achieve. The caramel drizzled on makes this dessert look shimmering, but not too cloyingly sweet - the taste control is decent.


Classic Apple Tart, Calvados Caramel, Vanilla bean Ice Cream


Overall Review:

Napa has many restaurants, leaning towards Italian and French cuisine. Plus the beautiful scenery and fine wine here make this one-star restaurant not stand out as much.

However, the environment with mountains as a backdrop is something other restaurants cannot match. Just like Michelin restaurant ratings don't only look at how good the food is, but also value the dining environment. Looking at this hotel's name itself when translated to English is "The Inn in the Sunshine", thus revealing the importance of location and scenery. Speaking purely about the food itself, in the Napa area, it's on par with other restaurants, of course compared to the more fancy restaurants in Napa.

Bay Area's most expensive three-star establishment, even waiters are spectacular Saison


Cuisine: New American

Michelin Stars: 3 ⭐⭐⭐

Dining Date: September 2017.

Restaurant Address: 178 Townsend St, San Francisco, CA 94107


Before arriving, this was rumored to be the Bay Area's most expensive three-star Michelin restaurant;

Before arriving, this place was said to have a kitchen construction cost that could match another entire storefront;

Before coming here, this place was rumored to be the restaurant with the best wine collection...

Now FF decided to come experience it personally and see what kind of three-star establishment it really is!


Saison's elevated location is still in a bustling urban area. Since I left early enough, being close to my home, yet even so we arrived a few minutes late. Parking spots around here are incredibly difficult to find!!! So friends planning to go should allow extra time for parking. But of course, at the grand entrance there's also Valet parking available.


The entire space appears to have an open kitchen taking up the largest area, giving a sense of watching a fashion show while eating.


Saison is mysterious. After being formally seated, the waiter came to ask what we disliked or had allergies to. We said nothing, that it was fine as normal. He then went directly to prepare the dishes, creating a feeling of completely not knowing what would come next, at the mercy of others 😂

Let me mention this particular waiter who served us. This isn't official praise from them, but he truly gave me a sense of a "waiter" as if from fiction! Downton Abbey style. This waiter has European features and performs his duties with meticulous attention to detail, completely embodying the feel of an aristocratic family's steward.

Ended up not knowing what to say to him, feeling this mysterious awkwardness, plus he's just ridiculously tall... Standing in front of the table talking to us creates an invisible height pressure... Bro, I'm here just to eat a meal, and besides, I'm paying for it...

What is Edomae Sushi?

The name "Edomae sushi" has many origin stories; here are two explanations. The first: Edomae sushi is believed to be named because it uses fish caught from the sea before Edo (now Tokyo) (Tokyo Bay), but actually "Edomae" originally referred to a way of slicing eel. In Kansai, eels were sliced by cutting from the belly; in Edo, they were sliced from the back. The different cutting methods led to the name Edomae sushi. This means Edomae sushi is Tokyo-style sushi. The second: Because many samurai lived in Edo during the Edo period, slicing eel from the belly was considered unlucky, so the way eels were cut in Edo began with a back-to-back approach.

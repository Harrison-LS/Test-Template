Golden Gate Bridge | "San Andreas"


The Golden Gate Bridge is America's western gateway and has appeared in countless movies. Recently, the disaster epic "San Andreas" starring Dwayne Johnson featured thrilling scenes where skyscrapers and the Golden Gate Bridge were both destroyed by the super destructive power brought by earthquakes, with extremely shocking visual effects.


In Hollywood movies, the Golden Gate Bridge has indeed been destroyed many times: the first time the Golden Gate Bridge was destroyed was in the 1955 film "The Beast from the East"; 1978's "Superman" saved the Golden Gate Bridge; in 1989 James Cameron's early sci-fi film "The Abyss," the Golden Gate Bridge also nearly reached the edge of destruction; in 2003's "Center of the Earth: Inferno," strip radiation passing through the ozone hole melted the Golden Gate Bridge into two pieces; in 2004 NBC's "10.5" earthquake, the Golden Gate Bridge could not escape unharmed; in 2006 sci-fi film "X-Men: The Last Stand," the Golden Gate Bridge was uprooted completely; in 2009 animated movie "Monsters vs. Aliens" it was destroyed once more; in a low-budget TV film from 2010 called "When Meteorites Strike Earth," the Golden Gate Bridge was pierced by an asteroid, collapsing entirely; in 2011's "Rise of the Planet of the Apes," that minor damage might not even be worth mentioning; in 2013's "Pacific Rim," the first city to be attacked was San Francisco, and the Golden Gate Bridge suffered again; in 2014's "Godzilla" it destroyed the Golden Gate Bridge once more; then came "San Andreas," which used a container ship to crush the bridge—a method somewhat imitating the Korean film "The Raid on Haeundae"; and in 2015's "Terminator: Genisys," the bridge was blown up so thoroughly there wasn't even a trace left.


Golden Gate Bridge

·Built using over 100,000 tons of steel, it is a symbol of San Francisco. This orange-red bridge spans across the San Francisco Bay and the Golden Gate Strait, spectacular and magnificent.

·During the Gold Rush era, it was named because it resembled a gateway leading to gold mines.

The observation point north of the bridge offers the best view of its entire panorama.

• Many American blockbusters such as "Rise of the Planet of the Apes" and "X-Men" also frequently feature the bridge.

• Dedicated pedestrian walkways are set up on both sides of the bridge; walking across it with the sea breeze or cycling back and forth are both great experiences.


Redwood Park | "Rise of the Planet of the Apes"


"Rise of the Planet of the Apes" Trilogy


is a prequel to the science fiction film "Planet of the Apes", directed by Rupert Wyatt, and starring James Franco, Tom Felton, Freida Pinto, Brian Cox, etc. The plot mainly tells various experiences before apes evolved into highly intelligent beings and subsequently conquered Earth; the theme has a cautionary nature—the evil consequences produced by human crazy ambition.


The exterior filming location of this film is Redwood Park north of the Golden Gate Bridge. California redwoods are generally divided into two types; the ones appearing in the film are called Coast redwoods, one of the tallest plants in the world, able to grow up to 120 meters tall with an age of about 2000+ years.

Trees with the scientific name Sequoia sempervirens are known as Redwoods (translated as Redwood), have brown-black bark, and are among the tallest plants in the world. They can live for approximately 2000+ years.


The Red & White Ferry | "How to Be a Millionaire"


"How to Be a Millionaire" is a 2015 mainland Chinese urban romantic drama, starring Huang Xiaoming, Yang Mi, Tong Dawei, and Angelababy. This film officially began filming in Shanghai on December 17, 2014, then moved to San Francisco, USA the following February for additional location shooting. Many scenes of San Francisco appear in the film. In 2008, America: On a cruise ship departing from San Francisco Bay and heading toward the famous Devil's Island, Zhang Mo Sheng took photos for tourists at a price of $5 per photo. The scene where the female lead takes pictures on the ferry is the famous Red & White Ferry in San Francisco.


Since 1939, this cruise tour has become San Francisco's unique bay area sightseeing service. It is also the only San Francisco boat tour experience offering interpretation in 12 languages. Admire the magnificent Golden Gate Bridge and the infamous Alcatraz Prison; as you pass famous landmarks along your route, audio commentary brings you into contact with San Francisco's rich history.


Palace of Fine Arts | Cliffhanger


In the movie "Cliffhanger," the touching scene where Mason, played by Sean Connery, reunites with his daughter in a garden is actually filmed within part of the Palace of Fine Arts. Built to commemorate San Francisco's rebirth from the devastating earthquake, this ancient Roman-style architectural masterpiece was constructed for the 1915 Panama-Pacific Exposition held in San Francisco. Besides its distinctive Roman dome and colonnades, there are willow-shadowed fountains swaying gently—the atmosphere is superb.


Many films were shot at the Palace of Fine Arts, including "The Princess Diaries" (2001), starring Julie Andrews and Anne Hathaway, as well as "Mrs. Doubtfire" (2006) with Robin Williams in the lead role.


American Palace of Fine Arts

The original Palace of Fine Arts in San Francisco was built in 1915 specifically for the Panama-Pacific Exposition and attracted visitors numbering up to 18 million at that time. However, after the event concluded it fell into disuse until 1962 when renowned German-born architect Meinhard de la Merced was commissioned to redesign and rebuild it. Specializing in meticulous interior detailing, his work explains the exquisite craftsmanship style of this Palace of Fine Arts.

Today, the Palace of Fine Arts has become a favorite leisure spot for San Franciscans. Swans and ducks play in the pond together, and the Palace of Fine Arts Theater is also located here - making it an ideal place to relax on holidays.


Alcatraz | "Escape from Alcatraz" "Birdman of Alcatraz"


When mentioning San Francisco's Alcatraz, people will inevitably think of the movie "Cliffhanger", where this island is the main setting and two protagonists engage in fierce combat with rebels here. Recently, FOX TV series "ALCATRAZ" has reignited interest in Alcatraz. Additionally, the 2006 sci-fi film "X-Men: The Last Battle" starring Hugh Jackman was also filmed on the island.


"Birdman of Alcatraz" from "Escape from Alcatraz" has attracted even more movie fans to visit. From 1907 to 1963, this place served as a prison for serious criminals. Today it's become a tourist prison with restaurants, seafood shops, and souvenir stores everywhere. Departing from Fisherman's Wharf, take a cruise at Pier 41, and you can reach the island in just 15 minutes. There are visitor limits on the island, so it's best to make an appointment in advance.


Alcatraz

Alcatraz is a 22-acre rocky island that was once America's federal prison for housing serious criminals.

Notorious criminals who were once imprisoned here include Chicago's "Godfather" Capone, the bloodthirsty yet gifted "Birdman" Strauder, and the ruthless "Machine Gun Killer" Kelly, among others. Inmates could only gaze at the vibrant San Francisco through the prison's iron windows, subjected to another cruel form of mental punishment. Today, the entire island preserves as much of its original prison appearance as possible, with facilities including a bird garden, museum, bookstore, and more. The shops on the island sell memoirs from those whose sentences have ended, all claiming it was an "eternally unforgettable painful experience." Despite its grim history, this island also serves as a sanctuary for wildlife, with many bird species nesting here, making it an ideal spot for visitors to relax and reflect.


Chinatown | "Police Story 4: First Strike"


Starring Jackie Chan, Anita Mui, Danny Niu, and Faye Yu, the film tells the story of Hong Kong police officer Ma Hanqiang's brave fight against criminal syndicates in America. The "Fan Qu" in Red Fan Qu refers to the San Francisco Bay Area, while "Red" signifies bloodshed and violence. "Police Story 4: First Strike" was Jackie Chan's first film with a production budget exceeding 100 million yuan. The cost of shooting this film resulted in Jackie Chan injuring his left leg. During one fight scene, when he performed an aerial somersault to kick at his opponent, his left leg accidentally struck the corner of a refrigerator, causing the calf muscle to tear instantly and blood to gush forth.


San Francisco's Chinatown

San Francisco's Chinatown is the only place in Western America that can be compared with New York's Chinatown, formed at the intersection of Grant Avenue and California Street.

About 80,000 overseas Chinese residents live here, making it completely a city within a country with an exotic atmosphere. Although San Francisco's most spectacular Union Square and the western financial center are located right next to Chinatown, inside Chinatown you can see signs written entirely in Chinese characters, and everything you hear is also in Mandarin. This is a place where overseas Chinese feel extremely familiar. Pan Am Pyramid | "The Pursuit of Happyness" In 2006, Will Smith starred in an inspirational movie based on true events that made people cry: "The Pursuit of Happyness". Most scenes were filmed in San Francisco, telling the story of a medical equipment salesman with extremely bad luck who drifted around with his son, struggled hard, and finally became a millionaire. The skyscraper Pan Am Pyramid Building shown in the movie is pyramid-shaped, with 48 floors total. It was once the tallest building in San Francisco and west of Chicago in America. Now it has been replaced by the SALESFORCE tower. There's also the scene where Will takes his child to a daycare center, which was filmed at a childcare center in San Francisco's Chinatown. The protagonist saw a character painted on the wall: "Happyness", and he immediately said "spelled wrong". In fact it should be "Happiness", yet this movie used "Happyness" for its title, changing i to y. It seems the screenwriter wanted to express a deeper meaning.


Pan Pacific Pyramid

The Pan Pacific Pyramid Building is a pyramid-shaped structure with 48 floors, making it San Francisco's tallest building. When first completed, people could not accept its style and nicknamed it "a spear thrust from hell" and "an Indian's tent." This building serves as the symbolic headquarters of Union Fidelity Life Insurance Company, with many company artworks displayed in the entrance lobby. The exterior features a half-acre urban open space where music concerts can be held. The Pan Pacific Pyramid Building is primarily used as an office tower and is not open to the general public; tourists cannot climb to the top floors for sightseeing. However, on the 27th floor observation deck that is accessible to visitors, one can see the Bay Bridge, Golden Gate Bridge, Alcatraz Island, and other landmarks.


Crissy Field Park | "Hereafter" "Vertigo"


In 2010, director Clint Eastwood helmed "Hereafter," a film exploring where life goes after death. It depicts the emotional journeys and convergence of three people from different countries.

One scene featuring the male lead Matt Damon, who resides in San Francisco and possesses telepathic abilities. A conversation with a friend was filmed at Crissy Field Park in San Francisco.


In the past, many famous films and television series have been filmed here, including Alfred Hitchcock's 1958 film "Vertigo".


This location offers an expansive coastline with gentle beaches and the San Francisco Bay ahead. To the right lie wetlands, while to the left are rocky cliffs and the piers of the Golden Gate Bridge. Many couples often come here to enjoy romantic moments together.


It is also the main viewing stand for the annual Blue Angels air show, with many films having been shot here. The most famous among them is "The Presidio" (1998), starring Sean Connery.

Union Square | "Zodiac"


The 2007 suspense thriller "Zodiac" was adapted from true events. Jake Gyllenhaal starred in the film, portraying a cartoonist for the "San Francisco Chronicle". Relying on intuition and thorough research, he solved a cold case that had remained unsolved for 40 years.

The three-story old building of the "San Francisco Chronicle" featured in the film is located on Mission Street in downtown San Francisco, just two blocks away from Union Square.


Union Square is a popular dating and meeting spot in San Francisco as well as a shopping district. On one side of the square stands a building with red and white exterior and classical floral railings - the Union Square Hotel, a premier hotel themed around movies. The hotel lobby, room furnishings, furniture and wall decorations are all inspired by films, while its classical-style exterior ornamentation is also worth stopping to admire.


Woody Allen's series of urban films were shot last year in an apartment building at Union Square, the liveliest part of San Francisco. Before that, Union Square had always been a must-visit location for filming movies in San Francisco. In 1996, The Rock starring Sean Connery and Nicolas Cage featured car chases with Ferraris and Hummers, finally crashing into San Francisco's famous cable cars at Union Square - considered a classic police chase scene.


Union Square is located in the downtown area. In the center of the square stands a tall monument established to commemorate the US Navy defeating the Spanish fleet at Manila Bay. Four streets surround the square, featuring unique shops and upscale department stores. Besides I. Magnin and Macy's, there are hundreds of high-end boutiques and fashion stores, truly a paradise for shoppers.

Additionally, San Francisco's Theater District is located not far southwest of Union Square. Even if you don't buy anything, simply strolling around will leave you with endless surprises.


Nob Hill | "Just Like Heaven"


Released in 2005 and starring Reese Witherspoon and Mark Ruffalo, "Just Like Heaven" tells the story of a San Francisco architect who falls into an out-of-this-world romantic love with a beautiful woman in a coma. The film's setting is an elegant old apartment building that has made many movie fans eager to visit this historic house. Its address is on Manson Street in Nob Hill, one of San Francisco's oldest neighborhoods.


Nob Hill locals also call it "the Hilltop." Several streets in this area feature stunning old houses, and numerous films have been shot here. Marilyn Monroe's former residence was also located in this district. Visitors can enjoy elevated views overlooking the bay scenery from Nob Hill.


Nob Hill

Nob Hill is the highest hill in downtown San Francisco and also represents the most aristocratic area within the city. "Nob" is a Sindhi term from India's colonial era, meaning "great wealthy person" or "influential figure." In fact, when the first cable car reached this hill directly in 1878, two magnates who became wealthy through gold mining and four tycoons who made their fortune through the Pacific Railroad built magnificent mansions on the hill, transforming it into a gathering place for the rich and giving the area its name. However, all these grand estates were destroyed by fire in the great earthquake of 1906. What stands today are luxury hotels and beautiful churches. These outstanding buildings center around Huntington Park like satellites orbiting a moon, creating an atmosphere that continues to radiate past elegance and refinement. Besides the large hotels and churches, Nob Hill remains San Francisco's upscale residential district today, home to many members of high society. Most people strolling and resting at Huntington Park are well-dressed ladies, most still leading a long-haired small dog, together enjoying California's clear sunny skies—a world entirely different from the ragged homeless wandering in North Beach's Washington Square Park.

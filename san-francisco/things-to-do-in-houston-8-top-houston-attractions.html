Things To Do in Houston: 8 Top Houston Attractions

Houston is the fourth largest city in the U.S., but many travelers skip over it. Big mistake! You would miss a great museum district, the food scene influenced by over 145 different cultures, NASA’s Mission Control Center, and the Houston Livestock Show and Rodeo, the largest livestock show and rodeo in the world. You should prepare for it to be hot, and you should also get a car because driving is a lot easier than using public transport or ride hailing (though those options work in some neighborhoods). Here’s a list of 8 must-see places to visit in Houston, Texas.

1. Space Center Houston

Visit Space Center Houston, the official visitor center for NASA’s Johnson Space Center, in Texas.

Space Center Houston is not just for space fans. You can climb aboard the Independence shuttle replica, which is mounted on a Boeing 747, and you can walk next to the Saturn V (the largest rocket that was flown successfully, until NASA’s Space Launch System was launched in 2022). You can also take a tram tour that goes through active parts of NASA's Johnson Space Center, including the Apollo Mission Control Center, which is kept as it was during the lunar missions.

At Starship Gallery, one of the unique opportunities patrons can experience is the chance to touch a real piece of the Moon. Patrons should also plan to spend at least half a day at this location. Due to the fact that this attraction can get very busy, it is recommended to purchase tickets online ahead of time, especially for weekends. Adult tickets are approximately US$30-40. Starship Gallery is located about 40 minutes South of Downtown Houston. If you do not have a vehicle, taking a rideshare service such as Uber is typically the easiest option. If you are also planning to visit Kemah Boardwalk, it is only about 15 minutes away, so both attractions can be visited in the same day.

2. Museum District

District of museums in Houston

Houston’s Museum District has 19 museums around Hermann Park. It is one of the most concentrated cultural areas in the country. The Houston Museum of Natural Science has dinosaur fossils and a famous gemstone and mineral hall. The Museum of Fine Arts, Houston (MFAH) has multiple centuries worth of artwork, and the Cullen Sculpture Garden, which is free to access. The Menil Collection also has free access from Wednesday to Sunday and has modern and contemporary art, including a collection of Surrealist works. It is located in a calm residential area near the Rothko Chapel.

Many museums have certain days of the week when you can go for free. For example, the Museum of Fine Arts, Houston (MFAH) is free every Thursday. The Houston Museum of Natural Science is free every first Tuesday of the month from 5 pm to 8 pm, but you will have to pull out your wallet for the special exhibits and other premium experiences. Our advice: Trying to see everything in one day is not the best idea. If you go to more than two or three museums, you will likely experience museum fatigue. This can happen even before the afternoon is over.

3. Buffalo Bayou Park

Buffalo Bayou Park, Houston

Located on the water west of downtown, Buffalo Bayou Park offers unmatched views of the Houston skyline. The park is a favorite spot for walking, running, cycling, and kayaking. Starting in spring and going through fall, at dusk, Mexican free-tailed bats create a striking local spectacle as they come out from under the Waugh Drive Bridge. Bat viewing is a free activity and one of the most unique ways to spend an evening in Houston.

The Cistern is a cultural venue that offers guided tours that can be booked online. The stunning columns, unique vibe and special acoustics are what make this place one of a kind. The Buffalo Bayou Park is located a few minutes away from downtown Houston, as long as the traffic isn’t bad. Parking is first come, first served so there are a few free spots located by the Sabine Street, though they tend to fill up quickly. Just like with other urban parks, keep any valuables hidden in the car and make sure to lock it!

4. Areas to discover

East Downtown, Houston

Houston is over 1,600 km². That's why driving or using a ride-hailing service is the easiest way to get around here. For instance, you can start in Montrose, one of the most diverse districts where you can find many galleries, bars, and restaurants and even access The Menil Collection. Further northwest of downtown, The Heights offers an even slower pace with early 20th century wooden homes, and weekend farmers' markets as well as independent coffee shops.

Murals on entire buildings are the norm in EaDo (East Downtown), and also where a majority of Houston’s street art is located. Downtown gets busy in the evenings around the stadiums and Discovery Green. An interesting feature of the city center is an air-conditioned tunnel system that allows for a break from the oppressive heat of Houston’s summers. One thing to keep in mind is that the tunnels are primarily designed for office workers, so access is restricted during non-business hours and several parts of the tunnel are closed on weekends.

5. Houston Zoo + Downtown Aquarium

Entry to Houston Zoo

You can easily spend a day at Houston Zoo, especially if you are going with kids. The zoo is spread over 55 acres, and has thousands of animals from hundreds of species. The zoo has attractions like the African Forest with gorillas and okapis, and South American Pantanal where you’ll find jaguars. If you plan on going, make sure to purchase your tickets online prior to your visit, especially on holidays/weekends as tickets sell out quickly. Also note that the first Tuesday of every month, you can visit the zoo for free, however, you must make online reservations which are limited. Lastly, do not buy food and drinks there, and bring a water bottle to save some money.

The Downtown Aquarium is designed for families with kids. It is located inside an old freight train building and power station (built in the late 19th century) and includes immersion exhibits, a tiny train ride that goes through a shark tunnel, a ray touch tank, and a couple of rides. The most concerning part of the visit is the white Bengal tiger exhibit. The outdoor enclosure that was added after advocacy groups put concerns about the conditions of the animals. Captive conditions of the animals have been criticized for a long time.

The restaurant does have a unique space, as the dining area is set up around a 560,000-litre aquarium. If you are traveling solo or as a couple without kids, you may want to consider skipping it as there is an entrance fee and most guests don’t spend much time there.

6. Kemah Boardwalk

Kemah Boardwalk, close to Houston

Kemah is located southeast of Houston along Galveston Bay, which is about a 45 minute drive. Kemah is good for a laid back day. If you're looking for some fun after visiting Space Center Houston, which is about a 15 minute drive, you can check out the waterfront boardwalk. There you will find a ferris wheel, a wooden roller coaster, speedboat rides in the bay, and a few seafood restaurants that overlook the water. Access to the boardwalk is free and there are outdoor concerts and seasonal events that take place.

The price for a day pass or individual attraction tickets is around $30 to $40 depending on age. The area is a little bit cheesy and designed for families to have fun at the attractions but can be less enjoyable for couples or solo travelers. It’s an easier and more enjoyable experience on weekdays as there is a lot more room to enjoy the views of the bay. For those looking to eat at the waterfront restaurants, it’s recommended to make reservations on weekends as it gets busy.

7. Going to a game

Daikin Park, Houston

Going to a pro game in Houston is a whole experience. There is entertainment during breaks, you can get Texas style food, and the crowd is hype. You can even go without knowing any of the rules and still enjoy it. For your first American sporting event, you can choose between the Houston Astros (baseball, Daikin Park) and the Houston Rockets (basketball, Toyota Center). Make sure to buy tickets way before the game, especially when there are big teams playing against each other because middle seats get sold out fast.

Do you plan to go to Texas between the end of February and early March? You have to make a visit to the Houston Livestock Show and Rodeo which is a Texas tradition at NRG Park. This multiweek event includes rodeo competitions, major concerts, agricultural exhibits, carnival rides, and tons of food! With millions of attendees, this event is one of the largest livestock shows and rodeos in the world.

8. Culinary Tour in Houston

Ninfa's Original, Houston

In Houston, over 145 languages are spoken, and this is diversity is also present on the city’s dining scene. The fajitas that have become commonplace at restaurants across the U.S. were first introduced in the 1970s at Mama Ninfa Laurenzo’s restaurant. The Original Ninfa’s on Navigation is a landmark of Tex-Mex cuisine. Killen’s Barbecue is renowned for Texas barbecue and its slow-smoked brisket, ribs, and house-made sausage.

Make sure to stop by and check out Viet-Cajun seafood: they serve crawfish and shellfish prepared using Vietnamese cooking methods and seasoned with Louisiana-style spices. This type of Houston-born fusion cuisine is still hard to come by. For even more of a food scene experience, check out Bellaire Boulevard, which has one of the highest concentrations of Asian restaurants in the country. The surrounding areas also have Indian, Ethiopian, and Nigerian restaurants.

For more creative cuisine, you can’t go wrong with Nancy’s Hustle. The cheeseburger served on an English muffin, spicy pork dumplings, and Nancy cakes, which are little griddled cakes that come with cultured butter and smoked trout roe, can be ordered with the cocktail list. The restaurant is confident and distinct, steering clear of the common cliches of Texan dining.

Useful info for your visit to Houston

What is the way to Houston?

Taking a flight is the only realistic option from France. There are nonstop flights from Air France from Paris Charles de Gaulle to Houston George Bush Intercontinental Airport (IAH) with an approximate flight time of 11 hours. Return fares vary based on season and how far in advance you book, so it is best to look at different dates and airlines for the best deal.

How to get around Houston

In order to fully experience Houston a car is advised. Houston has an area of more than 1,600 km² which is too large to walk and explore different neighborhoods. Although METRORail has three lines that run through Downtown and the Museum District and end at NRG Park, they do not go out as far as the Space Center Houston, Kemah, or The Heights. Uber and Lyft are also good options but the prices can be very high. If a car is not rented then be prepared to spend a lot of money on ridesharing.

Where to stay in Houston

For short-stay visitors to Houston, Montrose is one of the most practical neighborhoods. It is centrally located close to the Menil Collection and the Museum District, featuring a variety of accommodations for all budgets. If you want easy access to bars and restaurants, Midtown is a good option. Downtown is good for the games and big events, but other than that it can feel pretty quiet. If you are just trying to visit the space center then don’t book near Space Center Houston.

Will you need a visa to enter the United States?

Traveling from France to the U.S.A. is easy, as there is no Visa requirement. However, a travel authorization (ESTA) must be obtained prior to travel. This authorization can be completed online for a fee of $40. An ESTA is valid for 2 years, so you don’t have to pay the fee again if you travel to the U.S.A. multiple times within the next 2 years. Keep in mind that your travel authorization has to be completed at least 3 days prior to your flight.

What is the best time to visit Houston?

The best time to visit Houston is March to April and October to November. During these months, the weather is usually mild with a temperature range between 20 to 27 degrees, and the humidity is not as bad. Try not to come in July and August as the temperature goes up, the humidity is unbearable and it even thunderstorms. These conditions make sightseeing impossible.

Are tourists safe in Houston?

Yes, especially in the main tourist attractions. Like any other large city, do not walk alone in the quiet, unfamiliar areas of Midtown and south of Downtown during the night. Usually, a car or rideshare is the better choice for visitors when it is dark.

What is the language in Houston?

Although English is the official language, Houston is one of the most linguistically varied cities of the United States. Spanish is often heard, particularly in numerous Hispanic and Latino communities throughout the city. the usage of French is rare.

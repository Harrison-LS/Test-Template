Memory Lane | Highway One + San Francisco + Napa: An Unconventional Journey

【PROLOGUE】


On Christmas Day in 2017, some friends came to Los Angeles and planned a Highway One trip, so I happily joined them.

The outbound journey was all highway, driving straight to San Francisco, then slowly making our way back with stops along the route for the return trip.


Yes, Los Angeles is just a vast, desolate rural area.


【San Francisco】


I prefer this translation: "San Fran City" over "San Francisco".


Day and Night


San Fran City sits nestled between mountains and sea, with constantly shifting elevations along the roads that make driving physically demanding. Yet this challenging terrain perfectly captures my vision of San Francisco - dramatic winding paths rising and falling, densely layered architecture, trams gliding through streets, unexpected beauty around every corner like elegant churches or artistic murals. I'd casually step into a café for a moment to drink coffee, then continue my journey...


Behind me lies the famous Nine Curves Flower Street, crowded with people. Paradoxically, this path leading toward the sea is even more beautiful.


[Accommodation]

We stayed at the InterContinental Hotel in San Francisco, which has an excellent location. Restaurants, coffee shops, and shopping are all within walking distance. Because the weather was so nice, even when we later went to Nine Curves Flower Street and Fisherman's Wharf, we took our time with stops along the way, slowly walking over. However (there's always a "however" 😂), because this hotel is quite old, the facilities are very outdated, which slightly affected the experience. Nevertheless, many hotels in the United States are actually quite aged, so being worn down is normal.


San Francisco InterContinental Hotel

The San Francisco InterContinental (InterContinental San Francisco) represents the newest luxury hotel opening in the Bay Area. This impressive 32-story glass facade building stands prominently among city structures, silently observing the growth and transformation of the South of Market district. As a non-smoking property, it offers convenient transportation with easy access throughout the city, including proximity to Moscone Convention Center, Union Square, Chinatown, and the Powell Street cable car station.


Pier39


It seems every coastal destination has its own Fisherman's Wharf 😂, and San Francisco is certainly no exception to this pattern.

Pier 39 is one of the busiest piers, offering everything from food and entertainment with plenty of options. I visited during the evening and was greeted by beautiful pink-purple sunset clouds - absolutely stunning! There are many good restaurants here too; I carefully selected one to try and will discuss it later.


Pier 39 Marina


Golden Gate Bridge


The Golden Gate Bridge is probably the San Francisco attraction I'm most interested in. This magnificent red bridge spans across both sides, and I've seen this iconic red bridge countless times in movies or other films. Finally, I was able to view its full form from afar, looking beautiful against the blue sea and sky!


Walking down from the bridgehead, there's a viewing platform by the seaside. Looking up at the Golden Gate Bridge, turning around and you'll encounter sea lions playing in the water - so cute!


Golden Gate Bridge

·Built using over 100,000 tons of steel, this is a symbol of San Francisco. The orange-red bridge spans across San Francisco Bay and the Golden Gate Strait, spectacular and majestic.

·Named after its role as a gateway to gold mines during the Gold Rush. The viewpoint on the north side of the bridge offers the best panoramic views of the entire bridge.

·The bridge appears in many major American films, including "Rise of the Planet of the Apes," "X-Men," and more.

·Dedicated walking paths are set up on both sides of the bridge. Walking with sea breeze above the bridge, or cycling back and forth, are both excellent experiences.

This coffee shop uses pure handmade equipment for brewing coffee. The most famous item here is siphon-style coffee, which can usually only be found in Tokyo. Their morel mushroom poached eggs and European sandwiches are also very popular.


Can't Stop Eating


All the restaurants in California feel pretty much the same, and here there are probably a huge number of Chinese people. I feel like there are many reliable Chinese restaurants—especially Cantonese food.


Lingnan Restaurant


On Christmas Day, the first day we arrived in San Francisco (California time), almost all restaurants were closed. Even the hotel's dining rooms were fully booked... For a while we actually couldn't find anywhere to eat... Starving and anxious... We had no choice but to come to this super popular Chinese restaurant that was already famous enough on normal days, plus Christmas... There was a dense crowd of people standing outside... We got in line for tickets, then went across the street to slurp down a bowl of Vietnamese noodles, then came back to continue waiting.


We didn't actually get seated until around 10 PM or so? I'm not entirely sure. At that moment we were truly starving, hungry enough to feel dizzy, hungry enough to be too weak to take pictures (so there isn't a single photo), hungry enough that I completely forgot what we ate (or if it was good or bad)... It's that popular for a reason, right? Should taste good! 😂


Lingnan Restaurant

Located in San Francisco's Chinatown, Lingnan Restaurant has been recommended by the Michelin Guide for multiple consecutive years. Its exquisite cuisine has not only won over the hearts of many Americans and international tourists seeking authentic Chinese food, but also satisfied the discerning palates of many local Chinese people. It can truly be said to be one of the famous Chinese restaurants you must try when visiting San Francisco.

Absolutely must-try signature house specialty: salted-crusted steamed crab! During this crab's peak season, if you don't eat it, your trip would be in vain!

【Napa】


The Hess Collection Winery


People who drink red wine can't not know Napa, there are countless wineries here, we randomly selected one, very good.

For one person, the cost is about 20-30 USD to taste approximately 5-6 glasses of wine. Very lucky to have met a sommelier from China who bought some wines and then enthusiastically recommended a local restaurant for us - really I can give it a full score!


When we arrived at the vineyard, it was already near evening, and it was winter as well, so we didn't get to see their hillside grapevines. But unexpectedly, there is actually a small contemporary art gallery here, and even more amazingly, there is a work by a Chinese contemporary artist.


The temperature difference between day and night is huge, please pay attention to staying warm...


Heitz Vineyard

Heitz Vineyard is built upon historical ruins, witnessing Napa Valley's history of first wine production in the 1860s. The stone building at the original vineyard site was completed in 1903. The initial stone structure now serves as a world-class modern art collection museum. Recognized by "ARTnews" magazine as one of the top 200 art galleries worldwide. Currently this vineyard is staffed with Chinese-speaking employees, serving numerous Chinese tourists.

17 Miles


It is said that 17 Miles got its name because it is the most beautiful 17 miles on Highway One.

So I want to say... um... actually it's quite beautiful.


17 Miles

The 17 Mile Drive is a world-famous tourist destination located in Monterey County, central California, surrounding the renowned Pebble Beach resort. The western and southern sides border the Pacific Ocean, while the eastern side traverses through Del Monte Forests. Along the way, you can not only see the blue sea and sky, white beaches, but also drive on rugged winding mountain roads with strange rocks creating a new view at every step. You can occasionally see spectacular luxury villas and four golf courses, including Pebble Beach Golf Course which is one of the best golf courses in the world.


Carmel

I really love this small town!!! Surrounded by mountains and sea, roads are lined with charming little houses each with unique styles facing the ocean. People on the beach either happily walk their dogs or leisurely sunbathe lying down. In the town, there's about a gallery or art shop every few steps. I randomly chose an Italian restaurant—also delicious!


Carmel Seaside Town

Carmel seaside town has become a major highlight along California Highway 1 and the 17 Mile Drive with its beautiful natural environment and elegant artistic atmosphere.

Here, whether it be a fashion boutique or antique shop, candy store or art gallery, toy shop or craft emporium, each will captivate visitors from the storefront to every item on display.

Centuries ago, "Carmel of artists, poets and writers" was already renowned. In 1969, the famous Chinese traditional painting master Zhang Daqian once resided here, calling his dwelling "Ke Yi Ju."

In this Carmel that resembles a paradise on earth, many uniquely styled artists and writers live in this small Bohemian city with mountains behind and sea before. The peculiar architecture and scenery are as beautiful as fairy tales. Residents here strive against modernization. Today the town still prohibits advertising signs, neon lighting or fast food establishments to maintain its original character. Heading west along Ocean Avenue, the main street of the town to its end lies Carmel Beach - where 17-Mile Drive possesses a one-of-a-kind expansive sandy beach.


Bixby Creek Bridge


Do you find it familiar? Have you seen the American drama【Big Little Lies】? Is that the winding bridge from the opening sequence???? It truly appears in countless dramas and films... When visiting during foggy weather, there is an entirely different charm.


Bixby Creek Bridge

The Bixby Bridge is an open single-arch structure situated on California's Highway 1 in Big Sur, spanning approximately 100 meters - the longest concrete arch bridge in California's highway system.

The bridge opened in 1932, was the world's highest single-arch concrete bridge at that time, and to this day remains one of the most popular tourist attractions.

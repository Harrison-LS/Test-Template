Top 10 Must-Visit Attractions in San Francisco, USA (Including 2-Day Itinerary Recommendations)

Everyone has their own unique version of San Francisco in their heart, but if you're visiting San Francisco for the first time, you must know about these attractions! If after reading this guide you have any other questions you'd like to ask, click on "Contact Local Butler" or "Contact Them" above~


I. Top 10 Must-Visit Attractions in San Francisco


1. Golden Gate Bridge—San Francisco's Postcard


Highlights: Hollywood movie photo spot, mist-covered Golden Gate Bridge


The Golden Gate Bridge is a symbol of San Francisco and even California, as well as an important landmark building representing the city. The blue water reflecting against its orange-red structure makes this bay area so beautiful. It has also been featured in many Hollywood movies such as "San Andreas," "Rise of the Planet of the Apes," "X-Men," and more. Walking or cycling across the bridge with the sea breeze is an excellent way to admire it. Dedicated walking paths are set up on both sides, with bicycle traffic permitted on the west side. To see the mist-covered Golden Gate Bridge, try visiting in the morning when your chances of seeing this sight are higher.

Renting a bicycle to ride across the Golden Gate Bridge is also an unforgettable memory.


The Golden Gate Bridge

·Constructed using over 100,000 tons of steel, it stands as a symbol of San Francisco. The orange-red bridge spans across San Francisco Bay and the Golden Gate Strait, impressive and majestic.

·During the Gold Rush era, it was named because it resembled a gateway to gold mines. The viewing point on the north side offers the best vantage for enjoying panoramic views of the entire bridge.

·The bridge often appears in numerous major American films such as "Rise of the Planet of the Apes" and "X-Men".

·Walking paths are specially set up on both sides of the bridge. Walking across the bridge with sea breezes accompanying you, or cycling back and forth across it are both excellent experiences.


Tips:

There are many viewing points for the Golden Gate Bridge; everyone can choose according to their own route:

1. The Golden Gate Bridge Pavilion on the south side of the bridge has a large parking lot with mountains as its backdrop, suitable for morning photography. It also features visitor centers, educational exhibitions, and more.

2. The Vista point on the north side of the bridge is a location where you can view the Golden Gate Bridge from above (just go down off the bridge). Morning photography has favorable lighting, and you can also enjoy city views.

3. Fort Point, located beneath the eastern side of the bridge on the south side, is an excellent viewpoint for seeing the Golden Gate Bridge from below. Morning photography provides good lighting.

4. Marin Headlands in the west direction from the north side of the bridge offers many parking spots along the way with distant views of the Golden Gate Bridge.

5. Battery Spencer observation point on the other side, near the western section, is also quite nice.

6. If you want to drive onto the bridge, take US Highway 101 or SR 1.


Tickets: Pedestrian walkways are free to both sides of the bridge; parking at viewpoints is free; vehicle tolls from north to south are one-way only and do not accept cash. Payment can be made online, with a payment window of 30 days before crossing up to 2 days after crossing the bridge, $6 per trip

Operating Hours: Free passage for vehicles and bicycles on the Golden Gate Bridge is available all day; pedestrian access hours on walking paths: regular time 5:00 AM - 6:30 PM, daylight saving time 5:00 AM - 9:00 PM

Recommended Visit Duration: 1-2 hours

Transportation: Take buses 2/4/8/10/18/24/27/38/44/54/56/58/70/72/72X/74/76/92/93/97/101/101X to the Golden Gate Bridge Toll Plaza stop, or drive.

Location: Golden Gate Bridge, San Francisco, CA 94129


Fisherman's Wharf — Eat Giant Pacific Crabs and Watch Sea Lions


Highlights: A great place to taste seafood in San Francisco


Fisherman's Wharf generally covers the area from Ghirardelli Square to Pier 35. The landmark is a round sign featuring a large crab, and finding this sign means you've arrived at Fisherman's Wharf.

Although there is no fine soft sand beach, here you will find a dazzling array of shops. Every morning, fishermen set sail from Fisherman's Wharf to catch the treasure crab and other seafood that California produces abundantly. Seafood lovers remember to visit Pier 47 for fresh catches! Besides this, if you have time, be sure to stop by the nearby Boudin Bakery to taste its natural and healthy sourdough bread. Finally, it must be mentioned that the famous Pier 39 of Fisherman's Wharf is simply the essence of Fisherman's Wharf—definitely don't miss out!


San Francisco Fisherman's Wharf

·Fisherman's Wharf is a household name in San Francisco, extending from Ghirardelli Square to Pier 35.

·You can see the bustling Pier 39 with its colorful shops, restaurants, and street performers, and also enjoy distant views of Alcatraz Island, the Golden Gate Bridge, and the Bay Bridge's various landscapes.

·At Pier 39, you can see numerous sea lions lazily lying on rafts basking in the sun and taking naps.

·From November to June each year is exactly the best season to enjoy seafood. During this time at Fisherman's Wharf, you can savor fine Dungeness crab.

·For shopping enthusiasts, Fisherman's Wharf not only allows you to see various specialty products but also offers locally distinctive souvenirs.


Tickets: Free

Opening Hours: Open all day

Suggested Visit Time: One day

Transportation: Can take buses 2, 4, 8, 18, 24, 27, 38, 44, 54, 56, 58, 72, 74, or 76 to Beach St &

Taylor St Station, then walk 2 more minutes to arrive. Or take the F Market light rail line and get off at Beach St & Stockton St Station or The Embarcadero & Stockton St Station

Sight location: Pier 39, San Francisco, CA 94133


3. Nine Curves Flower Street—one of the world's super curved streets


Highlights: Z-shaped road, surrounded by flowers


The official name of Nine Curves Flower Street is Lombard Street, which is one of the most curved streets in the world, with a total of eight turns. Driving through it is a challenge that tests your skills! To avoid unnecessary traffic accidents, the area is planted with plants and flowers. It's named after these flowers and greenery, hence the name Nine Curves Flower Street. From a high point on Lombard Street, you can enjoy a panoramic view of San Francisco, with layers upon layers of houses surrounded by thriving flowers. Walking slowly down the curved slope of Lombard Street, people inside cars open their windows, shouting, laughing at tourists outside. Some even stick their bodies out of the car windows, making strange poses to show off their happiness and joy~ So after arriving in San Francisco, Lombard Street is also a must-visit attraction.


Lombard Street

·Officially named Lombard Street, it is the world's most curved street. From the upper portion of Flower Street, you can view the Bay Bridge and Coit Tower in the distance.

·The city government constructed flower beds to prevent traffic accidents; when vehicles pass through here, speed must not exceed 5 miles per hour.

·Within a short stretch contains eight sharp turns. The combination of a 40-degree slope with "Z"-shaped winding roads seriously tests driving skills.

·Flower beds on both sides of the lanes are filled with roses; during bloom season, viewed from afar, it resembles a velvet embroidery hanging diagonally, a sight beyond compare.


Ticket: Free

Operating Hours: All day

Suggested Visit Duration: 1 hour

Transportation: Take the Powell-Hyde Cable Car Line to Lombard St & Hyde St station

Location: Lombard Street, San Francisco, California


4. Palace of Fine Arts—a popular spot for locals taking wedding photos


Highlights: Architecture resembling ancient Roman ruins


The American Palace of Fine Arts was constructed in 1915, originally built for the Panama-Pacific International Exposition; unfortunately it was abandoned after the exposition concluded. Until 1962, German-American architect Bernard Meybeck came to redesign and rebuild it. Through his meticulous craftsmanship, the Palace of Fine Arts in America became one of the attractions that amazed tourists. The architectural style here imitates ancient Roman ruins. Those who visit can see swans playing happily in the lake, old people sitting quietly on benches daydreaming, new couples taking wedding photos - a scene full of comfort and relaxation. Today, the Palace of Fine Arts has become a popular recreational spot for San Franciscans.


The Palace of Fine Arts

Originally built in 1915, the Palace of Fine Arts in San Francisco was constructed for Panama's "Panama-Pacific International Exposition," which attracted 18 million visitors at that time. However, it was abandoned after the exposition and not until 1962 when a famous German-American architect named Meybeck was commissioned to redesign and rebuild it. He specialized in detailed decoration, which is the origin of this Palace of Fine Arts' meticulously carved style. Today, the Palace of Fine Arts has become a popular recreational spot for San Franciscans, with swans and ducks swimming together in the pond, and the Palace of Fine Arts Theater is also located here - an excellent place to relax on holidays.


Tickets: Free

Opening Hours: All day

Suggested Visit Duration: 2-3 hours

Transportation: Take bus PresidiGO Crissy Field to Palace of Fine Arts Station

Location: 3301 Lyon St, San Francisco, CA 94123


5. Alcatraz Island - Filming Location for Movie "The Rock"


Highlights: Paradise for Adventurers, Offers Night Tours


Alcatraz Island is situated in San Francisco Bay and was a prison used by the former US government to imprison criminals. This prison has been well-preserved to this day and is open to tourists. Visitors can take ferries to reach the island, then tour while listening to audio introductions. The audio guides allow people to experience prison life rather than simply viewing it as scenery. Meanwhile, Alcatraz was also a filming location for the movie "The Rock." For adventure enthusiasts, there are night tours available to make your visit more exciting! However, daily spots are limited, so remember to book tickets in advance!


Alcatraz

Alcatraz is a small rocky island covering about 22 acres. This was formerly the place where the U.S. federal government imprisoned serious criminals. Notorious inmates who were held here included Chicago's "Godfather" Capone, "Birdman" Stroud—a ruthless killer yet also gifted—and the cold-blooded "Machine Gun" killer Kelly. Prisoners could only gaze at San Francisco through the iron window bars of the prison, where they endured another cruel form of psychological punishment. Today, the entire island preserves its original prison atmosphere as much as possible. There are still facilities on the island such as a bird sanctuary, museum, and bookstore. In the shops on the island, memoirs written by former inmates whose sentences were completed are sold; all claim it was an unforgettable painful experience. Despite its spooky history, this island also serves as a refuge for wildlife. Many species of birds make their homes here, making it a good place for visitors to relax and reflect.


Opening Hours: Pier 33 has daytime ferries between 8:45-15:20 daily, approximately one boat every 30 minutes to Alcatraz Island. From Alcatraz Island back to Pier 33, about one boat every 30 minutes between 9:55-18:30. Night ferries have a total of 2 boats; departure times from Pier 33 are 17:55 and 18:30, while departure times from Alcatraz Island are 20:40 and 21:25. Ferry operating hours may vary slightly, so it is recommended to check the official website for specific departure times to avoid missing your trip.

Suggested Visit Time: Half day

Transportation: Take the ferry from Pier 33

Location: Alcatraz Island, San Francisco, CA 94133


6. Golden Gate Park - 20% larger than New York's Central Park


Highlights: One of the world's largest artificial parks; suitable for family leisure travel


Golden Gate Park is one of the world's largest artificial parks, extending three miles west from Stanyan Street to the Pacific Ocean, 20% larger than New York's Central Park.

The park spans 53 streets and consists of more than ten small parks, each with unique charm, making it easy for visitors to get lost while exploring.

Inside the park are various entertainment venues such as golf courses, tennis courts, children's playgrounds, museums, art galleries, and botanical gardens. Among these are three highly popular city museums—the California Science Center, Asian Art Museum, and M.H. de Young Memorial. It is an ideal destination for family leisure travel.

On holidays, you can even see many artists auctioning their works here. Overall, it's a wonderful place to relax and perfect for families on vacation.


Golden Gate Park

Golden Gate Park stretches impressively from the Pacific Ocean coastline with waves crashing against the shore all the way into downtown San Francisco. The park spans 53 streets and is composed of more than ten small parks, each with unique charm. It is the world's largest man-made park.

While exploring, visitors may find themselves getting lost at any time. Inside are various attractions including golf courses, tennis courts, children's playgrounds, museums, art galleries, and botanical gardens. Among these are three popular city museums—the California Science Center, Asian Art Museum, and M.H. de Young Memorial.

It is an ideal destination for family leisure travel.


Tickets: Free admission to enter the park; special attractions require additional payment—for example, boat rentals per hour cost 13-17 dollars, Surrey-style horse-drawn carriages cost 20 dollars, bicycles cost 8 dollars, and inline skates cost 6 dollars.

Opening Hours: Open all day

Suggested Visit Duration: One day

Transportation: Take buses 21 or 33 to Stanyan St&

Get off at Hayes Street Station

Attraction Location: Golden Gate Park, San Francisco, CA, United States


7. Twin Peaks — Overlooking San Francisco from a bird's-eye view


Highlights: 360-degree panoramic view of the entire city, natural hill preserved within San Francisco.


Twin Peaks in San Francisco are two hills standing side by side. Early Spanish immigrants nicknamed them "Indian maiden's breasts" due to their shape. If you drive from downtown, you can reach Twin Peaks quickly. Standing on the observation deck of Twin Peaks, you can enjoy a 360-degree panoramic view of San Francisco's beautiful scenery. Therefore, I highly recommend visiting during sunset or at night—the twilight views and night scenes offer unique charm~


Twin Peaks

Twin Peaks are two hills with an altitude of approximately 270 meters. Named "Indian maiden's breasts" by early Spanish immigrants due to their shape, they represent the only natural hill remaining within San Francisco.

Climbing Twin Peaks is quite a challenging experience. You need to wear multiple layers of clothing to protect against strong winds and chilly fog, while also making the effort to climb up steep slopes. However, the hardship is worth it - on clear days, you can overlook the entire cityscape of San Francisco, and even see the red Golden Gate Bridge beside San Francisco Bay. It's also an excellent place for enjoying night views!


Admission: Free

Opening Hours: All day

Recommended Visit Duration: 2 hours

Transportation: Take Bus Line 37 to Twin Peaks Station.

Location: Christmas Tree Point Rd, San Francisco, CA, 94114


8. California Academy of Sciences - One of the world's rare quadruple museums


Highlights: Among the top 10 natural history museums globally and a leading center for modern natural sciences research


The California Academy of Sciences, located within Golden Gate Park, is one of the few in the world that combines an aquarium, planetarium, natural history museum, and four-story rainforest. It also serves as a multifaceted museum.

There are solar panels on top of the roof for generating electricity, and the soil serves as a natural insulator. Most of the walls are made of glass to absorb natural light. When visiting the rainforest on the fourth floor, visitors can take a glass elevator from above down to the bottom level, where they can observe small fish swimming around overhead through an acrylic tunnel~ At the same time, visitors can also watch scientists at work through glass partitions and ask questions anytime if needed—truly a paradise for nature science enthusiasts!


California Science Museum

The California Science Museum is America's first comprehensive museum integrating sea, land, and air biology with education, exhibitions, and research. It is also one of the top 10 natural history museums globally and a leading international center for modern natural science research. Inside are Steinhart Aquarium, Morrison Planetarium (the world's largest all-digital planetarium), Kimball Natural History Museum, and a tropical rainforest. At the planetarium, visitors can experience an awe-inspiring journey through the universe; in the aquarium, they can explore underwater coral reef worlds home to over 2,000 fish species. There is also the world's largest breathing ecological living roof. Through glass partitions, you can watch scientists at work and ask questions freely. You can even go directly to the natural sciences center on the second floor to seek answers from the scientists.


Tickets: $29.95/adult, $19.95/child

Opening hours: Monday to Saturday (9:30-17:00), Sunday (11:00-17:00)

Recommended visit time: Half a day

Transportation: Take bus route 44 or streetcar line N to the 9th Ave & Irving St station

Location: 55 Concourse Drive, Golden Gate Park, San Francisco, CA, 94118


9. Haight Ashbury Street - Hippie Culture Sanctuary


Highlights: Birthplace of the hippie movement, colorful graffiti


At Golden Gate Park's eastern entrance lies Haight Street (or Haite Street as written), which in the 1960s was once a dreamland for young people worldwide seeking simple beauty. Herds of hippies gathered on Golden Gate Park's lawns.

Today's Haight Street features all kinds of unique specialty shops. One famous sight is the "Soul Man Socks" sign - the mannequin legs with socks appearing to fall out of a second-floor window. When you go inside, you'll discover it's simply a secondhand clothing store. On Haight Street, you can see many old houses wrapped in exaggerated, personality-filled graffiti and various signs. The entire neighborhood exudes hippie culture~


Haight-Ashbury District


10. Streetcars - A Fantastic Way to Tour San Francisco


Highlight: Vintage transportation method


Streetcars are located in downtown and are a famous vintage public transport system in San Francisco, very popular among locals and tourists. They have been running on San Francisco's hills and valleys since 1873, becoming a unique city scenic view. Currently there are only three routes: Powell-Hyde, Powell-Mason, and California Street. The F-line streetcar runs between Market Street and Fisherman's Wharf. Riding a streetcar feels like returning to San Francisco of a century ago. Tickets can be purchased at many ticket counters, or you can buy tickets from the conductor after boarding.

And at Powell/Market, the starting station of both the Powell-Mason and Powell-Hyde lines, one can also see an interesting scene of people manually pulling up-and-down cars to turn them around.


Fare: Single trip $7/person


II. Two-Day San Francisco Itinerary


DAY 1: Palace of Fine Arts → Nine Curves Flower Street → Union Square → Golden Gate Park → Twin Peaks


Day one has many attractions to visit, with a total distance of 11 kilometers. Walking is inconvenient, so here I recommend taking a private car tour. In the morning, first visit the Palace of Fine Arts and Nine Curves Flower Street, which really tests driving skills. Then head to Union Square in downtown for some shopping. After lunch, return to rest. In the afternoon, go visit Golden Gate Park. Since Golden Gate Park is quite large, you should allow extra time for your visit. Finally, after dinner in the evening, go to Twin Peaks to appreciate either the sunset or San Francisco's night view.


DAY 2: Alcatraz Island → Fisherman's Wharf → Golden Gate Bridge


Day two's itinerary involves taking a boat, so more time is spent on transportation. Therefore, the sightseeing pace is quite relaxed.

Alcatraz Island is best visited in the morning or evening. Today's recommended route is for the morning. Start by taking a ferry from Pier 33 to Alcatraz Island, then after visiting, take the return ferry back to Fisherman's Wharf restaurant for lunch. After finishing lunch and resting a bit, you can explore some classic spots around Fisherman's Wharf, and finally in the late afternoon after dinner, head to Golden Gate Bridge to feel the sea breeze~ enjoy the night view~


These attractions are quite scattered. If you want to visit them all at once, you may be rushing through time, especially for first-time visitors to San Francisco who have different languages and aren't familiar with maps or routes, it can be quite troublesome.

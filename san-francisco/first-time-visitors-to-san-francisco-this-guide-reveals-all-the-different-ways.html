          1. For Movie Fans


              Over 100 films have been shot here. So you can experience this city with the feeling of watching a movie, walking on streets where the maglev train from "Big Hero 6" drove through, imagining yourself traveling into an animated world. Head to the redwood forests from "Dawn of the Planet of the Apes," and like Caesar, move through millennia-old giant redwoods.

Its destruction is extremely visual. To achieve shocking visual effects, in films like "San Andreas," "Aquaman," "Superman," and "The Abyss," the Golden Gate Bridge has been destroyed to varying degrees.


② Redwood Park | Rise of the Planet of the Apes

The film's location is Redwoods National Park north of the Golden Gate. The 120-meter tall Coast redwoods featured in the film are among the tallest plants in the world.

San Francisco Through the Lens of Cinema


③ Red and White Number Cruise Ship | "How Time Flies"

In a cruise ship traveling from the Golden Gate Bridge toward Devil's Island, Zhao Mo Sheng took photos of tourists to earn money; the famous Red and White Number cruise ship is where they were traveling.


Tips:

The entire experience features narration in 12 languages to help you learn about San Francisco's rich history. Along the way, admire the Golden Gate Bridge and the infamous Alcatraz Prison.

To avoid waiting in line for same-day tickets, you can purchase boat tickets online a day in advance to save queue time and allow more time to explore Alcatraz Island.

④ Palace of Fine Arts | "The Rock"

The touching scene where Sean Connery's Mason meets his daughter in a garden in "The Rock" — that garden is actually part of the Palace of Fine Arts. Many movies were filmed here, including "Ever After" (2001) and "Mrs. Doubtfire" (2006).

Film View of San Francisco

⑤ Alcatraz Island | "Escape from Alcatraz", "Birdman of Alcatraz"

Mentioning San Francisco's Alcatraz Island, you'll immediately think of the film "The Rock," where this island serves as a primary setting. The two main characters have an iconic battle scene against rebels in this location.

keyword: San Francisco attractions and films

⑥ Pan American Pyramid | "The Pursuit of Happyness"

The skyscraper Pan American Pyramid that appears in the movie is a pyramid-shaped building with 48 floors. It was once the tallest building in San Francisco and west of Chicago in the United States, and now has been replaced by the SALESFORCE Tower.


Tips:

Alcatraz is not free entry; tickets are required to visit the island. When you plan your trip, be sure to book tickets in advance~ If you buy tickets on-site, it often results in no tickets being available.


San Francisco Through Movie Cameras


⑦ Crissy Field Park | "Contact" and "Vertigo"

In "Contact", the male lead Matt Damon lives in San Francisco and possesses psychokinetic abilities. One scene featuring a conversation with his friend was filmed at Crissy Field Park in San Francisco.


⑧ Union Square | "Zodiac"

Union Square is a popular meeting spot for dates in San Francisco, as well as a shopping district. On one side of the square stands a building with red and white exterior colors and classical floral railings. This is a top-tier Union Square Hotel themed around movies. The hotel lobby and room furnishings, furniture, and wall decorations are all inspired by films. Its classical-style architectural decoration is also worth stopping to admire.


San Francisco Through Movie Lenses


9 Nob Hill | "Possession"

Nob Hill locals also call it "The Top." This neighborhood has several streets with many beautiful old houses. Many films have been filmed here. Marilyn Monroe's former home is in this area as well. Visitors can overlook the bay scenery from a high vantage point.

In the film "Bullitt," Steve McQueen's powerful car jumps over the hilltop and lands south of Market Street. On Lombard Street in Nob Hill, it is known as "the world's most winding street." Films like "Vertigo," "The Big Bounce," and "True World" were filmed here.


10 Bay Bridge | "The Graduate"

In the film, when Dustin Hoffman heads toward Berkeley, he takes a wrong turn and crosses this bridge.

II. Unlock Top 5 San Francisco Attractions, Lazy Travelers Can Get It All in One Click


Besides the Golden Gate Bridge, Nine-Turn Flower Street, and Fisherman's Wharf these regular attractions, why not go deep to explore niche influencer spots.


1. Street Murals, Experience Artistic Life


San Francisco street murals are basically concentrated in Mission Area.

The graffiti here is not random smudging, but a gathering place for artistic creation. The Mission district is home to many Latino residents, and many murals and floor tiles have obvious Mexican style. The totemic dancing skeletons with Mexican characteristics on the sidewalks are also very vivid - don't they look like prototypes from "Coco"!


San Francisco street manhole covers


2. Coit Tower 360° overlook of San Francisco


Coit Tower stands 64 meters high, not only serving as a commemorative landmark building, but also an excellent viewing spot to appreciate the city's beautiful scenery. After purchasing tickets at the gift shop on the first floor, you can take an elevator directly to the observation deck. Looking out from the windows, you can get a 360° panoramic view of San Francisco's beautiful cityscape, clearly seeing the Golden Gate Bridge, Bay Bridge, Fisherman's Wharf, CBD, and the vast Pacific Ocean...


Coit Tower


Mosaic art staircase, internet celebrity photo spot


The entire staircase is made with mosaic tiles, featuring various patterns including flowers, birds, stars, the sea, sun, moon, and more. The colors are vivid and very unique - it has become a popular photography destination.

You can navigate here using Google Maps. It's surrounded by residential buildings and has a very distinctive character. Arriving in the afternoon is ideal - at dusk, the scene becomes even more spectacular under the reflection of lights.


4. Napa Valley - enjoying local fine wines at wineries


There are over 400 wineries of various sizes here. You can ride the Napa Valley Wine Train or Napa Valley Wine Bus to tour famous wineries, taste local delicacies; sit in the vineyard and watch hot air balloons slowly ascend at sunset - a romantic sight; appreciate art works in small towns; or enjoy leisure time at the spa.


Tips:

Napa Valley hot air balloon rides are frequently affected by weather, so it's recommended to purchase tickets for featured activities in advance. Tickets can be rescheduled when encountering weather and other natural issues. Electronic tickets also help save queue time.


Napa Hot Air Balloon


5. Highway 17 - experiencing the world's most beautiful private road


Drive from San Francisco along Highway 1 for about an hour and a half, then you can reach the 17 Mile Coastline.

Entering the 17 Mile Drive requires paying a $10 toll fee. This is one of the finest sections of Highway 1. Besides the blue seas and clear skies, here you can find Pebble Beach Golf course - ranked number one in the world, where golf legend Woods achieved fame - along with various charming luxury mansions.


17 Mile Beach

V. San Francisco City Transportation


1. Self-driving is the most convenient:


In San Francisco, self-driving is recommended for sightseeing. Although public transportation in the city is quite developed, relying on public transportation here still has many shortcomings.

keyword: San Francisco dining and transportation guide

You can choose rental car companies such as Hertz, Avis, Alamo; picking up and returning vehicles is convenient, with most available directly at airport counters.


Car Rental Self-drive


Cable Car Special Experience:


Cable Cars are a must-ride mode of transportation for tourists. With the crisp clanging sound, it feels like returning to San Francisco during the Gold Rush era. To ride the cable car, you can board at either terminal station or any stop with a signpost. Fare: $6/adult.


San Francisco Cable Car


Tips:

Cable cars are divided into 3 routes:

1. Powell-Mason Line

2. Powell-Hyde Line

3. California Street Line


Metro is the cheapest:


San Francisco has six lines (J, K, L, M, N and T), operating from 4:30 AM to 1:30 AM the next day.

Taking Line F allows you to explore all of San Francisco. Line F starts at Fisherman's Wharf—San Francisco's most popular tourist attraction—and ends at Castro—the free-loving gay community. It passes through Embarcadero (over 40 piers) and Market Street—a shopping paradise. Line F has a vintage appearance, comfortable rides with leather seats, making it San Francisco's best sightseeing route. Uniform fare: $2.


San Francisco N Line Subway


5. Specialty Hotels Hidden in the City Center


1. Vintage Classic Hotel


Fairmont San Francisco

Address: 950 Mason St, San Francisco, CA 94108

Fairmont San Francisco is a hotel with 110 years of rich history. Walking through the Fairmont's corridors feels like strolling through a museum filled with memories. The castle-style exterior alone makes it classic, and this place is also an elegant social stage visited by many celebrities, even including presidents.


Fairmont Restaurant


2. Book-Themed Hotel


Rex Hotel

Address: 562 Sutter St, San Francisco, CA 94102

The hotel's name, Rex, honors Kenneth Rexroth—a prominent poet and translator associated with San Francisco's literary renaissance.

Hotel lobby features huge bookshelves, and Christmas trees are stacked from books - very creative.


Rex Hotel Bookshelf


3. Boutique Container Theme Hotel


Hotel Zephyr

Address: 250 Beach St, San Francisco, CA 94133

The hotel is located at Fisherman's Wharf. In design and decoration, it strives to be as close to nature as possible, using eco-friendly and recycled decorative materials, including recycled old metal, containers, and shipping crates. Most rooms include private open-air balconies. Looking outward from the guest rooms offers an excellent view of San Francisco Bay sunsets.

Six、7-Day San Francisco Experience Route Recommendations


Day 1: Concentrated tour of northern San Francisco. Morning seafood feast at Fisherman's Wharf, experiencing fishing port culture. Evening arrives at Golden Gate Bridge with sea breeze, admiring magnificent San Francisco night scenery.


Day2: Depart early in the morning to arrive at the Golden Gate Bridge and admire it through the fog. In the afternoon, climb Alcatraz Island to experience America's most famous prison with a night tour activity. On your way back in the evening, you can also enjoy San Francisco's night view from the ferry.


Day3: In the morning, visit the Palace of Fine Arts and Lombard Street, known for its challenging driving conditions. Enjoy beautiful cityscapes before heading to Chinatown for a hearty meal to experience Chinese culture abroad. In the evening, explore Union Square with its dense array of shops, restaurants, and theaters.


Day4: Morning visits to San Francisco's stunning public buildings - the City Hall and Painted Ladies, a rare Victorian architectural ensemble that survived major earthquakes. Afternoon relaxation in Golden Gate Park followed by panoramic views from Twin Peaks.


Day5: A scenic 90-minute drive along Highway 1 leads to the legendary 17-Mile Drive. Watch thousands of wild sea lions basking peacefully on coastal beaches.


Day6: Yosemite National Park sits in California's central Sierra Nevada mountains, reachable from Highway 1 in about 3.5 hours. Engage in forest hiking, observe ancient giant sequoias, and pursue diverse outdoor adventures including mountaineering, rock climbing, cycling, skiing, and river rafting.


Day7: In the morning depart from Yosemite National Park, about a 3.5-hour drive to reach Napa Valley, America's largest wine-producing region on the West Coast. You can try a wine train experience crossing through Napa Wine Valley. You could also try hot air balloon rides for panoramic views of Napa Valley, or sit under vineyard trellises at wineries tasting authentic local wines. Staying overnight in Napa Valley is also a good option, or you can drive back to San Francisco within about 1 hour.


Tips:

If time permits not too much, custom tours or multi-day trips are also excellent choices. Depart from nearby destinations and customize your perfect itinerary based on your own schedule.

VII. More Than Just San Francisco, Multiple Destinations Connected for Layered Experiences


1. San Francisco + Seattle Two-City Connection


If San Francisco isn't enough for you, you can try multi-day tours departing from San Francisco.

Experience the world's tallest plants at Redwood National and State Parks; pass through Rose City Portland to witness a million roses blooming simultaneously; finally arrive in Seattle to feel the cultural history of University of Washington. On your return, board the Coast Starlight train back to San Francisco. Along the same route, enjoy different scenery in two different ways. Coast Starlight Tips: Departing from Seattle, passing through Oakland near San Francisco, with Los Angeles as the final destination. The train arrives in Oakland around 8 AM and reaches Los Angeles by 9 PM, allowing you to spend an entire day on board while enjoying American West Coast scenery. This makes up for the regret of not being able to self-drive Highway 1. 1. The Coast Starlight operates daily with one round-trip per day. From Seattle to San Francisco, the train departs at 9:40 AM and arrives in Oakland near San Francisco at 8:35 AM the following day (on time). The return train from San Francisco to Seattle departs from Oakland at 9:39 PM and arrives in Seattle at 7:56 PM the next day (on time). It is recommended that you arrive at least one to two hours early to avoid ticket pickup issues or delays caused by queuing. For ticket options, you can purchase round-trip tickets or buy one-way tickets for specific segments based on your itinerary. The train's final destination is Los Angeles.

Only by visiting San Francisco can you truly understand America's West Coast.

Mention San Francisco, and you think of the "Beat Generation," the hippie revolution, the iconic Golden Gate Bridge, Silicon Valley where high-tech businesses cluster, and Twin Peaks from which you can see a full view of San Francisco. This city is surrounded by sea on three sides and mountains on one side, with over a hundred films filmed here. American-style streets and old buildings all make this romantic city easy to feel a sense of ease.

PLAN A: Waste a day in museums & bookstores


//


Come to San Francisco, and first experience the hippie and Beat Generation culture firsthand. You'd never have enough time wandering around bookstores and museums all day.


The gathering place of "Beat Generation" writers


CITY LIGHTS was founded by poet Lawrance Ferlinghetti and Peter D.Matin in 1953. It's a very famous bookstore in the Bay Area, one of only a few independent bookstores in America, and has now become a city landmark.

Since its founding, this place has become a literary meeting venue, simultaneously publishing and selling books including poetry, prose, novels, and more, featuring works by international and local writers, pushing the cultural movement of that time to new heights.


To date, CITY LIGHTS has published over 200 different book titles. The bookstore's own collection is also very rich, with world literature, art, and progressive movements being the most comprehensive categories. Because it combines a bookstore and publisher, as Ferlinghetti put it, "the public is invited to join the 'great conversation' between authors from all ages of ancient and modern times." City Lights Bookstore was designated by the San Francisco government as Historic Building No. 228, attracting many visitors who come to check it out, perhaps even purchasing a copy of Kerouac's "On the Road."


CITY LIGHTS Booksellers & Publishers


Address: 261 Columbus Avenue, San Francisco, CA 94133


Cats darting out from alleys give you more surprises than anything else


Mission district's Dog Eared Books, popular among many readers, and Alley Cat Books &

Gallery is an interesting comprehensive independent bookstore. This seemingly small bookstore surprises people like a cat suddenly darting out from an alleyway.


The "gallery" inside the bookstore displays works by local artists and provides a free space for various reading clubs, film screenings, workshops, and other events.


A large amount of second-hand or remaining old books, easter egg details filling the entire space, thought-provoking ornaments and paintings; also a small amount of published local literature, LGBT-related works, and all kinds of peculiar publications... Simply put, this is a place where literary people can be completely absorbed.


Alley Cat Books & Gallery


Address: 3036 24th Street, San Francisco, CA 94110


Art museum with "community" new functions


The San Francisco Museum of Modern Art (SFMOMA) was established in 1935 and is the first art museum on America's West Coast. In 2016, the museum collaborated with the Snøhetta architectural firm to design and construct an expansion project, adding two main entrances along the pedestrian walkway surrounding the original building and on Howard Streebetter, giving this historic museum a more open "community-type" art space.


The new San Francisco Museum of Modern Art spans approximately 43000 square meters. Its east façade consists of over 700 composite fiberglass panels, with sunlight refracting and moving from different angles, resembling the sea mist of the San Francisco Bay Area. This expansion primarily adds public areas beyond the main exhibition spaces, attracting more visitors to communicate and exchange at the museum, enhancing its social functions.


At its founding, Albert M. Bender's 36 permanent collection artworks he donated remain in the museum. According to records, before his death in 1941, he gave over 1100 pieces of art to the museum in total. General adult tickets are $25, teenagers aged 19-24 are $19, members and those under 18 enter free, additionally if you have San Francisco CityPASS* you'll receive a 45% discount.


San Francisco Museum of Modern Art


Address: 151 Third St, San Franciso, CA 94103


Preserves a remarkably comprehensive collection of American art history


The de Young Museum was established in 1895 in San Francisco's Golden Gate Park. As one of the Three District landmarks, the de Young Museum attracts countless visitors with its significant cultural status and distinctive architecture.


The de Young Museum was rebuilt and completed in 2005. The main building is an inverted trapezoid wrapped in metal, a 44-meter-high tower. From the top of the tower, through unobstructed 360-degree glass windows, you can see the music pavilion at Golden Gate Park and enjoy views of the Golden Gate and Marin Headlands. Additionally, the exterior of the museum is clad with 15,154.2 square meters of copper plates that will eventually oxidize into a greenish hue. Both the outdoor park and the plantings in the courtyard make the entire museum appear lush and green.


Inside are exhibits of American art from the 17th century to present, international contemporary art, textiles, and fashion, among others. Permanent exhibitions include paintings by native American artists, artworks from Africa, Pacific Islands, and Native Americans in America, as well as works by internationally renowned contemporary artists. These collections make the de Young Museum a major resource for cultural research and study in this field. The de Young Museum is one of the top art museums in the western United States with one of the most complete collections of American art, and it is also one of the ten largest museums nationwide that comprehensively presents non-Indigenous American art history.


de Young museum

Address: 50 Hagiwara Tea Garden Drive, San Francisco, CA 94118

PLAN B Experience authentic retro culture


//


When talking about the clothing style of San Francisco, you cannot mention Vintage culture.

As the birthplace of cowboys, you can experience the most authentic retro style here. Since you're already here, of course you should visit this vintage collection store.


Retro Pilgrimage Destination


The extremely popular UNIONMADE offers a complete selection of American vintage and recreated workwear. It's one of the hottest specialty men's clothing stores in America, also serving as a comfortable integrated store. Within less than 1 year since opening, UNIONMADE made GQ magazine's list of top 10 men's specialty stores nationwide.


Wooden racks are filled with everything casual fashion enthusiasts desire, including twill and wax canvas jackets, nautical striped T-shirts, Heritage boots, vintage jeans, plaid and striped shirts - all selected for superior craftsmanship and quality.


UNIONMADE also collaborates with Alden, where you can find many exclusive leather boot styles. Beyond that, there are complete home goods sets and Vintage books available for purchase. The Japanese super-popular retro style magazine Free & Easy can be found here. Not only can you discover many treasures, but you can also experience authentic Vintage culture. It's said UNIONMADE has two seasonal clearance sales per year - students wanting to acquire Filson or LVC items should definitely not miss this opportunity.


UNIONMADE


Address: 493 Sanchez St, San Francisco, CA 94114


A shopping paradise for vintage lovers


This always-impressive hippie street never disappoints, and vintage lovers will definitely not go wrong coming here. Highly recommended is Decades of Fashion - the vast space features magnificent European palace-style decor, with countless old items catching your eye. From hats to suits, shoes to accessories - everything is available, like a retro exhibition, making visitors unsure where to start.


Timeless printed dresses are an essential combat piece in every woman's wardrobe. The fitted cut accentuates a graceful figure, while the vintage print exudes unique charm. Moving to the accessories section within the store, selecting a vintage brooch as a complementary detail serves as the perfect finishing touch for your ensemble.


With over 1000 items here, patience is essential in searching - you're guaranteed to discover several pieces that become your true favorites.

These designs remain remarkably stylish today, radiating distinctive vintage allure as one-of-a-kind treasures.

Decades of Fashion


Address: San Francisco 1653 Haight St, San Francisco, CA 94117


A Laundry with Coffee


The café Laundré is actually a self-service laundry. To reduce the time people spend waiting for their clothes to be washed and to attract more customers during business hours, Arianna Roviello designed an open-style café in the main space of the store, allowing customers to continue working while waiting for their wash or meet with friends.


The café space is spacious and simple. The outer walls are replaced with windows. Customer seating runs along the windows and a central bar counter, while the remaining areas are left open, intentionally creating a sense of multiple possibilities and flexible functions.


Laundré

Address: 2401 Mission St, San Francisco, CA 94110

PLAN C These Delights Should Not Be Missed


//

With Michelin three-star restaurants and highly photographable, delicious viral bread shops, after walking around this not-so-large city for a day, one must properly soothe their stomach.

A food factory selling desserts, smoked meats, and coffee


Elisabeth Prueitt and Chad Robertson, a couple who originally operated a small bakery called Tartine in San Francisco, had to expand their business due to its success. This led to the creation of this food factory that sells bread, pizza, bacon, coffee, desserts, and ice cream all at once.


Tartine Manufactory opened in 2016, located in a "warehouse" building converted from a laundry factory, covering an area of 557 square meters. The design firm Commune drew inspiration from Danish cafes and Japanese tea rooms. During the renovation process, materials such as rusty metal, Isamu Noguchi lamps, and western red cedar wood were extensively used, perfectly complementing the warehouse's original structure.


The open-concept bakery, pizza market, bacon restaurant, cafe, and ice cream shop are all interconnected. Someone who originally just wanted to buy bread can enter from the bakery and end up at the bacon restaurant for drinks. In this large food factory, one-third is dining area, while the remaining two-thirds of the space is used to showcase the food production process. By the way, the bread that was only available until 4:30 PM at Tartine is now served all day.


Tartine Manufactory


Address: 595 Alabama St, San Francisco, CA 94110


First Michelin Three-Star Restaurant on the West Coast


Located in a building constructed in 1900 at Napa Valley winery, The French Laundry is considered one of the most famous French restaurants across America. Chef Thomas Keller purchased this courtyard after visiting it in 1994, retained its original "French Laundry" name, and resolved to transform it into an exquisite restaurant. With a pastoral atmosphere inherent to its location in Napa Valley, although its exterior appears simple and the dining area is not large, it gradually established itself on the West Coast and throughout America thanks to its excellent menu quality, careful ingredient selection, and the chef's culinary skills.


The restaurant sources nearly all ingredients from their own garden directly across, continuing California's principle of high standards for freshness in dishes. Precisely because of this, chefs hold daily meetings to research menus. The menus follow a TASTING MENU format, with chefs deciding what you eat, so each visit brings different surprises to guests. From appetizers to desserts, under the renowned chefs of The French Laundry, every dish resembles a work of art.

The salmon cone is considered a signature dish of the restaurant, combining rich and dense salmon mousse with scallions, red onions, and fresh cream, topped with a crispy outer pastry skin. The portion may be small but leaves you wanting more.


The restaurant has won numerous awards and is one of the few in San Francisco to have been awarded three Michelin stars for consecutive years. The French Laundry has a beautiful garden where guests can relax and chat, so even without reservations, visitors will come to stroll around.


The French Laundry


Address: 6640 Washington Street, Yountville, CA 94599


You need to book 90 days in advance.


Opened in 2012, it was awarded a Michelin star in its first year. Saison is located in the SOMA district of San Francisco, transformed from a former power plant. The over-century-old red brick exterior combined with modern industrial interior design makes it difficult to associate with Asian cuisine. Saison's head chef and partner graduated from the French Culinary Institute. Although it is an American-style restaurant, as its name suggests, saison means season in French, so the menu selects different ingredients according to seasonal changes.


Not only that, but confident in their cooking process and ingredient quality, Saison has also established a substantial open kitchen in the dining area where you can see the chef team, led by Head Chef Joshua Skenes, completing new dishes in an orderly and planned manner. Of course, being able to maintain Michelin three-star standards for several consecutive years is unquestionable evidence of Saison's dish quality. Following the concept of "fire," most dishes are grilled at the hearth, with both creativity and visual appeal on point. Those who want to experience this need to book 90 days in advance.


SAISON


Address: 178 Townsend Street, San Francisco, CA 94107


All about the outdoor scenery


This bar lounge with its iconic views, located on the top floor of the Marriott Hotel, serves as an ideal destination for both leisure and business socializing. The View Lounge's design effortlessly captivates all guests; a semi-circular large floor-to-ceiling window occupies the entire wall area, while the high ceiling space and curved bar counter design add to the romantic atmosphere.


In this environment, guests can enjoy freshly mixed cocktails and California-style exquisite simple meals while enjoying the views of San Francisco;

Charming sunset evenings, the city's night illuminated with twinkling lights - hills, bridges, and streets all in view. However, if you want a seat by the window, you have to beat out hotel guests and tourists who've come for its reputation; mars seriously warns: don't forget to book early.


The View Lounge


Address: 780 Mission Street, San Francisco, CA 94103


A century-old house hiding elegance and sophistication


The Bacchus Group, known for its word-of-mouth favorites Spruce and Village Pub's Bacchus, has added another distinguished member to its portfolio. The Saratoga, this stylish cocktail bar, is housed in an old building in the Tenderloin district, constructed in 1907. Before undergoing this extensive renovation, it also served as a base for hotels and financial institutions; after years of weathering time, the exterior bears scars from history.


The arrival of The Saratoga has brought new colors to the nearby neighborhood. The interior design blends classic and modern styles, featuring a circular glass chandelier designed by Coup D'Etat and Magnus Schevene that spans two floors and hangs straight down;

Deep gray tones create a calm and elegant atmosphere, emphasizing the privacy of the space.


Behind the bartender stands a floor-to-ceiling wine rack displaying fine wines from various countries. The collection is breathtakingly impressive, and it's truly no easy task for bartenders to memorize where each bottle is located. For those seeking a tranquil and comfortable evening, visiting here is Mars' sincere recommendation.


The Saratoga

Address: 1000 Larkin Street, San Francisco, CA 94109

Excellent boutique hotel to stay at


//


Before every trip, you may also find yourself, like Mars, quite conflicted about where to stay. Here are our recommendations for boutique hotels.


Boutique hotel with exhibitions to visit


The Tilden Hotel, a boutique hotel located in downtown San Francisco, is more specifically situated between Union Square and Tenderloin, combining the fresh energy of both areas with the prosperity of the older community. This neighborhood boasts numerous artist studios, galleries, restaurants, bars, and more. Nearby attractions include the Marrakech Magic Theater, Grady Memorial Church, and Shannon Street Murals, all very accessible.


The hotel's predecessor was the Twain Hotel, built in 1928. After renovation by New York architecture studio Studio Tack and being renamed Tilden Hotel, it reopened. The current hotel features simple yet exquisite design with natural-textured marble, warm-toned wood, large windows, and an open floor plan filled with California sunshine. Contemporary artworks with vibrant colors adorn the walls.


The hotel has a total of 118 guest rooms. In addition to the restaurant, it features an open-to-the-public café and bar. Furthermore, to support local San Francisco artists, the hotel has established a dedicated planning department that regularly hosts events and small exhibitions.


Tilden Hotel - Tilden Hotel, San Francisco


Address: 345 Taylor St, San Francisco, CA 94102


A Modern Hotel Within the Century-Old Iron Building


The San Francisco Proper Hotel is nestled within a historic iron building on Market Street, offering an exceptional urban getaway experience. Renowned interior designer Kelly Wearstler has crafted its contemporary aesthetic, seamlessly bridging the city's past, present, and future through innovative design.


From early modernism to the aesthetic era of Cubism, then to the Art Deco artistic period, hotel designs beautifully blend the romantic warmth of old times with the vibrant energy of new eras.


Proper Hotel features 131 guest rooms, providing a stay experience infused with artistic design. Under the influence of rich colors, diverse textures, and natural materials, the original historic building has been revitalized to renewed glory.


San Francisco Proper Hotel

Address: 45 McAllister St., 1100 Market Street, San Francisco, CA 94102

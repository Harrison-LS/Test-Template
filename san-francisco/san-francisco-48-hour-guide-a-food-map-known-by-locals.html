San Francisco 48-Hour Guide: A Food Map Known by Locals

2006, on my first visit to San Francisco, I fell in love with The Golden City that has both sunshine and fog; since then I've come every year for a few weeks to try out the local restaurants; 2016, I moved to San Francisco and worked at Uber; today, I'm sharing my private "Weekend Food Tour List" through short videos. Next time you visit San Francisco, why not take advantage of good weather and embark on a delicious 48-hour exploration~


A 3-minute short film takes you to quickly eat your way through San Francisco~


Donuts + coffee is a classic American breakfast pairing. Originally simple donuts, with unique and particular flavors, become a microcosm of American middle-class life. Dynamo means an energetic person; the shop lives up to its name - this small shop is the engine for nearby office workers. Sugar-filled handmade donuts paired with rich coffee = an energized morning.


A $1 donut meant for dogs


Classic flavors

Bacon Maple Apple flavor

Contains apple-fried dough rings, paired with crispy bacon and delicate maple syrup. After finishing, be sure to go on a brisk walk to burn off those calories.

Spiced Chocolate is also worth trying, the spicy chocolate has a rich taste and lingering aftertaste.

If you have limited time in San Francisco but want to experience a variety of culinary delights, Ferry Building is an excellent choice. Many popular shops have branches here. My favorite is the mushroom and vegetable flavored Empanada (South American pastry).


Frog Hollow Farm sources all ingredients from its own organic farm, and all jams and toppings are homemade.

This market, spanning 65,000 square feet, is located within a central plaza. It's a unique marketplace that brings together agricultural products from the Bay Area and famous specialty food vendors.

Open every day. High-quality restaurants and shops form a complete feast of delicacies.


Every day at noon, office workers at Ferry Building line up to buy Vietnamese rice rolls and Banh Mi sandwiches.


Out the Door is a sister restaurant to The Slanted Door, California's first famous Vietnamese restaurant. Its cuisine is more casual. Both restaurants are located at Ferry Building, with sunshine, sea water, and the bridge right outside - visually delightful food.


The Slanted Door means "slanted door," because the designer created a unique slanting line. And Out the Door simply means "outside the door." Since it serves Vietnamese street food, that's exactly what it's called - got it?


Isn't a $3 bun and porridge a standard Chinese breakfast meal?

Humphry Slocombe's name is somewhat tricky to pronounce, derived from the main characters' names in the British comedy "Are You Being Served?" There are shops at both Ferry Building by the waterfront and Mission, each matching their prestigious names in craftsmanship.


Rated as one of America's top five ice cream shops, they're soon opening an ice cream truck.

Bi-Rite is a small grocery store with 70 years of history.

Most people know Bi-Rite for their ice cream. Bi-Rite Creamery has been named one of America's best ice cream shops by numerous major lists multiple times. The shop is small in size, but its rich, creamy ice cream attracts countless people who queue up daily to taste it. Beyond the ice cream, every item in the store exudes a passion for life and a pursuit of quality.

Oboro tofu is Eiji's signature dish. Unlike regular ready-made tofu, it arrives in a whole pot of tofu and soy milk, paired with an assortment plate containing minced ginger, perilla leaves, chopped scallions, shredded katsuobushi (bonito flakes), sesame seeds, grated pink radish, and soy sauce.

When tasting cloud tofu, do not rush to add ingredients. First, try the original flavor of the tender and smooth tofu to experience the rich bean fragrance.


Besides the signature cloud tofu, Eiji's soba noodles (soba) and mochi are also excellent. The handmade mochi has a rough exterior but surprises inside, with an extremely thin skin that melts in your mouth. Paired with juicy strawberries, it's fresh and natural.


Eiji (Closed on Mondays)

Eiji


Blue Bottle Coffee from Oakland can be considered one of the pioneers of third-wave independent coffee.


This Blue Bottle at the corner deep inside Mint Plaza is clean and minimalist, creating a striking contrast with the surrounding limestone buildings. At first glance, an unassuming Blue Bottle sign catches your eye—square-shaped with blue trim. It's understated yet not subtle; those who understand can tell by quickly scanning the coffee machines on the counter, siphon equipment, stylishly dressed baristas, and the long queue at the entrance that this shop holds its weight.

This coffee shop uses handmade equipment to craft coffee. Their most famous offering is the siphon-style coffee that's typically only available in Tokyo. Their morel water-poached eggs and European-style sandwiches are also highly loved.


Greek gyros is actually a wrap, using Pita bread rolled with slices/skewers of grilled meat, tomato, onion, fries, and yogurt-cucumber sauce. It's also convenient to take away~

Mama Ji's is a Chinese home-style restaurant outside Chinatown. The owner lady's hometown is in Sichuan, and most of their signature dishes have traditional Sichuan flavors. The owner is from Europe and enjoys craft beer. They've blended their respective cultures into this Chinese restaurant, so you'll see pairings like Sichuan cuisine + Austrian craft beer.

Sightglass Coffee is a cafe invested in by Twitter's founder. With its convenient location plus a design aesthetic that flows from inside to outside, this has become a favorite coffee shop among Airbnb and Pinterest employees.


The summer's most popular item is undoubtedly affogato: A piece of soft vanilla or chocolate ice cream paired with espresso—the melting sweetness gives way to a hint of tart coffee bitterness. Cold mixed with warmth, indescribably wonderful.

Namu Gaji is a modern Korean restaurant where the owners come from three different fields - chef, engineer, and musician. The Osaka-style okonomiyaki tastes good too, with bonito flakes and seaweed topped with mayonnaise, crispy and fragrant.

Traditional Italian pizza with classic flavors like Margherita (tomato, mozzarella cheese and basil), 4 Formaggi (four cheeses) etc., yet it has won over many customers because of the thin, crispy but chewy crust.

Another major feature is that there's a sunny-side-up egg with a runny yolk in the center of the pizza.

How to explore San Francisco—inside and out? This guide has the answers.

When thinking of San Francisco, what comes to mind are its iconic Golden Gate Bridge, the cable cars rolling slowly, beautiful beaches...

But San Francisco's scenic beauty extends far beyond these! Fisherman's Wharf, Lombard Street, Alcatraz Island, Union Square and more sparkle like stars within the city; Yosemite National Park that often appears on Apple wallpapers, Napa Valley loved by literary youth and more beckon visitors outside the city.

Inside and outside the city, countless beautiful sights drive people to enthusiastically support them.


I. Only having checked in at these 11 places can be considered truly exploring San Francisco's urban area


Apart from the well-known Golden Gate Bridge, San Francisco has numerous attractions within its urban area~


Golden Gate Bridge - If you haven't photographed it, have you really been to San Francisco?


The Golden Gate Bridge (Golden Gate Bridge) is a symbol of San Francisco and even California. Blue waters reflecting against the orange-red bridge structure, the beauty of the bay area also owes much to it.


The bridge is frequently featured in Hollywood movies. Typically, it appears in disaster films such as "San Andreas," "Dawn of Planet of the Apes," "X-Men" and more, being destroyed time and again...


Walking or cycling across the bridge while feeling the sea breeze is the best way to appreciate this magnificent structure. Walkways are specifically designated on both sides, with bicycle access allowed on the western side.


There are many viewing points for the bridge, and everyone can choose several based on their route:

The Golden Gate Bridge Pavilion at the southern end of the bridge has ample parking space, with mountains as a backdrop - perfect for morning photography. It also houses visitor centers, educational exhibits and more.

The Vista Point on the north side of the bridge is an excellent location to view the Golden Gate Bridge from above (right after getting off the bridge). Morning photography has good lighting, and you can also enjoy city views.

Fort Point at the southeastern area below the south side of the bridge is a great spot to view the bridge from below, located under the bridge with good morning lighting.

The Marin Headlands on the west of the north side have many parking spots along the way, allowing you to see the Golden Gate Bridge from a distance.

Battery Spencer overlook at the western edge of the other side of the bridge is also excellent.

If you want to drive onto the bridge, take US101 highway or SR1.


To witness the spectacular scene of fog enveloping the Golden Gate, visit early in the morning when you're most likely to see this phenomenon.


The most popular activity around the Golden Gate Bridge is a maritime tour aboard a boat. Viewing the bridge from the water offers a different and rewarding experience~


You can also choose different routes. Exploring both the Golden Gate Bridge and Bay Bridge while visiting will let you see enough of San Francisco's bay area~


Golden Gate Bridge

·Built with over 100,000 tons of steel, this orange bridge spans San Francisco Bay and the Golden Gate Strait. It is grand and magnificent as a symbol of San Francisco.

  • During the Gold Rush era, it was named because it resembled a gateway to gold mines. The viewing point on the north side is the best place to enjoy the full panorama of the bridge.
  • The bridge frequently appears in many major American films such as "Rise of the Planet of the Apes" and "X-Men".
  • Dedicated walkways are set up on both sides. Walking across the bridge accompanied by sea breezes, or cycling back and forth are both enjoyable experiences.


    • Fisherman's Wharf - The liveliest and most "delicious" pier

    Fisherman's Wharf roughly covers the area from Ghirardelli Square to Pier 35. The landmark of Fisherman's Wharf is a round sign featuring a big crab. When you find this, you've arrived at Fisherman's Wharf.


    A dazzling array of shops, bizarre and amusing wax museum, uniquely flavored restaurants, and exciting street performances are enough to give you great fun.

    Certainly when coming here you cannot miss the famous sourdough bread. It is both healthy and natural, using traditional crafting methods, being a food that must be tasted if coming to San Francisco. The famous old shop Boudin Bakery here is very well-known, recommended to try~

    As a wharf, the seafood here can surely satisfy your appetite completely. If you are interested in seafood and able to get up early, go to Pier 47 at dawn. Watch the lively scene of fishermen unloading catches, get first-hand seafood!


    Under no circumstances should you miss Pier 39, it has great fame and rich visiting content, being the essence of Fisherman's Wharf.

    Moreover, this is also the habitat of sea lions. Before even walking to the seaside, you can hear the coming and going calls of sea lions. After getting closer, you will see on the floating platforms under the wharf are lazy sea lions sunbathing, giving you a chance for close contact with them.

    San Francisco Fisherman's Wharf

    ·Fisherman's Wharf is well-known throughout San Francisco, the area extending from Ghirardelli Square to Pier 35.

    ·You can see all kinds of shops, restaurants, and street performances at the busiest Pier 39, and enjoy various views in the distance including Alcatraz Island, Golden Gate Bridge, and Bay Bridge.

    ·At Pier 39 you can see numerous sea lions lazily lying on rafts sunbathing and taking naps.

    ·Between November each year and June of the following year is the best season to enjoy seafood. At this time if you come to Fisherman's Wharf, you can eat excellent Dungeness crab.

    ·For friends who love shopping, Fisherman's Wharf not only allows you to see all kinds of specialty products, but also buy souvenirs with local characteristics.


    Nine Curved Flower Street - the street least friendly to new drivers


    Lombard Street is actually a section of Nine-Bend Flower Street, located between Hyde Street (higher elevation) and Leavenworth Street (lower elevation). This road is quite steep, but this short stretch has been designed with eight hairpin turns. The slope combined with the Z-shaped winding road means cars can only travel downhill in one direction.


    Experienced drivers can come here to test their driving skills; for new drivers, hmm, it's more pleasant to stroll through it leisurely. The flower beds on both sides of the lanes are filled with roses, and residents living on both sides of the street also grow flowers and grass at their doorsteps. During June-July when flowering season arrives, from a distance it looks like a velvet embroidery hanging diagonally, extremely beautiful.

    From Hyde Street at the high point of Flower Street you can see far out to the bay and Alcatraz Island. To capture the complete nine-bend effect in photos, take pictures from Leavenworth Street at the lower elevation.


    Time Reference:

    1 hour

    Transportation:

    Take Powell-Hyde Cable Car Line to Lombard St & Hyde St station

    Tickets:

    Free

    Opening Hours:

    All day

    Location:

    Lombard Street, San Francisco, California


    Nine-Bend Flower Street

    • Official name is Lombard Street, it's the world's most curved street; from high up on Flower Street you can see far out to the Bay Bridge and Coit Tower.

    • The city government built flower beds specifically to prevent traffic accidents; when cars go there, speed must not exceed 5 miles per hour.

    · Within a very short street block, there are eight sharp turns, 40-degree slopes combined with Z-shaped winding roads that seriously test driving skills.

    · Rose gardens filled with roses line both sides of the lanes. When flowers bloom, from a distance they look like hanging velvet embroidery - breathtakingly beautiful.


    Palace of Fine Arts——gorgeous courtyard, great place to relax


    The Palace of Fine Arts actually has no indoor space; it is simply a very magnificent courtyard.

    Elegant carvings, grand colonnades, combined with fountain lakes and countless swans and ducks, create a romantic atmosphere. Today, the Palace of Fine Arts has become a popular leisure spot for San Francisco locals.


    Time reference:

    2-3 hours

    Transportation:

    Tickets:

    Opening hours:

    Open all day

    Location:

    3301 Lyon St, San Francisco, CA 94123


    American Palace of Fine Arts

    The Palace of Fine Arts in San Francisco was originally built in 1915, constructed for the Panama-Pacific International Exposition. It attracted 18 million visitors at that time but was abandoned after the exposition ended. It wasn't until 1962 that a famous German-American architect named Meyberg was commissioned to redesign and rebuild it. He specialized in detailed interior work, which is the origin of this palace's exquisitely carved style. Today, the Palace has become a popular recreational spot for San Francisco residents. Swans and ducks play together in the pond, and the Palace Theatre is also located here, making it an ideal place to relax on holidays.


    Alcatraz Island—though without actual demons, it's still incredibly thrilling


    Alcatraz Island was once a federal prison housing America's most notorious criminals. The facility remains remarkably preserved and now welcomes visitors. Dim lighting and chilly cells create an eerie atmosphere where audio guides explain the prison's architecture and history, allowing tourists to hear actual recordings of former inmates' voices.


    Beyond its reputation as a hauntingly grim prison, the island is steeped in supernatural folklore. Adventure enthusiasts can experience an even more thrilling visit through special night tours that delve deeper into its mysterious history.

    Daily visitor slots are strictly limited, so secure your reservation well in advance by clicking the link below to ensure you don't miss out on this unique experience.

    Alcatraz Island

    Alcatraz Island is a 22-acre rocky island that was once used by the U.S. federal government to incarcerate major criminals. Notorious inmates included Chicago mob boss Capone, the murderous yet gifted "Birdman" Stroud, and the cold-blooded "Machine Gun" killer Kelly. Prisoners could only look at the vibrant city of San Francisco through the prison bars, experiencing another form of cruel psychological punishment. Today, the island retains much of its original prison appearance, with facilities including a bird sanctuary, museum, and bookstore. In the shop, you can find memoirs from those whose sentences have been served; they all claim it was an "unforgettable painful experience." Despite its gloomy history, this island also serves as a wildlife refuge where many species of birds reside; for visitors, it remains an excellent place for reflection.


    Union Square—The Bay Area's Most Fashionable Shopping Paradise


    If you want to experience the feel of a big city, then come to Union Square (Union Square). This is absolutely paradise for shopping. Stores with international top brands and an abundance of goods decorate this square surrounded by palm trees.

    When you walk down this street, you'll feel like strolling through European alleyways, experiencing the perfect blend of modern and classic. Even if you don't make any purchases, a casual stroll will bring surprises.

    Union Square

    Union Square is located in the downtown area. At the center of the square stands a towering monument erected to commemorate America's naval victory over the Spanish fleet at Manila Bay. Union Square is surrounded by four streets with unique shops and designer department stores. Besides I. Magnin and Macy's, there are over a hundred high-end boutiques and fashion stores—truly paradise for shoppers. Additionally, San Francisco's theater district is located just southwest of Union Square. Even if you don't make any purchases, a casual stroll will leave you constantly surprised.


    City Hall—The Architecture Itself Is A Work Of Art


    San Francisco City Hall is a government office building with magnificent architecture, very classical and artistic, featuring a huge dome. For architecture enthusiasts, it's simply a visual feast.


    Often newlyweds hold wedding ceremonies on different floors or take wedding photos here, happy under the witness of city hall staff.

    San Francisco City Hall

    San Francisco City Hall is the government office building of San Francisco's municipal center in America, featuring distinctive Collegiate Gothic architecture style. Its huge dome ranks 5th in the world. The current city hall building replaced the old one that was completely destroyed by the 1906 San Francisco earthquake. For architecture enthusiasts, the scenery here is undoubtedly a visual feast.


    Twin Peaks - The Best Platform to Overlook San Francisco Scenery


    Twin Peaks are two hills standing side by side, called Twin Peaks because of their shape.


    If you want to enjoy a panoramic view of all of San Francisco, climbing to the top of Twin Peaks is the best choice. Driving from downtown gets to Twin Peaks very quickly. From the observation deck, you can see the entire cityscape of San Francisco at a glance, and even see the Golden Gate Bridge. The twilight scenery at sunset and the night views have their own unique charm.


    Time reference:

    2 hours

    Transportation:

    Take bus #37 to Twin Peaks stop.

    Tickets:

    Free

    Opening hours:

    All day

    Location:

    Christmas Tree Point Rd, San Francisco, CA, 94114


    Twin Peaks

    Twin Peaks are two hills with an elevation of about 270 meters. Because of their shape, they were nicknamed "Indian Maiden's Breasts" by early Spanish immigrants. They are also the only natural hills preserved within San Francisco. Reaching Twin Peaks is quite a challenge - you need to wear plenty of clothes to guard against strong winds and cold fog, and exert effort climbing the steep slopes. However, the hardship has its reward: on clear days, you can overlook the entire cityscape of San Francisco, and even see the red Golden Gate Bridge by the San Francisco Bay. This area is particularly ideal for admiring night views!


    Chinatown - A Place Where Every Chinese Person Finds a Sense of Belonging


    San Francisco's Chinatown (Chinatown San Francisco) stands as the western United States' only district comparable to New York's Chinatown, positioned at the intersection of Grant Avenue and California Street.


    The streets resemble Hong Kong architecture from the last century, with a nostalgic feel reminiscent of Hong Kong films from the 1980s. The signs you see are all written in Chinese characters, and everything you hear is also in Mandarin. This is a place that feels very familiar to Chinese people.


    Time reference:

    3-4 hours

    Transportation:

    Take the Powell-Hyde cable car to the Powell St & Jackson St stop

    Tickets:

    Free

    Opening hours:

    All day

    Location:

    Grant Avenue, San Francisco, CA, 94108


    Chinatown, San Francisco

    San Francisco's Chinatown is the only place in western America that can be compared to New York's Chinatown. It forms at the intersection of Grant Avenue and California Street. There are approximately 80,000 Chinese residents living here, creating a complete city within a city, full of exotic charm. Although San Francisco's magnificent Union Square and the western financial center are located next to Chinatown, within the district, all signs you see are written in Chinese characters, and everything you hear is also in Mandarin. This is a place that feels very familiar to Chinese people.


    California Academy of Sciences—Sailing through an ocean of natural science


    The California Academy of Sciences is absolutely paradise for nature science enthusiasts, being the only venue in the world that combines an aquarium, planetarium, natural history museum, and a four-story tropical rainforest.


    You can watch scientists at work through glass partitions and ask questions freely if you have any inquiries. Every Thursday evening, there's "Museum of Wonders Night" waiting for you to explore.


    Time reference:

    Half day

    Transportation:

    Take bus #44 to the 9th Ave & Irving St station, or take N line streetcar to the 9th Ave & Irving St station

    Tickets:

    Ticket $29.95/adult, $19.95/child

    Opening hours:

    Monday to Saturday (9:30-17:00), Sunday (11:00-17:00)

    Sight spot location:

    55 Concourse Drive, Golden Gate Park, San Francisco, CA, 94118


    California Science Museum

    Keyword: California Science Museum

    The California Academy of Sciences is the first comprehensive ocean-land-air biology museum in the United States, integrating education, exhibitions, research into one natural history museum. It ranks among the top 10 natural history museums worldwide and serves as a leading center for modern natural sciences research. The facility features Steinhart Aquarium, Morrison Planetarium—the world's largest fully digital planetarium, Kimball Natural History Museum, and a rainforest exhibit. At the planetarium, visitors can experience a mind-blowing journey through the universe; at the aquarium, explore the coral reef underwater world where over 2,000 fish species live. The site also boasts the world's largest living ecological green roof that breathes. Through glass viewing areas, visitors can watch scientists at work and ask questions on-site, or even visit the Natural Sciences Center on the second floor to consult directly with scientists.


    It is recommended to purchase the San Francisco tourism pass. With one ticket in hand, you save time on planning and queuing, while also enjoying a 50% discount on entrance fees—super bargain!


    II. Step outside San Francisco's urban area, embrace culture and nature


    San Francisco's city tour is destined to be rich and colorful. Outside the city, there are even more fun attractions, reachable with a very short drive (usually under four hours), perfect for day trips.


    Napa Valley—taste fine wines and enjoy scenic views in romantic wine country


    Over 400 wineries are located here, with 95% being family-owned businesses. Take the Napa Valley Wine Train or Napa Valley Wine Trolley to visit several wineries and enjoy fine dining in a day.


    Fine wine is just one part of Napa Valley's charm. The view of vineyards at sunset, seasonal delicacies gliding across your tongue, the romance of hot air balloons slowly ascending into the sky, appreciating art, cycling, and relaxation at spas all make Napa Valley intoxicating.

    Transportation Routes:

    After about an hour's drive, you can take a ferry and local VINE bus to Napa Valley. You can also arrange your journey through local travel agencies or rental companies.

    You can also choose a private car one-day tour. What should you do when coming to wine country? Of course, taste fine wines! But what if you're worried about drunk driving?

    A professional Chinese-speaking driver will take you on a private car one-day tour, let you enjoy fine wine with ease and pleasure.


    Monterey – Embrace the Bohemian Spirit in this Seaside Town


    Monterey is a coastal town in Northern California, renowned as California's "First City". It takes about 1.5 hours by car along Highway 1 from San Francisco.


    It is recommended to drive, cycle, or even walk on the famous 17-Mile Drive, enjoying the winding Pacific coastline scenery between Monterey Town and Carmel Town.

    Pebble Beach Golf Links, Cannery Row, Monterey Bay Aquarium, Carmel Beach, and more are also here. The streets are filled with exquisite small shops, restaurants, hotels, and art galleries worth visiting.


    Transportation Routes:

    Traveling along the scenic Pacific Coast Highway (Highway 1) for about two hours will bring you to Monterey. Additionally, you can take public buses, trains, or even boats from San Francisco.


    Monterey Town

    Monterey Town boasts a well-developed artistic community, with numerous museums and entertainment venues featuring both local and internationally renowned artists. The famous Cannery Row honors writer John Steinbeck, while the diverse marine life attracts visitors to one of North America's premier aquariums.


    Yosemite - Experience Nature Within a National Park


    Yosemite is approximately a four-hour drive from San Francisco. For those with limited time, I recommend focusing on the must-see attractions and iconic landmarks.


    After quickly passing through Yosemite Valley, you'll be greeted by stunning waterfalls, most notably Yosemite Falls. Continue your journey to the magnificent granite cliffs of El Capitan and the iconic Half Dome formation. Shouting at Glacier Point offers a powerful way to release stress from daily life.


    Hiking, rock climbing, cycling, skiing, rafting, wildlife watching - Yosemite has it all.

    If the weather cooperates, riding an open-air tram with friends for a deep experience of Yosemite is also a good choice.

    Routing:

    You depart from San Francisco, drive east along Highway 140 (more scenic) or Highway 120 for about three to four hours, or take Amtrak trains or chartered vehicles offered by numerous travel agencies (group tours or private options available).


    Choose a Yosemite one-day tour with dedicated transportation and professional guide commentary, plus access to Sequoia National Forest - getting both in one trip.


    Yosemite National Park

    Yosemite National Park is California's most famous tourist destination. Majestic waterfalls, towering granite peaks, and tranquil valleys make Yosemite appear like a fairyland from any angle. Towering trees, mountain streams, glacier-carved rugged rock cliffs, and shimmering lake surfaces transport visitors to an idyllic paradise. Without question, both liquid and solid water are the main contributors to Yosemite's magnificent landscapes, where you can find all your favorite natural scenery.


    Silicon Valley - Pay Respect to Tech Giants


    Heading south from San Francisco, about 40 minutes brings you to the global technology hub - Silicon Valley (Silicon Valley).

    This region is home to globally renowned companies like Google, Apple, Facebook, and countless startups. Many world-changing and trend-setting products were born here.

    Although you typically cannot tour inside company buildings, walking around their campuses is still a good experience. Feel the life of Silicon Valley people - is it like that popular TV show "Silicon Valley"?

    Routing:

    Drive north across the Golden Gate Bridge to reach Marin County for leisure time. You can also bike across the bridge. If you prefer crossing the bay rather than taking the bridge, you can choose buses, shuttles, or ferries.


    Choose a Silicon Valley one-day tour that allows you to admire tech giants while enjoying outlet shopping.

    IV. Transportation Recommendations


    External Transportation


    San Francisco has three airports: San Francisco International Airport (SFO) is the main airport located in southern San Francisco. Oakland International Airport (OAK) is on the east coast, spanning across the Bay Bridge. While Norman Y. Mineta San Jose International Airport (SJC) is one hour away from the city center.


    At San Francisco Airport, you can book private car pickup and drop-off services.


    Internal Transportation


    While many places in America are suitable for driving, San Francisco is among the cities where self-driving is not ideal, due to extremely steep street slopes and expensive parking fees.

    However, San Francisco has a very developed public transportation system. Buses, subways, historic cable cars, and trams - there's always a public transit option to serve your itinerary and take you exploring every corner of this city.


    Cable Cars are located in the downtown area, representing San Francisco's most famous vintage transportation method, extremely popular among visitors.


    San Francisco one-day tour guide with attractions, transportation tips, and travel recommendations.

    There are three routes: Powell-Hyde, Powell-Mason, and California Street. Riding the cable car feels like returning to San Francisco a hundred years ago. The clanging sounds create a very nostalgic atmosphere that's quite pleasant.

    The F line streetcar runs between Market Street (Market St.) and Fisherman's Wharf (Fisherman's Wharf). You can purchase tickets at various ticket booths, or buy them from the conductor upon boarding.

    Of course, relying solely on public transportation for sightseeing has its limitations, and you can also choose to rent a car. However, the streets in San Francisco's downtown area are somewhat bumpy and not very friendly for new drivers; experienced drivers might want to give it a try.

    If you plan to visit attractions around San Francisco, renting a car is highly recommended.

    V. Recommended Accommodation Areas

    San Francisco is the birthplace of boutique hotels, with rooms featuring unique styles. Hotel prices are mostly between $100-$300 nightly and include parking fees of $35-$50 per night. Most tourists choose to stay near Union Square for convenient transportation and easier access to attractions. A smaller number of visitors prefer staying near Fisherman's Wharf, where prices are relatively higher but there are more dining options nearby.

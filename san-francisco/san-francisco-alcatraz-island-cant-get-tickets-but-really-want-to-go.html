San Francisco Alcatraz Island | Can't Get Tickets But Really Want To Go - What Should I Do?

Legendary Devil's Island


Alcatraz Island, commonly known as Devil's Island, from the book "Uncle Sam's Devil's Island". Located in San Francisco Bay, California, USA, a ferry ride of about 10 minutes. Early on had a lighthouse and served as a military fortress. From 1933-1963 was Alcatraz Federal Penitentiary. In the 1970s it was occupied by localists from San Francisco for 19 months. Became a tourist attraction in 1972, designated as a US National Historic Site in 1986. Now part of Golden Gate National Recreation Park, with no plans to restore the prison again.


This is Devil's Island, accessible only via Pier 33, just a short walk from Fisherman's Wharf at Pier 39, of course this depends on walking speed - I should consider myself a fast walker


The tallest building standing on the right is the lighthouse. The two closest buildings in front of us, with the longest one being the factory where prisoners made brushes and other labor. All the buildings are dilapidated but quite atmospheric.


Alcatraz isn't just about housing many famous inmates, it also has many terrifying legends, mainly about ghosts. So night tours of Devil's Island have become a popular choice for many visitors.

Christmas Eve in the dark and silent prison, gazing at the warm lights of San Francisco across the water, hearing the laughter and joy on the other shore - just thinking about it makes me want to escape😂


How to Arrive


Taking a ferry from Pier 33 is the only way to reach the island. Tickets for visiting the island are not easy to get, especially during peak season - people book them as early as one week to one month in advance. The evening tours are particularly popular.


Here are several options:

1. Visit the island; since transportation on the island relies on your own two legs, walking is unavoidable. You can borrow a Chinese audio guide that introduces the history and stories along the way.

2. Cruise around Alcatraz Island for a full circle while getting close views of the Golden Gate Bridge; there are English narrations about both Alcatraz Island and the Golden Gate Bridge on the cruise ship.

3. Alcatraz Deep Dive Tour, lasting 4-5 hours. The first two hours offer in-depth exploration of Alcatraz Island, walking into areas that night cruises and daytime ships won't pass by, perfect for those who want a comprehensive experience. The latter two hours include the evening tour, following audio narration to visit cells and other areas.

4. Combined tour of Alcatraz and Angel Islands. Angel Island and Alcatraz Island are the two most famous islands in San Francisco Bay. During the Gold Rush era, Angel Island attracted many Asian immigrants.

This option takes approximately 5.5 hours, including the ferry to Alcatraz Island, free audio guide, plus the ferry to Angel Island and a hop-on-hop-off trolley tour. Operates April-September. The separate Angel Island itinerary typically departs from Pier 41. 5. Alcatraz + San Francisco city bus tour, this is also very popular. You can board the bus at any time yourself; the bus stops at all major attractions in downtown San Francisco. Available with 24-hour and 48-hour pass options. 6. Visit together with Redwoods and Sausalito. I recommend the first option for island visitation; the second tour covers everything in just one hour, with minimal difference between distant and close-up views. The main appeal might be purchasing a sunset ticket to enjoy an evening view from the boat. Island visits offer early morning tours with minimal crowds. Other times run every 30-40 minutes in both directions. Night tours provide two evening cruises, though slightly more expensive and harder to book. Most people prefer night trips for fewer visitors and a more atmospheric experience. Before docking, the boat circles Alcatraz Island—a unique feature not available on daytime excursions. There's another reason: night tours include areas unavailable during day visits—such as hospital wings and upper prison levels that enhance the historical immersion. Tips: The cell corridors are quite narrow with crowds often competing to view each room. A helpful strategy to avoid congestion is being among the last passengers off the boat, then entering the facility behind others to reduce immediate pressure on the pathways.


What should I do if I can't get tickets?


Getting tickets to Devil's Island is really, really hard. It's recommended to book at least three months in advance. If you really want to go but can't get a ticket, here are some solutions for reference.


1、On-site Ticket Purchase

Including staff members, if you ask if there are any same-day tickets for sale, they will all say no. But! These tickets do exist. This is a method to limit the on-site crowd flow and avoid letting more people be disappointed. Getting these tickets is also quite difficult; each person can buy only one ticket, and anyone boarding the island that day must be at the ticket purchasing site. If you leave the line for any reason (including using the restroom), you must rejoin from the end of the queue. The ticket office is located at Pier 33, with sales starting at 7:30 AM. On busy days, people start queuing as early as 4 AM, some even bringing sleeping bags. The total number of tickets available and whether you can purchase one depends entirely on luck. For instance, someone once started queuing at 6 AM with 130 people ahead of them, yet still managed to secure a ticket for the 10:45 departure boat. Conversely, all tickets could be sold out by 7:00.

2、After opening at 7:30, late-night boarding tickets are available for purchase daily in quantities of 10-20.

This ticket allows you to board Alcatraz Island before the night ferry departs, replacing someone who purchased a ticket but didn't show up. If no space is available, this ticket can be refunded at the ticket office.

3. Combine with other tour packages, such as Angel Island + Alcatraz, Alcatraz in-depth tour, etc. These tickets generally have availability starting one week in advance.


Escape from Alcatraz


A total of 80 prisoners attempted to escape from Alcatraz Island, with only three succeeding - the members of the five-person gang that escaped in 1962. Afterward, these three men were never heard from again; it remains unknown whether they drowned or were eaten by sharks.

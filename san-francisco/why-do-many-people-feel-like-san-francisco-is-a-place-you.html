Why do many people feel like San Francisco is a place you can visit again and again?

If you were to label San Francisco, what would come to mind?

Silicon Valley, Golden Gate Bridge, Fisherman's Wharf, technology, modernity, many Chinese people... These are all familiar labels. But its richness can surpass any label! Everyone in San Francisco can find their own "spiritual corner".

The various sloped streets turn this city into a scene of magical realism, and these amazing streets are one of San Francisco's most worthwhile attractions to see.


Nine Curves Flower Street


Here, both serious and rational financial elites move through the downtown area, while hippies enjoy their time in different interesting neighborhoods. It's also a city beloved by the LGBT community, with an atmosphere of remarkable inclusivity.


San Francisco Financial District


Haight-Ashbury Hippie District


Furthermore, San Francisco is never lacking tourists, whether domestic or international visitors.

As one of the must-visit destinations for a Western USA trip, how can you feel its heartbeat?


When thinking about traveling to Western USA, the first option that comes to mind for many people is driving yourself. However, for getting around in San Francisco's downtown area, this self-driving option is very unreliable.

The city center is filled with various slopes inclined at 20-45°. An ordinary person's driving skills may not be able to handle it. Additionally, parking spots are hard to find and parking fees are extremely expensive. If your driving isn't particularly impressive, I suggest you give up self-driving.


Since self-driving in San Francisco's downtown area is so inconvenient, what alternative methods can replace it?


The Historic Cable Cars (Cable Car)


The vintage cable cars are a perfect match for the steep streets. The cable car itself is one of San Francisco travel experiences and also this city's "walking attraction".


Inside these retro cars, hold onto the handrails to admire San Francisco's street views, as if completing an urban adventure, making you wonder if you're in a San Francisco from a hundred years ago.

Powell Street near Union Square is the cable car's main terminal. Boarding here makes it easier to get a seat. As a popular city transit option, not everyone can board smoothly everywhere.

It drives into Chinatown, where American vintage cars roll along Chinese-style retro streets, staging a show of retro chic!


It also passes through a real-life version of "Inception." Modern architecture on both sides, steeply sloped streets, and classic cars - these elements combine to create an air of surrealism here.


Tips:

If you just plan to experience the unique feel of the cable car, I recommend purchasing a one-way ticket based on where you want to go.

If you plan to ride the cable car and check out this city's various "attractions," consider buying a day pass instead.

Price: One-way $7, Day Pass $12 [Paper tickets $22] - I recommend downloading the MUNI app to purchase your ticket.


Cable Car Route Areas:

① Chinatown ② Financial District ③ Fisherman's Wharf ④ Nob Hill ⑤ Russian Hill ⑥ Union Square

If you have plenty of time, all these places can be reached by cable car.

The operating hours are from 6:30 AM to midnight. Riding the cable car at night offers another magnificent retro scene - it's just that evenings tend to get chilly (the vehicles are open-air design). Even in summer, remember to wear a jacket at night.

After all, Mark Twain said: "The coldest winter is San Francisco's summer."

MUNI Public Transportation System

The cable cars mentioned above are actually part of the transportation services provided by MUNI Group. MUNI is considered a local transportation giant, offering not only cable cars but also regular buses called Trolley Buses and regular subway service called MUNI Metro.


Trolley bus


Taking the Trolley Bus is especially suitable for checking out interesting shops, coffee shops, concept stores... scattered throughout the city... to discover fun hidden in some corners.


Some MUNI BUS stop signs are not very obvious, roughly printed on utility poles along the street, requiring you to actively look up and search.


Some stops are quite visible, immediately noticeable, with a flock of pigeons waiting for the bus alongside you.


MUNI Metro


MUNI Metro is one of the most popular transportation methods locally.

Compared to taking a bus, it can reach the city center more quickly, but you may also easily miss some small and beautiful places.


The stations where multiple routes converge in the middle are the absolute core areas of San Francisco. For tourists who want to fully explore San Francisco's urban area, if you're short on time, it's best to choose taking the subway, after all, the subway can avoid traffic jams.


If you plan to spend 1-2 days deeply exploring San Francisco's urban area, I highly recommend buying a one-day pass and downloading the muni app. It can help you get through San Francisco in a cost-effective and convenient way. The one-day pass also allows unlimited rides on MUNI's trolley cars.

MUNI routes are mainly distributed throughout San Francisco City. If you want to visit places like Berkeley and Oakland in the bay area, it's more recommended to take Bart.

For instance, if you want to visit pilgrimage sites like UC Berkeley, Berkeley Art Museum, Oracle Park (home of the Golden State Warriors), etc., Bart is the most convenient transportation method.


Bart — San Francisco Bay Area Rapid Transit System


Bart primarily connects San Francisco's urban core with surrounding major cities. Most professionals working in the city center live at considerable distances, making Bart their preferred commute option. Compared to MUNI's buses, it offers faster travel times with more predictable scheduling and comprehensive regional coverage!


For airport round trips, BART is one of the best options. From the airport to downtown, taking BART only takes about half an hour.

It's important to note that San Francisco's transportation varies greatly between weekdays and weekends. Before going out, be sure to check if weekend transit at your destination is also running normally.


BART Mon-Sat operating routes below↓


On Sundays, several lines are out of service, and train frequency drops instantly. After all, BART gets tired too and needs some rest.


Whether at San Francisco International Airport or Oakland Airport, you can't directly board BART to go downtown. You first need to take the free AirTrain to the BART station, then ride the train.


An interesting point is that BART officials strongly encourage everyone to travel by bike plus rail transit, mainly because it's more environmentally friendly. For this reason, stations are also equipped with "bike parking spots."


BART also allows passengers to bring bicycles onto carriages; inside the cars there are specially designed devices for securing bikes.

Taxis & Ride-Sharing Services


Taxis, like in any city around the world, will stop when you flag them down, though the prices aren't exactly pleasant, and there are fewer taxis on San Francisco streets nowadays.


San Francisco taxis have a base fare of $3.1, adding $1.39 for each additional mile. Additionally, upon getting off, you're required by regulation to tip the driver at least 10%. The number 333-3333, extremely prominent on the vehicle body, is the taxi's reservation phone number.


The reason there are fewer taxis is that ride-hailing platforms Uber and Lyft have captured the taxi market share. Ride-hailing services are now the mainstream mode of transportation, and they're also cheaper than traditional taxis; for shorter distances, the average cost falls between $7-$8.


Remember to download the app in advance; usage is similar to Didi in China. Didi has a partnership with Lyft, so you can directly use the Didi app to hail rides while in San Francisco.

If traveling to Silicon Valley, Uber or Lyft are recommended. Taking a taxi alone can be quite expensive, but splitting fares among 2-4 people makes it an acceptable price.


GoCar Mini Convertible


If you want more freedom in getting around, you can also experience the "mini convertible" — GoCar. Fisherman's Wharf has a dedicated rental location for it. It was once named by Time Magazine as the "most stylish and coolest invention of the year."


Each vehicle is equipped with a global real-time positioning system, then the car will explain stories about surrounding buildings and attractions based on your location in real time, like having a live guide serving only you.


GoCar won't let you miss San Francisco's popular destinations, and will also take you to many niche but interesting places for a drive. However, the price is a bit expensive — over 900 RMB per two hours, and drivers must be at least 21 years old with a valid driver's license.


Big Bus Double-Decker Bus


Besides the "mini convertibles" mentioned above, some "large convertibles" are also very noticeable in San Francisco. You'll often see them shuttling through the city's streets and alleys.

This is a very famous lazy person's tour bus locally, operated by Big Bus company, especially suitable for tourists who are too lazy to do research and planning.


I think one convenient thing about it is you can get on and off at will; when the car drives to a place you like, you can immediately get off to explore. Plus there's Chinese commentary on board, so many attractions won't leave you confused.


San Francisco's Big Bus has three lines: Red Line, Blue Line, and Green Line. The Red Line covers most of San Francisco's popular places; the Blue Line is a night tour line; the Green Line is the "Charming Town Special Line".

Golden Gate Park

The world's largest artificial park, 20% larger than New York's Central Park


Golden Gate Park has a very inconsistent style; wandering through it, you seem to be in Japan one moment and Europe the next. Turns out this San Francisco cosmic center park is "spliced" together from over a dozen small parks with different styles.


Inside are three popular museums: California Academy of Sciences, Asian Art Museum, and de Young Memorial Museum.

As an art exhibition enthusiast, I want to strongly recommend Anli Asian Art Museum to everyone. It houses over 15,000 artistic treasures from Asian countries and regions such as China, Japan, Korea (North), Indonesia, etc.


You can immerse yourself here for an afternoon, having a deep dialogue with Asian art pieces.


Financial District

Wall Street of the West Coast


Here is known as "the Wall Street of the West Coast," tall buildings paired with narrow streets. Although walking inside feels somewhat oppressive, this is precisely the rational temperament required by finance.


Actually I think financial districts all over the world look similar: skyscrapers everywhere, cool building color schemes, and seemingly beautiful lighting. Behind it all might be hard-working overtime dogs diligently moving bricks. However, San Francisco's Financial District has an Easter egg on its rooftops.


The rooftop of Salesforce Transit Center is a massive sky garden. Here there are winding paths, a circular music hall. It creates a quiet space for busy San Francisco white-collar workers and tourists. Surrounded by over 600 trees, this place is an excellent stress-relief destination.


Drink and chat at rooftop bar El Techo de Lolinda; under the summer night breeze, both your stomach and heart are very happy.


From The View Lounge you can overlook the entire city; every minute you'll be moved by the skyline at sunset.


Chinatown

The most authentic Chinese food in America is here


Right next to the Financial District is Chinatown, crayfish hotpot, sour cabbage fish - order any of them and they can relieve your homesick stomach.


It's said that 100,000 overseas Chinese residents live near Chinatown. Eating, shopping, sending packages, depositing and withdrawing money... they've lived here with flavor. Hearing the Mandarin and Cantonese around your ears, you can teleport back to the continent on the other side of the Pacific in one second.


Fisherman's Wharf

Relax as a slacker and blow sea breeze


Fisherman's Wharf is the starting station for big bus tours, and also a good place to taste seafood, watch sunset, and take walks or do some shopping.


You can watch sea lions lazily sleep and munch on the famous Dungeness crab at Pier 39, happily being a slacker.


Golden Gate Bridge Crosswalk Tour

A breathtaking view you'll never forget in your lifetime


The Golden Gate Bridge, ruined by countless movies, is one of San Francisco's iconic landmarks. Instead of just gazing at it from afar, why not cross it.


This allows for an immersive experience with the architecture itself, and viewing San Francisco Bay Area buildings from above the bridge is truly impressive.


After taking the big bus across the Golden Gate Bridge, you'll arrive at Sausalito town. Next, ride the Green Line freely around this charming town!


📗Green Line: Sausalito's Charming Town 📗


Sausalito is a picturesque small town on the other side of San Francisco Bay. From any vantage point, you see the Pacific Ocean on one side and colorful houses scattered across hillsides on the other, reminiscent of Italy's Cinque Terre.


The Green Line circles around the small town, and the scenery from being on the bus itself is extremely beautiful. Halfway through, it stops at downtown, so quickly hop off to discover the cute corners of this town's streets.


Sausalito Downtown

Although this small town has only 7,600 people, it boasts 14 art galleries, with a strong artistic atmosphere. It maintains a peaceful ambiance, very suitable for humans who want to escape the hustle and bustle of city life.


These small but beautiful art spaces distributed at street corners, although not large, each is enough to touch one's heart.


The town hosts an art festival—the Sausalito Art Festival—where various artists set up stalls to share interesting handmade items and their original artwork.


Viewing the Golden Gate Bridge from this town offers a stunning perspective, particularly when fog rolls in, creating an almost mythical atmosphere that makes one wonder if spirits might be descending upon it.


🔷Blue Line: San Francisco Enchanting Night🔷


The Blue Line is San Francisco's night tour special route, circling around downtown and then crossing the Bay Bridge to "adventure" on Treasure Island.


Treasure Island

No gold or silver treasure in sight, but night views worth their weight in gold


From Treasure Island looking at San Francisco downtown, many say this view is perfectly framed. Night scenes are lovely. In fact, compared to Shanghai, this place still feels a bit underwhelming.


Today's Treasure Island serves as a U.S. military base. Originally there were plans to build San Francisco International Airport here, but the final location was chosen at San Marino. Every month's last weekend hosts flea markets that local food and shopping lovers come to enjoy for some relaxation.


Bay Bridge (also known as San Francisco-Oakland Bay Bridge)

A low-key bridge living under Golden Gate Bridge's famous shadow


Under the imposing Golden Gate Bridge spotlight, most tourists don't realize that the San Francisco-Oakland Bay Bridge (shortened to Bay Bridge) is quite stunning as well.


It lacks the Golden Gate's viral fame and can only boast its "width"—currently the world's widest bridge. Driving across while looking back at downtown views, visitors are easily amazed by this lights-and-bridge spectacle it creates.


To learn more about where Big Bus can take you, you can directly check the official website.

Tips:

During rush hour (commute times), it's better to stay on the bus, as traffic congestion might cause the next bus to be late.

San Francisco gets cold in the evening; during fall and winter seasons, those who are not very physically robust might want to sit on the lower floor, while those who are in good health and not afraid of cold can sit on the upper open-air deck.


In our fast-paced lives, we're already constantly rushing. During travel, instead of treating transportation as a means to get somewhere quickly, truly slowing down to enjoy the journey itself is more comfortable.


If you have travel plans for Western America, first, never forget San Francisco; second, if time permits, try to allocate several extra days for this destination.

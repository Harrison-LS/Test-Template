Cheap All Inclusive Holidays: Making New Zealand Getaways More Affordable

### Cheap All Inclusive Holidays - Affordable Getaways to New Zealand ### Cheap All Inclusive Holidays - Affordable Getaways to New Zealand Price is tricky. Often, consumers buy in to the myth that if you want something nice, awesome, remarkable, you have to pay for it. Cheap all-inclusive vacations can provide you with a way to budget out an escape to New Zealand with a clear end goal and it destroys that myth. For travelers who foresee continually increasing costs of accommodation, food, transport, and activities sweeping in and making a once affordable trip, unaffordable, a well thought out package could address this concern. Cheap is not a synonym for good. In New Zealand, fully inclusive breaks are not as abundant as in some other popular destination beach areas, so package deals that promote ‘all inclusive’ offers should be treated with extreme caution. The cost savings should line up with your travel goals and the inclusions that you deem important. If meals, transport, and required vacation activities are not included, a seemingly low cost offer may be practically a more expensive, broken deal. A cheap all inclusive vacation should be the budgeting unit in your travel long term money saving plan. If the deal includes the typical costs you would normally incur anyway and a few extras, go for it. Travel costs that are safeguarded should not drive you to spend more. ### Why all inclusive can work well for a New Zealand break For travelers that like to maintain and manage their independence, the landscape of New Zealand and the outdoor vacation nature of New Zealand often lends an air of independence that can make an all inclusive vacation feel at odds with the destination. This is a misconception. There are trade-offs to independence. Each choice becomes a purchase. You must budget accommodation, meals, transport, fuel, parking, equipment, guided tours, and excursion tickets. None of the individual costs have to be excessive. But, it can seem costly if you add them all together.

The goal should be to consolidate the essentials the best you can. Some vacation packages do just that. Cheap vacation packages can include accommodation, meals, a varying degree of transportation (a.k.a. – local transport, transfers to/from the airport, some activities, or even credits to help you pay for experiences). Packages can range the gamut, from full board to stay and activity packages. What’s important is the package structure, not the wording of the advertisement.

This can be a significant money and time saver for those with a set budget. In addition, meals, and most activities are covered for families. Couples can appreciate an all inclusive vacation that offers dining and relaxing. Solo travelers know they would save money and time if everyone else doesn’t have to book all individual components. In all cases, a package vacation is designed to lower the total uncertainty prior to arrival by arranging the essential costs of your trip.

This is actually quite freeing. With the essentials of your vacation taken care of, your disposable money can be put to other non-routine and more important, more fulfilling aspects of your trip.

The case against chasing the lowest advertised price

The advice to "book the cheapest package available" is weak. People tend to view the first price they find as the best price. However, this is far from reality. Cheap all inclusive holidays should be judged by their total value rather than the first price.

Before you book, ask what would you have to buy separately? Consider the different elements of the trip. Examples would be food, transport to and from, activities, booking, etc. After you've laid out the package, compare it to a realistic independent travel itinerary, not one built around the absolute cheapest options.

One simple example is a package that includes accommodation and breakfast. This would be great for someone who intends to go out and eat one main meal daily. However, for someone who expects to have all meals and transport and activities taken care of, this would not be great. Additionally, a package like this can quickly become expensive if the activities include a lot of transport.

The goal should be to maximize the value of your travel expenditures and to resist overpayment of value you won’t get. Although all inclusive resorts may include food and wedding in package deals, they often include limited value for travelers hoping to engage in a lot of activities.

The best cheap all-inclusive holiday offers you actual value. You find that by eliminating what is not needed.

Learn what all inclusive means before you purchase

There is not a single universal definition of all-inclusive. What travellers need to understand is that there is no fixed definition. In some cases, all-inclusive may mean accommodation and all meals and the use of certain amenities on the premises. In other instances, all-inclusive may mean the room with breakfast and one excursion. In other situations, all-inclusive may mean a travel company holiday package that combines flight tickets and hotel accommodations with substantial out-of-pocket costs.

The bottom line is that packages should not be avoided. However, disciplined reading should be conducted to understand the fine print.

Before booking, answer the following questions:

  • What meals are included, and does this include any restrictions on meals, venue, time and/or selection?
  • What is the policy on drinks (included, partially, or not at all)?
  • Do airport or local transfers are included in the fare?
  • Do activities come at an inclusive price or are there reduced prices or bookings?
  • Does the price of the package include rental equipment?
  • Are there any additional charges, deposits or fees?
  • What about other local costs?
  • What is included for transportation or do you need additional tickets?
  • What happens if there are changes to the activities due to weather changes, availability or operational changes?

This may seem like excessive caution. However, it will save you from the unexpected ultimately. The more details a provider includes the easier it is to understand the deal and make the assessment if it is actually a good deal. Think about how all inclusive New Zealand holidays should be more about what New Zealand is about. They should be about landscapes, food, and outdoor adventures. They should not be about staying on a property where you have prepaid meals. One way to enjoy a large local area is to have a short break all inclusive New Zealand holiday and enjoy a break at a lodge where you can partake in activities that are designed for relaxation and include elements of food and wellness. Another way to enjoy a New Zealand all inclusive break is to stay in an area for a number of days and go on a short number of activities or excursions in the area. New Zealand all inclusive holidays should be enjoyed for the activities but should also be enjoyed for relaxation. Families will have more value from a all inclusive holiday than a room only deal and then having to pay extra for meals. It's important to think beyond the boredom that typically accompanies finding inexpensive and convenient food options when away. A successful family trip is more about planning downtime activities with flexibility and convenience. This is more important than providing a range of activities and entertainment. The same can be said for couples trips. A smaller package may provide more value than a broad, overstuffed deal. Especially considering that a trip is so much more than a sum of its individual components.

Timing is the most important, yet most overlooked travel component. Package extras and value-adds are important, but timing is king. The flexible traveler can save the most money when demand is the lowest. For example, school holidays and peak events will drive prices and availability higher. Just because your travel dates are fixed doesn't mean there's no value in a simple package. Many destinations offer better packages if demand is low and the preferred dates are flexible.

It is important to find at least one energy sweet spot when choosing travel dates. Weather, daylight, and personal schedules should be taken into account. The ideal package for you may include flexibility in the dates, but fixed travel locations and accommodations. Go for a narrower search with focus around similar packages, instead of doing a more broad search across different packages and offerings. This shows whether a lower price means better timing or simply contains fewer bonus offers, a less convenient location, or a weaker room category. If you want to go to a specific property or traveling during a specific period, go ahead and book early. Other travelers who have more flexibility may want to wait for the later offers. Neither way is always correct. People often think that presenting an offer too late will always mean that it will be a very low price. If you wait for a late offer, it must still be a good offer that's for your desired travel, and doesn't have extremely long transportation times, poor locations or very brief travel times. One of the biggest reasons all inclusive holidays are not cheap is transportation. New Zealand tends to reward traversing long distances, but these kinds of long trips can really reduce how much money you save on a package. Do not assume that a very low price stay makes an extended, all stops, itinerary affordable. The best way to save money on transportation is usually to save unnecessary movement. Travel for the purpose of relaxation and try to enjoy just one location rather than seeing dozens. A short destination with less moves and transfers means more enjoyable relaxation time rather than opportunities for your budget to be wasted. Make sure you find out where transfers as well as the arrival and departure times for all travel packages fit into your desired travel. If transportation is not included, include the cost before buying. The transportation cost can make an offer that looks cheap a very poor value. A more expensive package might actually be better value. This is especially true if you don't have to worry about so many additional expenses, and if the package provides easier transfers. Transport for independent travellers may still be worth it to those who want to travel widely. However, independent travel should not be the hidden consequence of buying a cheap package that is far away from all the places people want to go. Affordability also doesn't mean having to chop every expense on your trip. Rather, it means directing your money to things that have value. Major costs should be bundled, after which the focus should be on experiences. Included activities and meals should be used to their fullest extent. Packaged meals don't have to be eaten just to satisfy the obligation. Packaged meals can even leave room for additional treats, such as a dinner out. Better yet, just use the credit that was included for an onsite activity, and save money. Meals, and other extracurricular activities, don't have to be unstructured, and vacuumed travel packages don't better guarantee that your trip will be a fun memorable experience. Finances should not be a reason to forgo taking a much-needed break. Travel packages should bundle major expenses to better ensure a relaxing and memorable trip. Separate it from the cost of the package. This amount can cover other things, like optional meals, souvenirs, spontaneous excursions, or other things you may need. Consider it an allowance. When you run out of allowance, see all further spending as a conscious decision, rather than something you automatically do. This helps avoid the common problem of people going on an all-inclusive trip, only to pay for things individually, which undermines the purpose of the all-inclusive trip.

Counterargument: independent travel is always cheaper

Independent travel is definitely cheaper for people who are able to research a lot, use basic accommodation, eat out less, travel slowly, and have lots of flexible choices. It can be a good option if you have lots of time and don't care about lots of extra things. Independent travel is also more time consuming, and if you go out of your planned budget it can quickly become expensive. The cheapest itinerary is not always the one people end up choosing.

A package has value when it reflects how you think you'll behave. If you know you will want good accommodation and meals, and lots of organized activities all you have to do is book an all inclusive package deal. Sometimes all inclusive package deals end up being cheaper because you get lots more value for your money. It is likely to be a better holiday too because you get a real break, rather than saving money at the cost of vacation enjoyable activities.

Practical conclusion: buy clarity, not a label

Discounted all-inclusive New Zealand holiday packages are a good option to help bring the cost of a holiday down, but they actually aren't the best option if customers only consider the term “all inclusive.” Don't think that the package with the most inclusions is the best. You get what you pay for, so spend a bit extra for a clearer, less restrictive package. This option narrows the search to the package with the most inclusions and least holes at a price that will not break the budget. Pick a flexible package. Have a look at a few options that fit the destination you want to visit, how long you want to travel, and your desired inclusions. Look at the cost of the package compared to organizing your trip yourself. Look at prices for costs like meals, transport, and activities. Look at what costs you may have to cover that other packages may include. While some travelers like to avoid packages with higher inclusions to save money, it may save you money in the long run. The best cheap all inclusive holidays take the hassle out of organizing a dream trip to New Zealand, and allow travelers to enjoy their trip without thinking too much about how much money they may be spending.

Choosing an Online Travel Agent for New Zealand Travel

An online travel agent simplifies holiday planning for New Zealand. However, consumers must resist bookingfragmentation. There are many agents that offer the same services, but travellers need to remember that booking through the lowest priced agent does not necessarily provide the best value. The value is really seen when travel plans need to be rescheduled due to flight delays and cancellations, sudden road closures, and activities that may unexpectedly be cancelled due to weather conditions. Booking the lowest price agent may seem like a good deal, but most travellers soon find out that flexibility and accountability are often worth more than a good deal. The best way to use travel agents is to scope out their booking conditions, quality of service, and their quantity of on-the-ground inventory before booking with the cheapest agent. Price should be the final criteria for evaluating travel services. For a complex travel destination like New Zealand, value should be placed on flexibility because a good deal isn't worth much when, in the end, the traveler loses flexibility. New Zealand travel is more complicated than a typical city break. One leg of the travel may require multiple bookings for accommodation, multiple transfers via car, multiple ferry rides, and booking activities that are weather dependent. Travelers often need to arrange multiple services which an online travel agent can consolidate. Travelers can use one agent's online booking service to plan availability for multiple services. Online travel agents have the ability to consolidate multiple services for travelers. An efficient online travel agent displays accommodations based on availability and incites ideas on room types, available transportation, and offers that may be tailored to best fit a group booking. It can consolidate booking cancellations and simplify booking arrangements when travelers coordinate multi-stop excursions. Reliability has its constraints. Online travel agents are usually bookings intermediaries and not travel suppliers for a given flight, accommodation, rental car, activity and/or other travel related services. When issues happen, “getting a supplier to rebook” transforms from an easy answer to booking a trip to a nightmare! This approach can work very well for travel booking agents that have simplified booking platforms and are responsive. It can be very frustrating for travel booking agents where booking policies are rigid and/or support is unresponsive. In travel booking, the correct question is not “What is the lowest price booking platform?” It is “What is the booking platform whose booking policies are the most flexible for changing my travel plans?” A discounted activity may be sold with restrictions making it difficult to reschedule. None of these restrictions is inherently unfair. Suppliers often provide price discounts when they receive more certainty. However, it presents a problem when potential customers compare only the total cost and believe two bookings are the same. They are not the same if one can be changed or canceled under reasonable conditions and the other cannot be. Consider two accommodations along a route through New Zealand. One is slightly cheaper through an online travel agent but requires an immediate payment and has no refund. The other is slightly higher but allows cancellation at a certain time. If your itinerary has many connections or is reliant on a rental car, then the flexible accommodation probably provides a better value. Without this flexibility, a minor disruption could result in a total loss for the cheaper booking. Travelers must learn how to ignore the pressure messages to book now. Travel agents use messages such as "Only one room left" to create a sense of urgency. If you book the first accommodation, you run the risk of booking under their terms. The most prudent approach is to treat the online agent as a careful consideration in your decision. Search the platform for location, accommodation type and style, configurations, reviews, cancellation policies, and a general price range. Once you find it, check supplier site and see prices. Once you have all the info, then you can book.

Booking directly with a hotel doesn't guarantee the deal is better than going through an online booking agency. Booking agencies stock deals that include payment options, loyalty programs, or packages that offer availability across booking channels that are not visible to others. It may be the easiest place to manage your booked trips, especially if you need to book multiple trips.

A booking with a particular agency may seem to be a better deal than booking directly with a hotel, or vice versa. Why? There are differences in terms and conditions for booking directly vs. booking indirectly, even if the hotel room itself or activities are the same across the channels.

During your search for a better deal, ensure online booking agencies state taxes and charges clearly on their website. Also ensure that if they claim 'breakfast included,' a matching room type is described consistently, and if they add terms and conditions based on time zones. Consider the same if you are booking any transport or activities.

If the online booking agency has better terms and conditions and a price that is not significantly different than booking directly with a hotel, then that agency should be used. If the hotel is booking directly, but offers the same package at the same or a better price, that booking should be used. The booking should not be made based on an unbreakable rule.

Support During Disruption Is the Real Test

An online booking agency shines when everything goes to plan for a traveler. Most websites can confirm a booking and send an email to you. The true test occurs when the plan changes. Most people look at price when booking online travel, but a booking platform that offers booking references and lets you contact support and amend bookings is useful way beyond the stated price. Travellers should look for a clear description of change and cancellation policies instead of promising good customer service. Before you buy, ask some basic questions: Is the online travel agent user friendly and does it clearly show the supplier, travel dates, booked components, and cancellation policy? Is it clear how the policy for a booking can be changed, cancellation, and refunds? Is the policy and contact info for support available before support is needed? Are the clear booking and cancellation policies available upfront and not hidden? Travelers should not assume that large brands offer the best support for all reservations. Nor should they assume small brands are not useful. Clarity is important. Who is responsible and what is the traveler’s responsibility should be clear. Vague language should not be used when booking high-cost components of a trip. A common, incorrect view, is that travel disruption insurance means booking support is less important. This view is incorrect. While insurance is important, reservations are still essential. An insurance claim may take weeks to evaluate. An insurance claim will not provide you a reservation in the meantime. Travel insurance is different from good booking terms and conditions. The combination is ideal for travel. A user-friendly inventory specific to New Zealand is much more valuable than a large, global search list. An online travel agent (OTA) is useful if it addresses the user's requirements for trip style and placement of accommodations. The OTA should offer accommodations for families and groups. Activities should be offered for the relevant season and region. The OTA should offer rental vehicles with a collection point of your choosing along with the vehicle type of your choosing. It should offer locally operated activities with well detailed descriptions. Providing many options may give the user a false sense of choice. A travel agent should present useful information in a consistent manner and make important filters truly usable rather than ornamental. Users should inspect detailed reviews, the map location, and consider areas of the descriptions that may be of particular importance for their stay. Most helpful reviews answer specific questions. Was the room configuration correct? Was check-in clear? Was the room location convenient based on the itinerary? Were the room amenities accurate?

One or two glowing or bad reviews are not most valuable. Recurring issues across multiple reviews are more important.

Do not look to the rating for your answer, read the booking details. A highly rated property might still be a poor fit for your itinerary if the property has poor cancellation terms, if the location means additional driving, or if the room type does not fit your needs.

Bundle Carefully: Convenience Can Also Reduce Flexibility

Booking flights, accommodation, cars, and activities together is a convenient way to travel. For example, bundling might also help simplify payment and consolidate reservations. However, bundled bookings can also be more restrictive. Changes that would normally be permitted for one of the bookings may not be allowed for related bookings. A compromise that allows for flexibility is to bundle only the flight and the accommodation. More flexibility is appropriate for elements likely to change. For example, these elements may include the overnight stops on a multi-day road trip or activities which are sensitive to the weather. There is no prize for having everything booked on one digital platform if you can't change the booking anymore.

Travellers should also avoid booking activities just because an online travel agent makes it easy to include a lot of activities on an itinerary. Remember, the booking path is not designed to protect the quality of your trip. Try to book to allow enough travel time and avoid booking every day with time-fixed activities. There is no glamour in reading conditions, but it is online travel agents that earn or lose your trust the most in this section. The conditions should explain what happens to your booking if you cancel, show up late, or change your dates. You should also know what happens if you ask for help.

For accommodation, you should verify the booking amount and the cancellation time. Check the refund policy and the penalty for not showing up. Confirm the number of guests, what room or unit you are booking, and the policies the property has regarding special arrangements for your arrival. For rented vehicles, you should look beyond the rental price. Check the terms for picking up and dropping off the vehicle. There may be special terms for the people allowed to drive as well as the deposit, the insurance you must buy, the fuel, and the terms for how much you can drive. There may be terms on the driving you can do and the route you can take. Clarify with the supplier all that you need to know before confirming.

When looking at activities, find the cancellation policy, duration, start location, and whether there's a requirement for booking in advance or on a first come first serve basis. It's a good idea to check the operator directly as well as with the online travel agent in case you're planning to book an excursion that's a major part of your trip. This is not a betrayal of the website, it's sensible trip planning.

A New Zealand Booking Strategy That Makes More Sense

The ideal strategy is to utilize each of these options. Online travel agents don't always offer the best value, but they can provide a lot of clarity in terms of pricing and availability, and they may also incorporate useful customer feedback. That's why you start there.

  1. Check through an online travel agent booking site for availability.
  2. Prioritize the bookings for key activities, transport connections, and accommodation.
  3. Understand the full terms of the booking before looking at extras.
  4. Compare the booking on the supplier's website.
  5. Select the booking channel that has the best value and the best terms, not the booking channel that has the lowest price.
  6. Keep all preferences, booking numbers, addresses, and support contacts saved in case of technological issues.
  7. Check the itinerary before you go.

This strategy takes more time than booking at the lowest price, but it isn't much more inconvenient. It draws attention to the places where a big mistake has the most significant impact. There is little value in saving money on one night if that results in the entire itinerary being subject to expensive change fees or needless confusion.

Final Verdict: Choose Control Over a Cheap Headline Fare

Online travel agents are useful and efficient when they help only compare options and book, however, it becomes a poor choice if they skip reviewing options in order to save some money with the discount badge or a fast checkout.

The straightforward solution is to use an online travel agent, but do not let it make the decision for you. Focus on a travel agent that offers transparent cancellation policies, best support, local information, and bookings that fit all of the stops on a multi-stop New Zealand itinerary. Compare direct bookings to evaluate prices that are actually the best, not the worst.

Choose an online travel agent wisely to save time and planning. Choose poorly to add a dictionary between you and the solution to your problem. It's too important to ignore for the planning of a New Zealand itinerary especially, because a lot of the bookings rely on one other.

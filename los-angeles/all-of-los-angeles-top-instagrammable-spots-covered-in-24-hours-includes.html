All of Los Angeles' Top Instagrammable Spots Covered in 24 Hours! (Includes Complete Route Guide)

Los Angeles is the second largest city in the United States by area, after only New York City. It's the birthplace of Hollywood and a trendy city with many Instagram-worthy attractions. This City of Angels is not only a fashion capital but also features various popular tourist spots. Each landmark has its own unique way of making it onto Instagram photo walls, attracting influencers and fashion enthusiasts from all over. Today I'll share how to explore Los Angeles' famous attractions thoroughly within one day, from morning till night!


8:30 | Departing from your accommodation hotel


Wake up at 8 am, have a Western-style breakfast near the hotel or where you're staying. Then depart from the hotel to begin the Los Angeles popular attractions tour for the day.


9:30 | Angels Flight Railway — Location of "La La Land" Film Scenes


Angels Flight is a sightseeing attraction located in downtown Los Angeles, United States. It has been listed on the National Register of Historic Places. This railway, with a total length of 99 meters (298 feet), can be said to have one of the shortest routes in the world. Started operating in 1901, it uses two cable cars to transport passengers up and down the steep slope between Hill Street and Olive Street.


The original intention for building the railway was to provide an easy way for wealthy residents living on hillsides to go up and down the mountain, avoiding the need to climb 200+ steps by walking. Passengers could take the cable car that only took about one minute for just one cent back then.


This short railway is like a silent angel, quietly witnessing the birth of over 100 films. The moment you board it feels like traveling back to Los Angeles more than 100 years ago along with the tracks, a unique experience. In "La La Land," Mia and Seb shared a sweet kiss here, making countless film fans filled with infinite longing for this romantic movie landmark.


Practical Information

Traffic: Take the subway Red Line or Purple Line to Civic Center Station or Grand Park Station, and it's within walking distance.

Tickets: One-way: $1/person, Round-trip: $2/person

Operating Hours: 6:45–22:00

Website: ***


Tips:

Address: 350 S Grand Ave, Los Angeles, CA 90071 USA


Angel Railway

The Angel Railway is a filming location for the movie "La La Land," where Mia and Sebastian share a romantic date, sharing a sweet kiss in the wooden car.

The Angel Railway has been operating since 1901, making it the world's shortest railway. Visitors can experience this brief and enchanting journey on the Angel Railway, riding in the vintage orange-yellow and black painted train with wooden carriages.


10:00 | "The Last Bookstore" — Filled with mysterious magical atmosphere

This is one of Los Angeles' large bookstores, collecting many new and used books across various categories, as well as vinyl records in various styles, etc. Its appearance ranks among the top globally for bookstores. It's also a must-visit place for avid readers. Strolling through this bookstore gives you a feeling of deeper spiritual connection with physical books, instantly creating the urge to pick up a book and spend the whole day here. You can easily take beautiful photos here too!


Tips:

Address: 453 S Spring St, Los Angeles, CA 90013, United States


10:30 | Melrose trendy street—Instagram-famous wall for shooting large photos


Melrose Avenue can be said to be Los Angeles's trendy hub. Here you'll find not only various trendy brands and second-hand vintage shops, but also Instagram-famous walls that attract numerous influencers for photos. Even more popular is the world's first famous influencer wall - the Pink Wall by Paul Smith. When there are many people, you have to queue just to take pictures; if it's not for magazine photography, cameras aren't even allowed. To commemorate LA Pride once a year, another side of Paul Smith has been completely painted with rainbow graffiti.


Besides the pink wall, here there are various walls suitable for trendsetters' photos, like the wing-adorned wall in the photo and the spontaneously graffitied walls.


Tips:

Address: Melrose Ave., Los Angeles, CA, 90046


Melrose Avenue
Melrose Avenue is a shopping street where young people in Los Angeles gather. Here there are many local specialty boutique shops that specialize in unique and rare items and avant-garde clothing stores. In addition, major brand stores, art galleries, vintage boutiques, and upscale restaurants are everywhere. When you're tired of wandering around, finding a restaurant is absolutely not difficult. In short, this place has everything you want.


11:30 | Bradbury Building - A Classic Must-Visit Destination in Los Angeles


The Bradbury Building was built by George H. Wyman in 1893 and is one of Los Angeles's landmark buildings. However, even looking at it now, it still carries a bit of modernity. Some people say this building exudes a strong Victorian style; others say it resembles Italian Renaissance architecture.


It features a massive glass dome where soft natural light filters through the roof, making the entire space feel transparent and exquisite. The interplay of light and shadow shifts throughout the day. Each elevator resembles a delicate "birdcage"—all are beloved Instagram-worthy shots. A helpful note: you can only view the elevators but not ride them, since only the first floor is open to tourists.


Tips:

Address: 304 S. BROADWAY LOS ANGELES, CA 90013


The Bradbury Building

If you've seen the Hollywood film "Blade Runner," you'll likely be familiar with this historic structure—it was filmed here.

This building, constructed in 1893 and named after its owner, is one of Los Angeles' earliest structures. Despite its age, this Victorian-style edifice continues to surprise every visitor with its charm.

Within these confined walls, you can appreciate the exquisite architectural details: a beautiful courtyard, ornate dark green wrought iron staircase, stained glass windows, marble flooring, and narrow antique elevators.


12:00 | Central Food Market—culinary paradise for food lovers

If roads are the blood vessels of a city, then markets are its stomach and intestines—witnessing an era and a city's history through down-to-earth, people-loving, vibrant ways.

Central Market at 317 South Broadway holds many nicknames like "LA's Stomach," "LA Cafeteria," and "LA's Big Kitchen." It is easy to imagine how much great food can be found here.

For locals, the Central Market that opened in 1917 and has weathered a century of changes remains an irreplaceable shared memory. This 30,000 square foot market gathers dozens of restaurants and food stalls representing world cuisine, a concentrated reflection of California's multiculturalism. You can also see this in the recently popular movie "La La Land"—one of the dating locations for the dream-chasing lead couple is at Central Market.

Here you can find all kinds of authentic traditional foods, plus many celebrity restaurants such as EGGSLUT, DTLA CHEESE, BELCAMPO, and G&B COFFEE can all be found here.

13:30 | Hollywood Walk of Fame—every photo looks like a magazine spread


The Hollywood Walk of Fame was built by Southern California artist Oliver Weismuller, who was hired by the city government with the goal of enhancing Hollywood's "image".


In the beginning, many recipients received several star awards for their contributions in various fields. However, this practice has not appeared in recent decades (late 20th to early 21st century), so only a very small number of early recipients received more than one star.


The first star was placed in 1960, and its owner is Joan Woodward.


Today's Walk of Fame is jointly maintained by the Hollywood Chamber of Commerce and the privately funded Hollywood Historical Trust. For visitors from China, this site has become one of the most renowned tourist attractions. It features over 2,500 five-pointed stars made of terrazzo and brass, embedded along sidewalks spanning 15 blocks on "Hollywood Boulevard" and 3 blocks on "Vine Street".


These "stars" serve as permanent tributes honoring exceptional achievements in the entertainment industry, commemorating actors, musicians, directors, producers, musical groups, theatrical ensembles, fictional characters, and other notable figures. For those unfamiliar with the names engraved on stars, five symbolic icons help identify each recipient's field:

1) Camera—representing film and television actors and directors;

2) Television set—honoring outstanding talk show hosts and TV program presenters;

3) Phonograph record—a natural tribute to soul singers;

4) Broadcast microphone—acknowledging renowned radio personalities for their exceptional performances in broadcasting media;

5) Tragedy and Comedy Masks, honoring theater artists' contributions to drama.


Practical Information:

Duration: 1-3 hours

Transportation: Bus: Take buses 212, 217, or 312 and get off at Hollywood / Highland station

Tickets: Free

Opening hours: All day


Tips:

Address: N Highland Ave & Hollywood Blvd, Los Angeles, CA 90028


Hollywood Walk of Fame

· The Hollywood Walk of Fame was built in 1958. It is a sidewalk extending along Hollywood Boulevard and Vine Street, making it a famous cultural historical landmark of Los Angeles.

· There are over 2000 star-shaped medals embedded on the street with the names of Hollywood celebrities, commemorating their contributions to the entertainment industry.

· Unless occasionally replaced due to nearby construction or other reasons, the positions of the star-shaped medals on the boulevard remain permanently unchanged.


14:30 | Los Angeles Observatory - The Glass Slide Popular on Douyin


OUE Skyspace Los Angeles is one of the taller observation buildings in Los Angeles and a sister building to New York's World Trade Center. It offers an excellent vantage point overlooking all of LA. Its official name, not searchable on maps, is Federal Bank Building. Here there are not only large wings for taking photos at the top floor, but also the glass slide that became extremely popular on Douyin.


The TianTai observation deck has two sections: both feature wing-shaped glass walls with different designs - one wing appears to point upward while the other points downward. Visitors can choose which wall to photograph based on where the sunlight falls!


Tips:

Location: 633 W 5th St, Los Angeles, CA 90071, United States.


16:00 | Griffith Observatory - Hollywood Sign Viewing Point


The Griffith Observatory faces across from the Hollywood Sign mountains and offers panoramic views of Los Angeles. As a famous viewing destination in Los Angeles for years, it beautifully showcases the city's landscape. The movie "La La Land" especially captured its stunning beauty.


The observatory consists of three domed buildings housing exquisite astronomical instruments and exhibits. A large rotating globe and planetary motion diagrams allow visitors to learn about astronomy knowledge, while a unique triple-beam solar telescope in the western building and a 12-inch Carl Zeiss refracting telescope in the eastern exhibition hall provide free opportunities for exploring the mysteries of the universe.


Los Angeles locals love visiting the Griffith Observatory at night. Looking up reveals brilliant starlight everywhere, then inside the domed rooms you can trace their life paths across the sky. For many years ago, people believed stars were light sent from paradise. Overlooking the city panorama at night here definitely gives a profound feeling of what "City of Stars" really means.


Practical Information:

Duration: 1-3 hours

Transportation: Take the downtown loop bus DASH Weekend Observatory Shuttle and get off at Griffith Observatory Station.

Tickets: Free

Opening Hours: 12:00-22:00 (January 1 - December 31, Tuesday - Friday), 10:00-22:00 (January 1 - December 31, Saturday - Sunday)


Tips:

Address: 2800 E. Observatory Rd., In Griffith Park, at the end of Vermont Ave., Los Angeles, CA 90027


Griffith Observatory

·Located on a northwest hilltop in Los Angeles, offering panoramic views of the entire city.

·Features astronomical knowledge about planets and the universe. The top sphere screen theater is built to mimic the sky, allowing 360° appreciation of celestial evolution films. The naked-eye 3D technology brings cosmic wonders within reach.

·The open-air platform offers views overlooking Los Angeles city - orderly streets, neat urban buildings, and downtown skyscrapers are all clearly visible.

·After sunset, the stargazing terrace is free to access, where you can see twinkling stars in the sky and lunar surface outlines.

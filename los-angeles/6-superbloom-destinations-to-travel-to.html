When visiting Joshua Tree National Park, many people imagine the bizarre twisted trees and the branches that reach out to the sky. If you visit the park during late winter or early spring, you may be surprised to see the desert floor shimmering with wildflowers. If you time your visit just right, you may even see a rare superbloom of wildflowers.

**When to go:** If you want to get a head start on springtime blooms, try visiting during late winter for a glimpse of some of the first flowers of the season. To see the Joshua trees unique pineapple like blooms you will want to plan your visit in March or April.

Saguaro National Park has superblooms that occur very rarely, as there will only be one occurrence once every 10 to 15 years. With thousands of parks across the country, there are few that are as striking. After a very wet season, the 40 foot tall saguaro cacti burst into white flowers at the top of their cacti, and sometimes along the stems and all the way down.

**When to go:** If you want to experience cactus blooms, the best time to visit would be the last week of May to the first week of June. If this brief time period does not fit in with your schedule, you can visit anytime between late February to mid-April. During this time you will be able to see yellow brittlebush and other bright and visually appealing wildflowers in the desert.

**Best photo spots:** The most amazing place to take pictures is Cactus Forest Drive. There are many huge blooming saguaros with the Rincon Mountains behind them. You might even see wildlife like the javelina, which looks like a pig. If you want to walk a little bit and learn some history, you can walk a little bit on the Signal Hill Trail and see some ancient petroglyphs.

While you're there: Tucson has plenty of Mexican restaurants so you won't run out of choices for dinner, AND it's only 30 minutes away! So you won't have to spend your time weighing dinner options, make your way to El Charro Cafe, which is known as the “home of the chimichanga.”

6 Superbloom Destinations Worth Traveling For

Think you know the desert? I thought that too, until I saw a superbloom. All it takes is a little extra rain to wake seeds that have been sleeping for months, sometimes even years. This turns dry, rocky soil into a vibrant carpet of blooming plants. Death Valley is one of the best places in California to see this event, but for blooming deserts, there are a few around the world, several of which are incredible.

Interested in what else spring has to offer besides tulips and cherry blossoms? These unique places showcase different ways to experience spring. First up, this destination is quite literally hot.

Atacama Desert, Chile

Wildflowers blooming in Chile's Atacama Desert. Image: mcodern/Tripadvisor

Every five years, the Atacama Desert, which receives only a few inches of rainfall, has the ability to transform the landscape into a blooming wildflower paradise. The landscapes are often described as more lunar than terrestrial than terrestrial. Atacama is one of the driest places on the earth, and yet, اanすanñucas and other species of wildflowers emerge from hibernation and fill the desert with color as red and yellow shades.

When to Go: If you want to take advantage of this location, you’ll need to plan ahead and monitor the weather closely. If Northern Chile is expecting more than average winter rainfall, it means that the “flowering desert” (desierto florido) will bloom in the spring (Sept - Nov range). The phenomenon has occurred more often in recent years, improving the odds of seeing it.

Where is the best place to take pictures? My number one pick for witnessing the desierto florido is Parque Nacional Pan de Azúcar, about two hours from Copiapó. It has flowering valleys with a rough coastline. Another stunning option is Parque Nacional Llanos de Challe, close to Huasco.

While you are there: The day doesn’t have to stop when the sun goes down! The Atacama Desert is home to one of the best stargazing spots in the world. Because of its high altitude and clear skies, there is little to no light pollution. The Milky Way is incredibly vivid at the Observatorio IOcrux & Etno-Astronomical Licanantay.

Antelope Valley Poppy Reserve, California

Visitors walk along a path lined with poppies at the Antelope Valley Poppy Reserve in California.

The Antelope Valley California Poppy Reserve. Image: traveler21132655/Tripadvisor

Experience a 'Wizard of Oz' landscape less than two hours away from Los Angeles at Antelope. These hills are covered with a brilliant blanket of mostly reddish orange and some golden yellow poppies. And lightweight poppy flowers will close and hide their colors from view if they are experiencing an overcast sky, so they are best to be seen on a sunny day.

Timing your visit: Late Fall and early winter rainfall gives the best indication for how the coming season’s performance will be. Blooms typically appear from mid-February to May but in wet years, blooms can even appear a few weeks in advance.

Location of the best photo opportunity: The reserve has eight miles of trails and close-up views of the poppies. Antelope Loop is particularly rewarding. It is a moderately challenging 5-mile walk to a hilltop that is surrounded by fields of flowers.

While you are in the region, you can enjoy natural attractions nearby which will make your trip even more enjoyable. I recommend Arthur B. Ripley Desert Woodland State Park, which preserves one of the last unspoiled California juniper forests. While you explore, take one of the trees' green-blue juniper berries and crush it. The smell is quite similar to a gin and tonic.

Fitzgerald River National park, Western Australia

The park has a shrub with yellow and orange foliage

Hakea victoria shrub in Fitzgerald River National Park. Image: SlaveToQueenMum/Tripadvisor

This wide-ranging coastal reserve is home to an incredible array of strange and beautiful wildflowers including leafy royal hakea and tubular grevilleas. The climate means that the landscape remains green and ‘blooms’ for an extended period, making it one of the more reliable superbloom locations to plan your trip around. It is also remote and takes about six hours to drive to from Perth. This is a positive benefit as it means less crowds and a more relaxing experience.

Royal hakea puts on its brilliant display from late August until November. During this time it’s foliage turns vivid shades of red, orange, and yellow. The leaves can grow up to 10 feet tall! When looking closely at the branches you will see small flowers. This is a great time to spot this species and other standouts.

Western Australia has a rugged coastline and makes a great backdrop for any photo opportunity. However, for an even better vantage point take on the steep East Mount Barren Trail. During the climb, you may spot purple-toned orchids called pink fairies. Once you reach the summit, the coastal views are breathtaking.

The easiest national park to visit from here is Stirling Range National Park. To put it into perspective, it's like visiting a national park neighboring the eastern United States coastline. The Bluff Knoll climb is difficult, but you might get lucky and spot the rare Queen of Sheba orchid along the trail.

Joshua Tree National Park, California

Delicate yellow flowers up close

Joshua Tree National Park is famous for its trees with twisted trunks and branches that reach into the sky. If you visit in late winter or early spring, however, you may see the desert floor come alive with wildflowers. With good luck and timing, you may even witness one of the park's rare superblooms.

When to go: If you'd like to make your winter months warmer, this is a great place to see early wildflower blooms ahead of the official start of spring. If you go to the southern entrance of the park in January or February, you will see the first blooms of the season including bluish purple Canterbury bells. If you want to see Joshua trees with their unique pineapple-like flowers, visit in March or April.

Ideal places where you can take some great photos are inside the different ranges of wildflowers located at the southern entrance of the park and the Bajada Nature Trail which is by the Cottonwood Visitor Center. It is a family-friendly, easy, and flat trail that is a quarter of a mile long. If you are looking for even better views, then you should do the Mastodon Peak Loop, which is 2.5 miles long. This is definitely my favorite because of the amazing viewpoints it offers.

While you’re there: As the main access point to Joshua Tree, take some time to wander through the unique desert town. One of my most memorable adventures was visiting the Palm Springs Aerial Tramway, the largest rotating tram in the world. The tram offers incredible 360-degree views of the surrounding area while you ride, and even more amazing views of Mount San Jacinto State Park from the top.

Namaqualand, South Africa

Close-up of vivid purple blooms

Purple flowers in Namaqua National Park. Image: DebbyKZN/Tripadvisor

In the Northern Cape Namaqualand is a dry desert with bare rocks. But at the end of August this roughness can give way to an extraordinary phenomenon. These rocks can transform into flowers creating a “sea of flowers.” It is no exaggeration to say that this is an incredible sight.

What time is best to come: From late July to early October the flowers may bloom depending on the rainfall in the region. If you wish to see the flowers at their peak you should visit in August to September.

Best Photo Spots: For the most remarkable views of wildflowers, head to these two top spots. Skilpad Wildflower Reserve in Namaqua National Park, is famous for its large rolling hills covered in orange and yellow daisies, the “carpet of gold” that attracts visitors year after year. Goegap Nature Reserve has a more diverse and colorful range of flowers with lively purple vygies and an interesting rocky backdrop.

While you are there: This great remote area has services only available in a few scattered communities, so it is encouraged to plan for meals in advance. If you need to get a meal, both Kamieskroon and Hondeklip Bay are great stops, one being closer to nature reserves the other being coastal. For local tastes, Die Kliphuis Kombuis is recommended where the owner's homemade breads have received great reviews on Tripadvisor.

Saguaro National Park, Arizona

With a single flowering cactus in the center of the photograph, we see the view from below

Horse riders cross a desert landscape.

Blooming saguaro cactus (L); horseback riding in Saguaro National Park (R). Image: 440ah440/Tripadvisor (L); kayak62/Tripadvisor (R).

Superblooms in Saguaro National Park are extremely infrequent and are considered marget natural wonders. After a particularly rainy winter, many of the park's 40-foot cacti will bloom at the tops as well as along the sides of the cactus, and white flowers will appear.

When to go: Plan to visit in the last week of May or the first week of June for blooming cacti. If that short time window doesn’t work, visit between late February and mid-April for a different type of show with yellow brittlebush and other colorful wildflowers that will be spread across the desert.

What’s the best place to take pictures? Drive down Cactus Forest Drive and stop for photos of blooming saguaros with the Rincon Mountains in the background. Look for javelinas who are local to the area. If you'd like to take a short walk with some history, take the Signal Hill Trail and see some ancient petroglyphs.

Don't forget about Tucson - it's just a half hour drive, and has plenty of options to get some Mexican cuisine! You won’t have to spend half the drive deciding on a place to eat, as you could go to El Charro Cafe, which is famous for being the “home of the chimichanga.”

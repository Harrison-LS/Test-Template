When it comes to San Francisco, what immediately comes to mind are its iconic Golden Gate Bridge, cable cars gliding slowly, and picturesque beaches...

But San Francisco's beautiful scenery extends far beyond these! Fisherman's Wharf, Lombard Street (Nine Curves), Alcatraz Island, Union Square, and others shine like stars within the city; Yosemite National Park, long featured as an Apple wallpaper backdrop, and Napa Valley, a favorite among writers and artists, beckon visitors outside the city.

The Golden Gate Bridge—if you haven't photographed it, can you claim to have visited San Francisco?


The Golden Gate Bridge (Golden Gate Bridge) is a symbol of San Francisco and even California. Blue water reflecting against the orange-red bridge structure, the bay area's beauty owes much to it.


The big bridge is also a frequent guest in Hollywood blockbusters. However, usually bridges appear in disaster movies, such as "San Andreas", "Dawn of the Planet of the Apes", "X-Men", etc., being destroyed again and again...


Walking or cycling across the bridge with the sea breeze is the best way to appreciate it. There are dedicated walkways on both sides of the bridge, and bicycles are allowed on the western side.


The bridge has many viewing points, everyone can choose a few according to their own route:

The Golden Gate Bridge Pavilion on the south side of the bridge has a large parking lot with mountains in the background, suitable for morning photography.

There are also visitor centers and educational exhibits here.

The Vista Point on the north side of the bridge is an excellent location to view the Golden Gate Bridge from above (right after exiting the bridge). Morning light provides good lighting for photography, and you can also enjoy city views.

Fort Point, located south of and below the bridge on the eastern side, is a great spot to view the bridge from underneath. Morning light works well here too.

Marin Headlands on the western side offers many parking areas along the way with distant views of the Golden Gate Bridge.

Battery Spencer lookout tower on the west side across the bridge is also quite excellent.

If you want to drive onto the bridge, take US 101 highway or SR1.


To see the wonderful sight of fog enveloping the Golden Gate Bridge, visit in the morning as you'll have a better chance.


Boat cruises around the Golden Gate Bridge are the most famous attractions.

Taking a leisurely boat ride on the sea and viewing the Golden Gate Bridge from a different angle will offer you unique rewards~


You can also choose different routes. While visiting the Golden Gate Bridge, you'll also tour Bay Bridge, giving you a complete view of San Francisco Bay~


Golden Gate Bridge

·Constructed using over 100,000 tons of steel, it stands as a symbol of San Francisco. The orange-red bridge spans San Francisco Bay and the Golden Gate Strait, appearing magnificent and grand.

·During the Gold Rush era, it was named for its resemblance to a gateway leading to gold mines. The viewing point on the north side offers the best vantage point for seeing the entire bridge.

·The bridge frequently appears in numerous major American films such as "Rise of the Planet of the Apes" and "X-Men."

· Dedicated walking paths are set up on both sides of the bridge. Walking across with sea breeze, or cycling back and forth, are all great experiences.


Fisherman's Wharf——The liveliest and most "delicious" pier


Fisherman's Wharf (Fisherman's Wharf) roughly covers the area from Ghirardelli Square to Pier 39. The landmark is a circular sign with a large crab painted on it—find this, and you've arrived at Fisherman's Wharf.


A dazzling array of shops, a thrilling and quirky wax museum, uniquely flavored restaurants, and wonderful street performances are more than enough to keep you thoroughly entertained.

Of course, when you come here you can't miss the famous sourdough bread. It's both healthy and natural, made using traditional methods, and is a must-try delicacy when visiting San Francisco. The well-known old shop Boudin Bakery here is very famous, I recommend trying it~

As a dock, the seafood here will surely satisfy your appetite. If you're interested in seafood and can get up early, head to Pier 47 at dawn. Watch the lively scene of fishermen unloading their catch, and get fresh seafood first-hand!


You absolutely must not miss Pier 39, it has a famous reputation and rich attractions, representing the essence of Fisherman's Wharf.

Furthermore, this is also a habitat for sea lions. Before even reaching the shoreline, you can hear their continuous calls echoing around. As you approach, you'll see lethargic sea lions sunbathing on platforms beneath the pier, giving you an opportunity to observe them up close.

Nine-Bend Flower Street - The street least friendly to new drivers


Nine-Bend Flower Street is actually a section of Lombard Street, located between Hyde Street (higher elevation) and Leavenworth Street (lower elevation). This road is quite steep, but this short stretch has been designed with eight hairpin turns. The slope combined with the "Z"-shaped winding road means vehicles can only travel downhill in one direction.


Experienced drivers can come here to test their driving skills; New drivers may as well enjoy this pleasant stroll. The flowerbeds on both sides of the street are filled with roses, and residents along the road also grow flowers and grass at their doorsteps. During blooming season in June and July, viewed from a distance, it looks like a velvet embroidery hung askew, highly aesthetic.

From the higher ground of Hyde Street, you can have far-reaching views of the bay and Alcatraz Island and others. If you want to capture the complete curve effect of Lombard Street, then take photos from Leavenworth Street at lower elevation.

Lombard Street

·Officially named Lombard Street, it is the world's most curved street. From higher ground along Hyde Street, you can have distant views of the Bay Bridge and Coit Tower.

·The municipal government has built flower beds specifically to prevent traffic accidents. When vehicles pass here, speed must not exceed 5 miles per hour.

·Within a very short street block there are eight sharp turns, with 40-degree slopes and roads curving in a "Z" shape, all of which seriously test driving skills.

·Rose bushes fill the flower beds on both sides of the lanes. When flowers bloom, from a distance they look like a hanging velvet embroidery - beautiful beyond measure.

Exquisite carvings, majestic colonnades, combined with fountain lake water and countless swans and ducks, create an atmosphere filled with romance. Today, the Palace of Fine Arts has become a popular recreational spot for San Franciscans.

Palace of Fine Arts

The Palace of Fine Arts in San Francisco was originally built in 1915 for the Panama "Panama-Pacific International Exposition." It attracted 18 million visitors during the event, but was abandoned afterward. It wasn't until 1962 that a famous German-American architect named Meibek was commissioned to redesign and renovate it. He specialized in detailed decoration, which is the origin of the palace's intricately carved style. Today, the Palace has become a popular recreational spot for San Francisco residents. Swans and ducks swim together in the ponds, and the Art Palace Theater is also located here, making it an excellent place to relax during holidays.

Alcatraz Island (Alcatraz Island) was previously a prison where the US government incarcerated serious criminals.

Today the prison is well-preserved and open to tourists. In dim lighting within cold cells, you can learn about the prison's structure and history through audio guides, and even hear original recordings of prisoners who were once held here.


This place is not only a spooky prison, but also has many supernatural legends. For those who enjoy adventure, the island has specially opened night tours for you to make your visit even more exciting.

Alcatraz Island

Alcatraz Island is a 22-acre rocky island that formerly served as the United States federal government's prison for notorious criminals. Famous inmates imprisoned here include Chicago's "Capone," the deadly yet brilliant "Birdman" Stroud, and the bloodthirsty "Machine Gun" Killer Kelly.

Prisoners could only gaze at San Francisco teeming with life through the iron bars of their cells, enduring another cruel form of spiritual punishment. Today, the entire island preserves as much as possible of its original prison appearance, featuring facilities such as a bird sanctuary, museum, and bookstore. In the shops on the island are sold memoirs from former inmates whose sentences have been completed; they all claim it was an "eternally unforgettable painful experience." Although this island has a gloomy history, it is also a refuge for wildlife, with many species of birds making their home here—for visitors, it remains an excellent place to relax and reflect.

San Francisco City Hall

San Francisco City Hall is the government building serving as the civic center of San Francisco, featuring distinctive Beaux-Arts style architecture with a dome ranked among the top 5 in the world.

The current City Hall building replaced the old municipal hall that was completely destroyed in the 1906 San Francisco earthquake; for architecture enthusiasts, the scenery here is undoubtedly a visual feast. Twin Peaks

Twin Peaks consists of two hills with an elevation of approximately 270 meters. Due to their shape, early Spanish immigrants affectionately nicknamed them "the breasts of an Indian maiden," and they remain the only natural hill preserved within San Francisco.

Climbing Twin Peaks is quite an arduous experience. You need to wear many layers of clothing to protect against strong winds and cold fog, while working hard to ascend the steep slopes. However, the effort is rewarded - on clear days, you can overlook the entire cityscape of San Francisco, even seeing the red Golden Gate Bridge beside San Francisco Bay. This place is also an excellent spot for enjoying night views!


Chinatown - A sense of belonging for every Chinese person


San Francisco's Chinatown is the only place in Western America comparable to New York's Chinatown, located at the intersection of Grant Avenue and California Street.


The streets resemble early 20th-century Hong Kong architecture, with some atmosphere reminiscent of Hong Kong films from the 1980s.

The signs you see are all written in Chinese characters, everything you hear is spoken in Mandarin Chinese; this is a place that feels very familiar to Chinese people.

Chinatown, San Francisco

San Francisco's Chinatown is the only place in Western America that can be compared to New York's Chinatown, with its center formed at the intersection of Grant Avenue and California Street.

Approximately 80,000 overseas Chinese residents live here, making it completely a city within a country with very exotic charm. Although San Francisco's spectacular Union Square and western financial center are located beside Chinatown, all signs you can see inside Chinatown are written in Hanzi, and all you hear is Chinese. This is a place where Chinese people feel very familiar. California Academy of Sciences—Sailing Through the Ocean of Natural Science The California Academy of Sciences is absolutely a paradise for natural science enthusiasts. It is the only venue in the world that combines an aquarium, planetarium, natural history museum, and a four-story tropical rainforest into one building. You can watch scientists at work through glass partitions, and you are welcome to ask them questions freely if you have any inquiries.

Every Thursday evening, there's also "Night at the Museum" waiting for you to unlock.

California Science Museum

The California Science Museum is the first comprehensive marine, terrestrial and aerial biology museum in America nationwide. Integrating education, exhibitions, and research into one natural history museum, it ranks among the top 10 natural history museums globally and serves as an internationally leading modern natural sciences research center.

The facility features the Steinhart Aquarium, the world's largest fully digital planetarium (Morrison Planetarium), the Kimball Natural History Museum, and a tropical rainforest. In the planetarium you can experience a breathtaking journey through the universe; in the aquarium you can explore the underwater coral reef world where over 2000 species of fish live. There is also the world's largest breathing eco-roof here—through glass partitions you can watch scientists at work, ask questions freely, or even go to the second-floor Natural Science Center to consult directly with scientists.

II. Step beyond downtown San Francisco to embrace art and nature





San Francisco's city journey is destined to be rich and colorful. Outside the city, there are even more fun attractions to visit, all within a short drive (typically under four hours), making them perfect for day trips.


Napa Valley——Taste Wine and Admire Scenery in Romantic Wine Country


Home to over 400 wineries, with 95% being family-owned businesses. Take the Napa Valley Wine Train or Napa Valley Wine Trolley to visit several wineries for tastings and enjoy fine dining.


Wine is only part of Napa Valley's charm. Vineyard views at sunset, seasonal delicacies gliding over the tongue, romance as hot air balloons slowly ascend, art appreciation, cycling, and spa relaxation all make Napa Valley intoxicatingly captivating.

Transportation Routes:

After approximately an hour of driving, you can take a ferry and local VINE bus to Napa Valley. You can also arrange your trip through a local travel agency or rental company.

You can also choose a private car one-day tour. What should you do when visiting this wine country? Of course, taste the fine wines! But what if you're worried about drunk driving?

A professional Chinese-speaking driver will take you on a private car one-day tour, enjoying good wine and a relaxed, pleasant experience.


Monterey - Become an artistic soul in this seaside town


Monterey is a coastal town in northern California, known as California's "First City". From San Francisco, drive along Highway 1 for about an hour and a half to reach it.


I recommend driving, cycling, or even hiking on the famous 17 Mile Drive to admire the winding Pacific Coast scenery between Monterey and Carmel towns.

There are also attractions such as Pebble Beach Golf Course, Cannery Row, Monterey Bay Aquarium, and Carmel Beach. Various charming shops, restaurants, hotels, and art galleries on the streets are worth stopping to visit.


Transportation Routes:

Driving along the scenic Pacific Coast Highway (Highway 1) for about two hours will bring you to Monterey. Additionally, you can take buses, trains, or even ships from San Francisco.


Monterey Town

Monterey Town

Monterey Town boasts a well-developed artistic community with numerous museums featuring local and internationally renowned artists, as well as entertainment venues. Cannery Row is famous for commemorating the writer John Steinbeck;

Also here the marine life is rich and diverse; Monterey Aquarium is one of the largest aquariums in North America.


Yosemite——Experience Nature Within a National Park


Yosemite is approximately 4 hours' drive from San Francisco. If your time is tight, it's recommended to head straight for some must-see attractions and landmark sights.


After quickly passing through Yosemite Valley, a series of waterfalls will present themselves before you, including the most famous Yosemite Falls.

Then you arrive at the magnificent granite cliffs—below El Capitan (El Capitan), where Half Dome (Half Dome) views are also visible. Standing on the cliff edge at Glacier Point, shouting two loud calls, and the stress from study and work disappears instantly.


Hiking, rock climbing, cycling, skiing, rafting, wildlife viewing—Yosemite offers everything.

If the weather cooperates, taking a convertible open-air trolley ride with friends to deeply experience Yosemite is also a great choice.

Transportation routes:

You depart from San Francisco and drive east along Highway 140 (more scenic) or Highway 120 for about three to four hours. You can also take Amtrak trains or hire private cars through various travel agencies (choosing between group tours or private excursions).


Choosing a Yosemite one-day tour includes dedicated shuttle service and professional guided commentary, plus the opportunity to visit Sequoia National Forest—two birds with one stone.


Yosemite National Park

Yosemite National Park is California's most famous tourist destination. Majestic waterfalls, towering granite formations and serene valleys make Yosemite look like a fairyland from any angle. Towering trees, mountain streams, jagged rock cliffs sculpted by glaciers, and glistening lake surfaces make visitors feel as if they are in an earthly paradise. Without doubt, both liquid and solid water are the main contributors to creating Yosemite's magnificent landscapes. Here you can find your favorite natural scenery.


Silicon Valley—Admire Tech Titans in a Tech Hub


Drive south from San Francisco about 40 minutes and you will reach the global tech hub—Silicon Valley (Silicon Valley).

Here home to global giants like Google, Apple, Facebook, and countless startups. Many world-changing products that lead trends were born here.

Although you typically can't tour inside the companies, walking around their campuses is still worthwhile. Feel Silicon Valley life - isn't it like that popular US TV show "Silicon Valley"?

Transportation route:

Drive north across the Golden Gate Bridge to reach Marin County for a relaxing time. You can also cycle across the bridge. If you prefer crossing the bay rather than using the bridge, you can choose bus, shuttle, or ferry.


Choose a Silicon Valley day tour, allowing you to worship industry giants while enjoying the joy of shopping at the outlet mall.

IV. Transportation Recommendations


External Transportation


San Francisco has three airports in total, San Francisco International Airport (SFO) is the main airport of San Francisco, located in the southern part of San Francisco.

Oakland International Airport (OAK) is on the East Coast, spanning across the Bay Bridge. While Norman Y. Mineta San Jose International Airport (SJC) is about an hour south of downtown.


At San Francisco airport, you can book private car service for pickup and drop-off at the terminal.


Internal Transportation


Many places in America are suitable for self-driving, but San Francisco is one of the cities that is not ideal for driving due to extremely steep street slopes and expensive parking.

However, San Francisco's public transportation system is very developed. Buses, subways, historic cable cars, and trams - there's always a form of public transport available to serve your journey, taking you to all corners of this city.


Cable Car (cable car) is located downtown, being San Francisco's most famous vintage transportation method, quite popular.


There are three routes: Powell-Hyde, Powell-Mason, and California St.


The F-line streetcar travels between Market St. and Fisherman's Wharf. You can purchase tickets at various ticket booths, or buy tickets from the conductor when you board.


Of course, relying solely on public transportation for sightseeing has its limitations; you may also choose to rent a car. However, San Francisco city roads are somewhat rugged and not very friendly to new drivers, though experienced drivers might enjoy the challenge.


If visiting attractions outside of San Francisco, renting a car is more highly recommended for your trip.

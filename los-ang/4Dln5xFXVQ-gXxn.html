First, enjoying the San Francisco city area


The first stop was chosen as San Francisco because Highway 1 leads directly to Los Angeles without having to return on the same route. It is recommended to arrive two days early; you will be able to participate in Chinese New Year celebrations when reaching Los Angeles.

San Francisco's Golden Gate Bridge has been featured most frequently among American landscapes. Many domestic TV dramas such as "Little Daddy" and "Bruce Lee Legend" have depicted the charm of San Francisco. A San Francisco citypass and boat tour can satisfy all your fantasies about this city. Some say San Francisco is old, yet some people love this feeling; it's a nostalgic familiar taste. Boarding San Francisco's historic cable car allows you to savor the unique charm of this city. At the intersection of Powell Street and Market Street in downtown - even in tech-savvy America, one can still witness the historical scene of trolleys being manually turned.


Suggested San Francisco route: Lombard Street (Nine-Flower Street) — Chinatown — Fisherman's Wharf — Golden Gate Bridge


[Lombard Street] The world's most winding road, and also a must-visit destination in San Francisco.

Transportation: Take the Powell-Hyde Cable Car Line to Lombard St & Hyde St station

Admission: Free

Opening Hours: All day

Address: Lombard Street, San Francisco, California



[Chinatown] The largest Chinatown in the United States, and a wonderful place to experience the American Spring Festival atmosphere. During the Spring Festival season, there are parade activities and various dragon and lion dance performances.


[Fisherman's Wharf] One of San Francisco's must-visit destinations. Not only does it have beautiful scenery, but you cannot miss the delicious seafood dinners here.

Transportation: You can take buses 2, 4, 8, 18, 24, 27, 38, 44, 54, 56, 58, 72, 74, or 76 to Beach St & Taylor St station, then walk 2 minutes to arrive. Alternatively, you can take the F Market light rail line to Beach St & Stockton St station or The Embarcadero & Stockton St station

Admission: Free

Opening Hours: All day

Address: Pier 39, San Francisco, CA 94133


[Golden Gate Bridge] Admission: The pedestrian walkways on both sides of the bridge are free to access; viewing area parking is also free;

Vehicles are charged a one-way bridge toll from north to south, and cash is not accepted; online payment is available. Payment can be made within 30 days before entering the bridge or within 2 days after crossing, at $6 per trip.

Opening hours: The bridge for vehicles and bicycles is open all day;

Pedestrian walkway operating hours: Weekdays 5:00-18:30, daylight saving time 5:00-21:00.

Address: Golden Gate Bridge, San Francisco, CA9412

IV. San Diego: A small city stamped with traces of American history


Recommendation A: San Diego Naval Port

Suitable for: Military enthusiasts

Climb aboard the largest naval base in the U.S. Pacific Fleet and gaze upon aircraft carriers, battleships, cruisers, and more. Get a close-up experience of these warships' charm—every military fan must not miss this opportunity. Retired veterans will provide free guided tours, sharing stories from those tumultuous times; you might receive unexpected insights.

Address: 910 N. Harbor Drive, San Diego, CA 92101

Opening Hours: Daily 10:00 AM - 5:00 PM PM, with entry gates closing at 4:00 PM (Closed on Thanksgiving and Christmas)

Transportation: Take the bus to Santa Fe Depot station


Recommendation B: San Diego Old Town

Suitable for: Those who appreciate culture and geography

Located at the border between the United States and Mexico, this is one of the first settlements where Europeans set foot on American soil. A historic old town steeped in rich Mexican atmosphere; you can experience its cultural landscapes without even traveling to Mexico, along with authentic Mexican cuisine.

Address: 2383 San Diego Avenue, San Diego CA 92110

Transportation: You can take buses 8, 9, 10, 20, and 30 to reach Old Town San Diego.


Old Town San Diego

The history of San Diego is filled with blood and turmoil: It was originally a gathering place for Native Americans, later occupied by the Spanish, then belonged to Mexico, until it regained its territory after the American Revolutionary War. This complex past created a unique cultural blend between Mexican and Spanish influences, with Old Town serving as the quintessential embodiment of this fusion. Located just minutes from downtown, the area features ornate fountains throughout its streetscape. Charming shops are surrounded by fragrant courtyards, while government buildings, post offices, restaurants, schools, and churches are all concentrated in this vibrant district. The cultural village preserves 19th-century architectural aesthetics with staff dressed in period-appropriate attire. Saturday markets offer visitors opportunities to purchase authentic handicrafts and local delicacies. Multiple historical museums enrich the area's educational landscape.


Recommendation C: Santa Barbara Spanish Village—An Authentic Recreation of Ancient Spanish Architecture

The Santa Barbara Cultural Village is hidden within America's premier cultural park, Balboa Park.

This park features tours to 14 top museums, 4 theaters, a multi-purpose sports arena, a rose garden, and a Japanese garden. However, many people don't know that there is also a Spanish Art Village deep within the park.

It is said that this place is a true recreation of an ancient Spanish village. However, now these small houses have all been rented by artists as their own studios, turning the area into paradise for artists - the entire village gathers artists who create oil paintings, glass art, watercolors, sculptures, woodcuts, and various other crafts.

Address: 1549 El Prado, San Diego

Transportation: Take bus route #7 to the Park Bl & Zoo Pl station.

Tickets: Free entry, some museums within require separate admission

Opening hours: Open 24 hours

Visitors can not only enjoy spectacular performances of various marine animals, but also interact closely with them. There's even an opportunity to have lunch with adorable orca calves.

San Diego Sea World

In this world-famous San Diego Sea World, there is no barrier between animals and humans. Adorable marine animals like dolphins and sea lions are within reach, with some even strolling on unfenced green lawns. The park isn't very large, the walking routes inside are clearly marked and simple, so you won't worry about getting lost or retracing your steps. As long as you schedule times for watching performances and visiting different exhibits separately, you can enjoy all sorts of unique animal shows: Curious to see how sea lions dance tango? Want to watch killer whales put on an impressive performance? Each show has its own characteristics. Don't think Sea World is only a paradise for children; adults can also gain much here!


V. Las Vegas


1. Ride the world's largest Ferris wheel and experience a romantic, luxurious Spring Festival.

This Ferris wheel is also called "The Big Wheel", stands 167.6 meters tall, was built with a $550 million investment, and is located near Caesars Palace on Las Vegas Boulevard. It's 30 meters taller than London's "London Eye" observation wheel, and one round of sightseeing takes 30 minutes.

Come to Las Vegas for the Spring Festival! Propose on the world's largest Ferris wheel, spend your honeymoon in this most luxurious city.


High Roller Observation Wheel

This world's largest Ferris wheel required a $550 million investment, positioned near Caesars Palace on Las Vegas Boulevard. It exceeds London's "London Eye" by 30 meters, surpasses China's "Nanchang Star" by 9 meters, and rises 2.7 meters above Singapore's "Flyer". A complete ride takes approximately 30 minutes. The wheel features 28 fully enclosed glass cabins with climate control, each accommodating up to 40 passengers maximum.


2. Las Vegas is a famous world entertainment capital; spectacular show performances cannot be missed, among which the best-reputed ones are O Show and Dream Show, truly a huge visual feast.


3. Grand Canyon: One of the Seven Wonders of the World, America's Grand Canyon is renowned far and wide, being a must-visit destination for many travelers to the United States.

You can take a helicopter to overlook the magnificent canyon landscape from high above, or paddle at the bottom of the canyon with a kayak, or ride mules through the valley floor, experiencing the most primitive American Western cowboy style. In short, the Grand Canyon not only offers excellent natural scenery but also provides comprehensive viewing options. The Grand Canyon is divided into West Canyon and South Canyon. If you ask me which one is more fun. I can only say that if you want to see authentic natural landscapes, go to the South Canyon; if you want to explore the canyon 360 degrees all around while also wanting to learn about Native American culture, I recommend going to the West Canyon. The main thing depends on where it's convenient for you.

Address: 3545 S Las Vegas Blvd Las Vegas, NV 89109 USA

Opening Hours: Daily 11:30–02:00

Transportation: Take the Las Vegas Monorail to Harrah's / The Linq Station or get off at Flamingo / Caesars Palace Station


        Grand Canyon National Park

        · Grand Canyon National Park is one of the Seven Wonders of the World, located in northwest Arizona, divided into two main parts - South Canyon and North Canyon, each with its own features.

· Of which the South Canyon is the most visited route for tourists, and its self-driving road is also built quite perfectly;

North Canyon has higher elevation with thin air, but the sky is as clear and blue as washed.

• Although not the deepest canyon in the world, its grandeur and breathtaking scenery are unquestionably top-tier, making it one of America's most worthwhile national parks to visit.

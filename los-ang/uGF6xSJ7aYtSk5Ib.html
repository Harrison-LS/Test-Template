# Recommendations for Overseas Study Tours—San Francisco, USA

Los Angeles

Brief

Hop on the Cable Car, Fall in Love with San Francisco!

Content

San Francisco, America's rightful alternative capital - it is the birthplace of hippie culture, the headquarters of the LGBTQ community, and the source of the "Beat Generation," while its surrounding Bay Area continuously supplies power to global geek culture.


San Francisco's tolerance and diversity is an innate temperament, naturally flowing through all aspects of urban life such as food, architecture, music, and more.


As American writer William Saroyan said: "If you are still alive, San Francisco will not bore you; if you have died, San Francisco can bring you back to life."


If you only had one day in San Francisco, where would you go?


Board the cable car and fall in love with San Francisco!


"Ding-dong, ding-dong..." A crisp ringing sound comes from afar, a cable car carrying memories of time slowly approaches.


Since the late 19th century, San Francisco's cable cars have been carrying people through streets and alleys to this day.


San Francisco's cable cars are not only an important part of the city's transportation network, but also one of the best ways to tour San Francisco, and they are witnesses to the diverse cultural and historical changes of this city. Today's 24-hour journey will begin by hopping on a cable car!


09:00 Depart from Union Square


This is the liveliest area in downtown San Francisco, marked by the Lady Liberty statue. The nearby neighborhood is filled with hotels, theaters, branded specialty shops, and shopping centers. Those who love to shop will certainly not want to miss this place!


09:30 Cable Car Museum


Since you're experiencing San Francisco by cable car, a visit to the Cable Car Museum is naturally in order.

The museum was established during World War II, featuring numerous models and historical photographs. There's also a souvenir shop inside where you can purchase all sorts of cable car souvenirs.


Imagine, during your free time, ringing the cable car bell. The crisp ringing brings memories of San Francisco to mind, as if you're back on that day when you boarded the cable car.


11:00 Lombard Street

"Lombard" is named for the eight sharp turns along a short stretch of road. "Street" refers to the abundance of flowering plants lining both sides.


From lower vantage points, you can capture the winding, blossom-draped streetscape. Standing at higher elevations offers panoramic views of the Golden Gate Bridge and Alcatraz Island, allowing you to photograph the perfect blend of Lombard Street with bay scenery.


*Lombard Street at Night


12:00 Fisherman's Wharf

Continuing north from Lombard Street leads to the famous Fisherman's Wharf. Pier 39 is particularly renowned, featuring colorful shops, restaurants, street performances, and opportunities to watch sea lions while enjoying a day by the water.


How can you visit San Francisco for your studies without tasting the seaside specialties? The most famous food at Fisherman's Wharf is "Clam Chowder" – a rich seafood soup containing potatoes, cream, seafood, and more. The fragrant and smooth broth coats tender and juicy seafood; taking one bite must be delicious!


EFL San Francisco campus is also located at Fisherman's Wharf. Walking just a short distance from the school allows you to take in the beautiful bay scenery, and from inside the campus, you can clearly see the iconic Golden Gate Bridge – that red bridge frequently appearing in American TV shows and Hollywood movies.


* EFL San Francisco Campus


14:00 Escape from Alcatraz


After lunch, you might take a boat to the nearby Alcatraz Island to burn off some calories.


Alcatraz was a prison in the 1930s-60s that detained many serious criminals. Many films are based on real people from within the prison, such as "Escape from Alcatraz" and "Birdman of Alcatraz."


*Alcatraz Island Under the Night Sky*


The highly acclaimed films "Cliffhanger" and "X-Men 3" were also filmed here. Perhaps you'll run into a movie star one day.


16:30 Searching for the "Beat Generation"


This area is locally called "Little Italy." In the 1950s-60s, it was home to a group of poets and writers known as the "Beat Generation."


The "Beat Generation" (Beat Generation) pursued spontaneous artistic creation, opposing forces that crushed human spirits. They harbored romantic yearnings for chaos and revelry - this was their quiet resistance to the rigid culture promoted by the post-WWII government. The Beat Generation was not only the peak of San Francisco's literary school but also a precious gem in American and even world literature. It deeply influenced later hippie culture.


If you are a loyal admirer of the Beat Generation movement, then you definitely should not miss City Lights Bookstore, as it is the headquarters of Beat culture, housing all Beat literary works and their inherently independent attitude toward the world.


18:00 Return Home Where Food Tastes Better - Explore Chinatown


San Francisco's Chinatown is the largest Chinese district outside of Asia, with approximately 100,000 residents.


For travelers, these attractions are definitely not to be missed. You can see authentic Chinese-style graffiti here, explore the marks left by the struggles of the first overseas Chinese immigrants, and most importantly, select your favorite restaurant for dinner.


19:30 Appreciate City Night Views, Gaze at the Golden Gate Bridge


After dinner, head to Twin Peak to enjoy San Francisco's city night view. Since San Francisco is located in hilly terrain (similar to Qingdao), the city has many steep roads going straight up and down. From high vantage points, you can appreciate the charm of the city under the cover of night.

If city night views aren't enough to attract you, after dinner you can return to Union Square for some shopping to help digest.


Of course, from Twin Peaks you can also see San Francisco's iconic landmark — the Golden Gate Bridge. With lights fully illuminated on the bridge and continuous traffic flow, shadows reflecting off the wide ocean surface are quite stunning. A time-lapse photography of the Golden Gate Bridge from this angle will certainly surprise you.


Diverse and Inclusive San Francisco


Of course, in addition to the cable car day tour mentioned above, San Francisco's diversity and inclusivity is also reflected in its many different communities, such as where the Flower Power movement was born — the Hippie District.


The Castro neighborhood... origin of America's LGBTQ+ equality movement with rainbow flags flying...


If you have plenty of time, you can also visit Silicon Valley campuses, or go to world-renowned universities like Stanford University for a visit, feel the academic atmosphere of top institutions. This will add some culture and technology depth to your trip.


Twenty-four hours in a day pass quickly. But you can drink a bowl of fragrant hot seafood soup at Fisherman's Wharf like a local, buy a soul-soothing book at City Lights, and admire this intoxicating city under the cover of night.


Every encounter evokes beautiful sentiments such as "If life were always as it was at first sight"; every midnight reverie leaves one longing to return. This is San Francisco, radiating endless light.
